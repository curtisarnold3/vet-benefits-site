Get free help with your claim

Free, trained, VA-accredited help exists in every state. Nobody may charge you for help with an initial claim. Start here before you pay anyone.

Free help, right now

  • VA's search for accredited representatives and VSOs: va.gov/get-help-from-accredited-representative/find-rep. Also the OGC accreditation search, which lets you confirm any person's accreditation.
  • County Veterans Service Officers (CVSOs): paid by your county, free to you, and they know the local offices for state benefits. Search "[your county] veterans service office."
  • National VSOs with offices in most VA regional offices: Disabled American Veterans (DAV), Veterans of Foreign Wars (VFW), The American Legion. You do not have to be a member.
  • State Departments of Veterans Affairs: most states have accredited officers. Find your state's agency.

To let a representative work your claim, you sign VA Form 21-22 (for a VSO) or 21-22a (for an individual attorney or claims agent). Read the limited-release boxes before signing.

How fees work for accredited attorneys and claims agents

  • Initial claims: no fee, ever. Federal law bars anyone, accredited or not, from charging to prepare, present, or prosecute a claim before VA issues its first decision on it (38 U.S.C. 5904(c)(1)).
  • After the first decision (an HLR, Supplemental Claim, Board appeal, or TDIU claim), an accredited attorney or agent may charge. The fee must be in a written agreement filed with VA.
  • 20% of back pay is presumed reasonable. Over 33 1/3% is presumed unreasonable (38 CFR 14.636). Fees come from past-due benefits awarded; if you win nothing, a contingency fee is zero.
  • VSOs never charge for claims work.

Sources: 38 U.S.C. 5904, 38 CFR 14.636, VA accredited representative FAQs.

How to spot a claim shark

VA calls them "claims predators": companies and individuals who are not accredited and charge veterans for help VA says is free or unlawful to charge for. Warning signs:

  • They charge for an initial claim, or charge up front, or charge a flat fee no matter the outcome.
  • They want a share of your future monthly pay (not just back pay), often for years.
  • They ask for your VA.gov login, or want to log in as you. Never share it. In Colorado this is also barred by state law.
  • They "guarantee" a rating or say they have a "secret" doctor or process.
  • They are not in VA's accreditation search. Look them up by name.
  • They call themselves "consultants," "coaches," or "medical evidence companies" to dodge the word representation.

Report them: VA's VSAFE line, 1-833-388-7233, or VSAFE.gov. VA's explainer: Beware: claims predators (Aug 9, 2024).
