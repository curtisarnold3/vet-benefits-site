# Connecticut (CT) — State Benefits for Disabled Veterans

Research date: 2026-10-16. Official agency: Connecticut Department of Veterans Affairs (CTDVA), https://portal.ct.gov/dva (287 West Street, Rocky Hill, CT 06067; Office of Advocacy and Assistance 1-866-9CT-VETS / 860-616-3600).

Method note: The CGA statute site (cga.ct.gov) rejects WebFetch's TLS check, so statutes and public acts were pulled with curl and read locally. The "current" revision of the General Statutes online is revised to January 1, 2025; 2025-session changes were read directly from Public Act 25-168 (HB 7287) and the Office of Legislative Research (OLR) "Acts Affecting Veterans" reports for 2025 and 2026. Several portal.ct.gov URLs that appear in search results (DVA property-tax page, DRS military page, OPM Q&A booklet for veterans) now return 404; substitutes are noted. The WebSearch budget for this turn ran out partway through, so a few items were confirmed only through statutes or legislative summaries rather than agency web pages; those are flagged.

Key definition (CGS 27-103, https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_506.htm): "veteran" means "any person honorably discharged from, released under honorable conditions from or released with an other than honorable discharge based on a qualifying condition from, active service in, the armed forces"; "service in time of war" means "service of ninety or more cumulative days during a period of war unless separated from service earlier because of an injury incurred or aggravated in the line of duty or a service-connected disability rated by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs." Period of war follows 38 USC 101 plus listed operations (Lebanon, Grenada, Earnest Will, Panama, Afghanistan 10/24/2001–8/30/2021, Iraq 3/19/2003–12/31/2011 or 6/1/2014–12/9/2021).

## 1. Property tax

Connecticut has no county government; exemptions are applied by the town/city assessor. Exemptions reduce assessed value (not a credit). All amounts below are statutory minimums; CGS 12-62g requires towns to multiply them by an "increase factor" after large revaluations, so actual amounts are higher in many towns (OLR 2023-R-0175: "the exemption amount is higher in more than half of the municipalities").

### 1a. Basic wartime veteran exemption — CGS 12-81(19)
Source: https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_203.htm (Chapter 203, revised to Jan. 1, 2025).
Quote: "property to the amount of one thousand dollars belonging to, or held in trust for, (A) any resident of this state who is a veteran, as defined in section 27-103, who was a member of the armed forces in service in time of war ... (F) any person who is retired from the armed forces after thirty years of service ..."
Combined with the mandatory income-based add-on in CGS 12-81g (below), OLR 2023-R-0175 (Oct. 6, 2023, https://prdext3.cga.ct.gov/2023/rpt/pdf/2023-R-0175.pdf) states: "By law, municipalities must provide at least $1,500 in property tax exemptions for veterans with wartime service."
Filing: proof of service (DD-214) must be recorded with the town clerk before the October 1 assessment date (CGS 12-93, 12-95). OPM: "the DD-214 must be recorded in the land records prior to October 1" (https://portal.ct.gov/opm/igpp/grants/tax-relief-grants/additional-veterans-tax-relief-program). PA 26-114 §§18, 20, 21 (effective Oct. 1, 2026) broadens the acceptable discharge proof beyond "honorable discharge" to match CGS 27-103 (OLR 2026-R-0083, June 22, 2026).
Surviving spouse/minor child: CGS 12-81(22): "property to the amount of one thousand dollars belonging to ... any surviving spouse while such person remains a widow or widower, or a minor child or both, residing in this state ... provided such amount shall be three thousand dollars if death was due to service and occurred while on active duty."

### 1b. Disabled veteran exemption (10%+) — CGS 12-81(20)
Source: Chapter 203 (above) and PA 25-168 §234 (https://www.cga.ct.gov/2025/ACT/PA/PDF/2025PA-00168-R00HB-07287-PA.PDF).
Quote (current text): "property not exceeding three thousand five hundred dollars in amount shall be exempt from taxation, which property belongs to, or is held in trust for, any resident of this state who has served, or is serving, in the Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Coast Guard, Air Force or Space Force of the United States and (i) has a disability rating as determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs amounting to ten per cent or more of total disability, other than a determination of (I) being permanently and totally disabled based on a service-connected disability rating of one hundred per cent, or (II) in any municipality providing the exemption under section 240 of this act, having a service-connected total disability based on individual unemployability, provided such exemption shall be two thousand dollars in any case in which such rating is between ten per cent and twenty-five per cent; two thousand five hundred dollars in any case in which such rating is more than twenty-five per cent but not more than fifty per cent; three thousand dollars in any case in which such rating is more than fifty per cent but not more than seventy-five per cent; and three thousand five hundred dollars in any case in which such resident has attained sixty-five years of age or such rating is more than seventy-five per cent; or (ii) is receiving a pension, annuity or compensation from the United States because of the loss in service of a leg or arm ..."
Note: the hint's figures ($1,500/$2,000/$2,500/$3,000) are outdated; the statute now reads $2,000/$2,500/$3,000/$3,500 (OLR 2023-R-0175 Table 2 agrees). Wartime service is NOT required (OLR: "Wartime service is not necessary to qualify"). Can be applied to real estate or a motor vehicle (OPM Form M-59a line 11 lists "Real Estate / Motor Vehicle / Personal Property / Supplemental Motor Vehicles").
Proof: "No individual shall receive any exemption ... until such individual has complied with section 12-95 and has submitted proof of such individual's disability rating, as determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs, to the assessor of the town in which the exemption is sought." Proof is not re-required unless the rating changes. Retroactive abatement/refund allowed up to three years if proof was obtained late.
Surviving spouse: "property belonging to ... such deceased veteran's surviving spouse, while such spouse remains a widow or widower, or ... minor children during their minority ... while they are residents of this state, shall be exempt in the same aggregate amount as that to which the disabled veteran was or would have been entitled."

### 1c. Severe service-connected disability — CGS 12-81(21)
Quote: "The dwelling house, and the lot whereupon the same is erected ... occupied as such person's domicile, shall be exempt from local property taxation to the extent of ten thousand dollars of its assessed valuation ... if such person is a veteran ... declared by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs ... to have a service-connected disability from paraplegia or osteochondritis resulting in permanent loss of the use of both legs or permanent paralysis of both legs and lower parts of the body; or from hemiplegia ... or from total blindness ... or from the amputation of both arms, both legs, both hands or both feet, or the combination of a hand and a foot ... The loss of the use of one arm or one leg ... shall qualify a veteran for a property tax exemption in the same manner as hereinabove, provided such exemption shall be for five thousand dollars." Additional to the 12-81(20) amount; residence only; surviving spouse keeps it while unremarried (or after a later marriage ends) while owning and occupying the home. 12-81(21)(C): municipal option to fully exempt a home bought/modified with VA Specially Adapted Housing assistance.

### 1d. FULL exemption for 100% permanent-and-total — CGS 12-81(83) (PA 24-46, amended by PA 25-2 and PA 25-168)
Source: Chapter 203 §12-81(83) (as of Jan. 1, 2025) and PA 25-168 §233 (text effective Oct. 1, 2025, applicable to assessment years beginning on/after Oct. 1, 2025).
Current statutory text (PA 25-168 §233): "(83) (A) (i) That fractional share of a dwelling, including a condominium ... a unit in a common interest community ... and a mobile manufactured home ... (I) that belongs to or is held in trust for any resident of this state who has served in the Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Coast Guard, Air Force or Space Force of the United States and has been determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs to be permanently and totally disabled based on a service-connected disability rating of one hundred per cent, or that is possessed by such a resident as a tenant for life or tenant for a term of years ... and (II) that is occupied by such resident as the resident's primary residence, or (ii) lacking such residence, one motor vehicle that belongs to or is held in trust for such resident and is garaged in this state. As used in this subdivision, 'dwelling' does not include any portion of the unit or structure used by such resident for commercial purposes or from which such resident derives any rental income."
History note in statute: "P.A. 24-46 ... added Subdiv. (83) re dwelling or motor vehicle owned by certain service members with service-connected permanent and total disability rating, effective October 1, 2024, and applicable to assessment years commencing on or after October 1, 2024."
OLR 2025-R-0084 (July 31, 2025, https://prdext3.cga.ct.gov/olr/Documents/year/AA/2025AA-0084_2025%20Acts%20Affecting%20Veterans%20and%20the%20Military.pdf): "This year, the legislature specified that a veteran qualifies for the exemption if he or she is determined by the U.S. DVA to be permanently and totally disabled based on a 100% service-connected disability rating" (PA 25-2 §§4-6). Municipalities "must provide this exemption" (it is mandatory, not local-option).
Who qualifies: 100% P&T only. A 100% schedular rating that is not P&T stays under 12-81(20) ($3,500 tier). TDIU does NOT qualify for 12-81(83); PA 25-168 §240 created a separate municipal-option exemption for TDIU (see 1e). If VA later changes the rating "to other than permanently and totally disabled based on a service-connected disability rating of one hundred per cent, such modification shall be deemed a waiver of the right to the exemption."
Spouse / surviving spouse (12-81(83)(B)): if the veteran lacks a dwelling or vehicle in their own name, the spouse's dwelling/vehicle is exempt if the spouse is domiciled with the veteran. After death, "the dwelling or motor vehicle ... belonging to or held in trust for such deceased resident's surviving spouse ... while such spouse remains a widow or widower, or ... minor children during their minority ... while they are residents of this state, shall be so exempt." Municipal option (F): towns may extend it to surviving spouses of qualifying veterans who died before October 1, 2024.
Local options added by PA 25-168 (vary by town): (E) limit the exempt lot to two acres; (G) cap the exemption at the town's median assessed residential value.
Filing/deadline (PA 25-168 §235 amending CGS 12-93): "In the case of any person claiming exemption under subdivision (83) of section 12-81, such claimant shall annually, not later than January first, submit such claim to the assessors for approval, on an application form prepared for such purpose by the Secretary of the Office of Policy and Management ... which submission shall include (1) all documentation necessary to demonstrate that the resident ... has been determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs to be permanently and totally disabled based on a service-connected disability rating of one hundred per cent, and (2) an attestation that such claimant has not submitted, and will not submit, a claim for the exemption under subdivision (83) of section 12-81 in another town." (I.e., an ANNUAL application due by January 1 following the October 1 assessment date.) CGS 12-95 (as amended) lets 12-81(20)/(83)/TDIU claimants prove eligibility "at any time before the expiration of the time limited by law for the board of assessment appeals ... to complete its duties."
Portability: PA 25-168 §239 adds (83) to CGS 12-81cc so a certificate from one town can be used in another in the same assessment year. §238 extends (83) to leased motor vehicles and dwellings on leased land.
Only one of (19), (22), (23), (25), (26), (83) and the new TDIU/KIA-spouse options may be claimed (12-81(83)(C)).

### 1e. Municipal-option full exemption for TDIU — PA 25-168 §240 (new section, effective Oct. 1, 2025)
Quote: "Any municipality, upon approval by its legislative body, may provide that, in lieu of the exemption prescribed under subdivision (20) of section 12-81 ... any resident of this state who has served in the Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Coast Guard, Air Force or Space Force of the United States and has been determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs to have a service-connected total disability based on individual unemployability shall be entitled to an exemption from property tax on (1) that fractional share of a dwelling ... occupied by such resident as the resident's primary residence, or (2) lacking such residence, one motor vehicle ..." Same spouse/surviving-spouse, proof, two-acre and median-value options as 12-81(83). OLR 2025-R-0084 confirms: new municipal-option exemptions for "(2) state residents determined by U.S. DVA to have a service-connected total disability based on individual unemployability rating" (PA 25-168 §§234, 237 & 240-242). Whether a given town has adopted it must be checked with that town.

### 1f. Additional state exemption (income-tested) — CGS 12-81g; local options CGS 12-81f, 12-81jj, 12-81kk, 12-81h
CGS 12-81g(a): "any person entitled to an exemption from property tax in accordance with subdivision (19), (20), (21), (22), (23), (24), (25) or (26) of section 12-81 ... shall be entitled to an additional exemption from such tax in an amount equal to twice the amount of the exemption provided for such person pursuant to any such subdivision, provided such person's qualifying income does not exceed the applicable maximum amount as provided under section 12-81l, except that if such person has a disability rating of one hundred per cent ... adjusted gross income ... is not more than twenty-one thousand dollars if such person is married or not more than eighteen thousand dollars if such person is not married." OLR 2023-R-0175: those over the income limit instead get "50% of the basic exemption" (12-81g(d)). 12-81g(b): municipal option, in lieu of (a), of three times the 12-81(20) amount for 100%-rated veterans with AGI (excluding VA disability pay) up to $21,000 unmarried / $24,000 married.
OPM program page (https://portal.ct.gov/opm/igpp/grants/tax-relief-grants/additional-veterans-tax-relief-program, no date shown): "those veterans meeting specified income limits may apply for an additional State exemption totaling up to 200% of the local exemption"; "an application must be filed biennially with the Assessor's Office of the town of residence between February 1 and October 1st." Form M-59a (Rev. 12/2019): "FILING PERIOD FEBRUARY 1st - OCTOBER 1st"; "VETERANS' DISABILITY PAYMENTS ARE NOT CONSIDERED INCOME FOR THIS PROGRAM."
Income limits: CGS 12-81l ties the limit to the OPM-adjusted figures used for the elderly circuit-breaker program. OPM's 2026 Homeowners Q&A Booklet (2025 program year, https://portal.ct.gov/opm/-/media/opm/igpp-data-grants-mgmt/q-and-a-tax-relief-booklets/homeowners-qa-booklet.pdf): "Claimant's 2025 total income must not exceed $46,300 if unmarried, or $56,500 if married." A veterans-specific OPM table could not be located (old Q&A URL is 404), so treat the figure as needing confirmation with the assessor.
Local options (town must adopt; amounts vary): 12-81f — up to $20,000 or 10% of assessed value for 12-81(19) veterans and surviving spouses, at least $3,000 for 12-81(20) veterans, income-tested; 12-81jj — up to $5,000 or 5% for veterans ineligible for the state exemptions; 12-81kk — 10% of the primary residence's assessed value for veterans with federal AGI of $50,100 or less; 12-81h — percentage exemption for one specially equipped motor vehicle (per OLR 2023-R-0175).

### 2026 session changes (OLR 2026-R-0083, June 22, 2026, https://prdext3.cga.ct.gov/2026/rpt/pdf/2026-R-0083.pdf)
PA 26-114 §8 clarifies the revaluation increase-factor calculation (eff. Oct. 1, 2026); §§18, 20, 21 broaden acceptable discharge proof and make "the assessor, rather than the tax collector, responsible for receiving proof a claimant is retroactively eligible for an exemption based on his or her service-connected disability rating" (generally eff. Oct. 1, 2026); §17 extends the WWII allied-service exemption to other honorable-conditions discharges.

## 2. Vehicles

### 2a. Disabled veteran plates, free registration for up to three vehicles, overtime parking — CGS 14-49(o) and 14-254
CGS 14-49(o) (https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_246.htm): "No registration fee shall be charged to a disabled veteran, as defined in section 14-254, residing in this state for the registration of three passenger, camper or passenger and commercial motor vehicles leased or owned by such veteran in any registration year, provided such vehicles shall not be used for hire."
CGS 14-254 (https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_248.htm): "'Disabled veteran', as used in this section, means (1) any veteran, as defined in section 27-103, who performed service in time of war, as defined in section 27-103, and (2) (A) one or both of whose legs or arms or parts thereof have been amputated or the use of which has been lost, (B) who is blind, paraplegic or hemiplegic, or (C) who has traumatic brain injury, any such disability described in subdivision (2) of this section being certified as service-connected by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs. The Commissioner of Motor Vehicles, upon application of any disabled veteran accompanied by such certificate ... shall issue without charge a special number plate or set of plates ... No penalty shall be imposed for the overtime parking of any motor vehicle bearing a number plate issued under this section ... provided the length of time ... shall not exceed twenty-four hours. The surviving spouse of a disabled veteran issued such special registration may retain any such registration and number plates without charge for his or her lifetime or until such time as he or she remarries."
Important: eligibility is by TYPE of disability plus wartime service, not by percentage rating. The DMV guidance PDF "Disabled Veteran Registration (class code 14)" (https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/dmv/disabledvetcc14pdf.pdf, PDF metadata dated Nov. 4, 2013): "Three registrations per year may be issued to any disabled veteran as defined above for NO FEE (class code 14) when the veteran presents a letter from the Veterans Administration"; "The overtime parking card does not allow the use of handicapped parking spaces but does allow the vehicle to be parked 'overtime' up to 24 hours"; "Initial requests for the Disabled Veteran plate and registration must be made in person at the DMV Wethersfield Office only"; "all other fees apply, such as, title, lien, and clean air act fee, etc." No current DMV web page for this plate could be found (several candidate URLs return the DMV "page not found" template), so the in-person Wethersfield procedure needs confirmation with DMV.
Accessible parking placards (CGS 14-253a): a VA psychiatrist may certify a veteran with service-connected PTSD who also "meets the definition of a person with a disability which limits or impairs the ability to walk" for a removable windshield placard.

### 2b. Standard/wartime veteran plates (fee-based) and veteran flag on license
DMV special plates page (https://portal.ct.gov/dmv/vehicle-services/get-vanity-special-plates, no date shown): flag-style Veteran / wartime-specific plates for vehicles "owned or leased by an honorably discharged veteran or eligible surviving spouse"; "$27.50 for replacement, remake, and standard requests, and $96.50 for new vanity plates"; Form M-46 with DD-214. CTDVA plates article (dated May 31, 2024): "There is a fee associated with the Veteran Plate Program." Veteran indicator on license/ID: apply to CTDVA first (Form VL1 or DD-214), "no additional cost" at renewal (https://portal.ct.gov/dmv/licenses-permits-ids/change-driver-license/add-veterans-status-on-license-or-id). New: PA 26-35 §§7-9 "requires the DMV to waive the fee for an original driver's license or identity card for DVA-verified veterans" effective Oct. 1, 2026 (OLR 2026-R-0083) — DMV page not yet updated.
Motor vehicle property tax: the 12-81(20) exemption and the 12-81(83)/TDIU full exemption (when the veteran has no qualifying home) can be applied to a vehicle; leased vehicles qualify via CGS 12-93a as amended by PA 25-168 §238.

## 3. Recreation

### 3a. DEEP Disabled Veteran Pass (Life Use)
Source: https://portal.ct.gov/deep/state-parks/special-passes---ct-state-parks-and-forests ("Content last updated June 2024"). Quote: "available free to Connecticut residents who have a service-connected disability." Accepted for the pass holder at Gillette Castle, Dinosaur, and Fort Trumbull State Parks (fee-charging attractions); "not valid for camping" or special events. Apply in person at listed DEEP/park offices or by mail with "VA card or VA Benefits Letter showing a service-connected disability" and proof of CT residency to DEEP Disabled Veteran Pass, State Parks Division, 79 Elm Street, Hartford, CT 06106-5127. No minimum rating is stated.

### 3b. Passport to the Parks (all CT-registered vehicles)
Source: https://portal.ct.gov/DEEP/State-Parks/Passport-to-the-Parks ("Content last updated May 2024"). "Anyone in a Connecticut-registered vehicle can park free of charge at all CT State Parks and Forests year-round." Funded by a registration surcharge: "It is $8 per year, which means $24 for the customary three-year registration renewal"; "the law does not provide exemptions for any groups" — disabled veterans still pay the surcharge.

### 3c. Hunting and fishing licenses — no veteran-rating-based free license found
CGS 26-29b (https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_490.htm): "No fee shall be charged for any hunting, sport fishing or trapping license issued under this chapter to any person with physical disability, and such license shall be a lifetime license ... a 'person with physical disability' is any person whose disability consists of the loss of one or more limbs or the permanent loss of the use of one or more limbs." Physician/PA/APRN certification required. DEEP 2026 Hunting Guide page (https://portal.ct.gov/deep/hunting/2026-connecticut-hunting-and-trapping-guide/hunting-opportunities-for-persons-with-disabilities): "provides a free hunting license (permits and stamps not included) to Connecticut residents" who "have lost one or more limbs or have permanently lost the use of one or more limbs." CGS 26-29: free lifetime sport fishing license for blind persons, with proof "in the case of a veteran, by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs." DEEP fishing license page (https://portal.ct.gov/deep/fishing/general-information/fisheries-licenses-and-permits): residents 65+ fish free (annual renewal); "Any active, full-time member of the armed forces may be issued a fishing and/or small game firearms license ... for the resident fee." The hint that CGS 26-28b gives free lifetime licenses to 100% disabled veterans is incorrect: 26-28b is the general marine-waters fishing license requirement and contains no veteran provision; neither Chapter 490 nor DEEP's pages show any rating-based veteran license waiver.

## 4. Education

### Tuition waiver at CT public colleges — CGS 10a-77(d) (community colleges), 10a-99(d) (CSU), 10a-105(e) (UConn); PA 25-95 §§9-13
Statute (https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_185b.htm): the boards "shall waive the payment of tuition ... (1) for any dependent child of a person whom the armed forces of the United States has declared to be missing in action or to have been a prisoner of war while serving in such armed forces after January 1, 1960, which child has been accepted for admission to such institution and is a resident of the state ... (2) ... for any veteran, as defined in section 27-103, who performed service in time of war, as defined in section 27-103, except that ... 'service in time of war' shall not include time spent in attendance at a military service academy, which veteran has been accepted for admission to such institution and is domiciled in this state at the time such veteran is accepted for admission." Also covers active CT Army/Air National Guard members and (per CSCU) dependents/surviving spouses of CT residents killed in action on or after 9/11/2001.
CSCU page (https://www.ct.edu/admission/veterans, no date shown): veterans must have "at least 90 or more cumulative days active duty" "in time of war," be "honorably discharged or released under honorable conditions," and be "domiciled in Connecticut" at acceptance (domicile of less than one year counts). Waiver is "tuition only"; books, fees, parking, room and board are not waived; waivers may be reduced by employer reimbursement; Charter Oak offers "a 50% course fee waiver for eligible veterans who are matriculated"; CSU gives "a 50% course fee waiver ... for Extension Fund courses (summer and intersession credit courses)."
2025 change (OLR 2025-R-0084): PA 25-95 §§9-13, effective July 1, 2025, "expands the higher education tuition waiver program to cover extension fees for eligible National Guard members and veterans with wartime service ... including for courses taken during summer and winter school sessions or intersessions" and "establishes a tuition fee waiver for eligible National Guard members and veterans with wartime service who attend Charter Oak State College." The CSCU page still shows the older 50% language, so confirm with the campus veterans office. 2026: PA 26-12 §§69-71 extends the National Guard waiver to all mandatory fees effective July 1, 2027.
No disability rating is required; the waiver is service-based. There is no separate state tuition benefit tied to a VA disability rating for the veteran's children (only MIA/POW and post-9/11 KIA dependents).

## 5. State income tax

Connecticut has a personal income tax that starts from federal AGI (CGS 12-701(a)(20)).
Military retirement pay — CGS 12-701(a)(20)(B)(xvi) (https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_229.htm): subtract "any income received from the United States government as retirement pay for a retired member of (I) the Armed Forces of the United States ... or (II) the National Guard." History: "P.A. 15-244 amended Subsec. (a)(20)(B)(xvii) to increase deduction for Armed Forces and National Guard retirement income from 50 per cent of such income to any such income, effective July 1, 2015, and applicable to taxable years commencing on or after January 1, 2015."
DRS 2025 Form CT-1040 Instructions (Rev. 12/25, https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/drs/forms/2025/income/2025-ct-1040-instructions_1225.pdf), Line 44: "Subtract income received as military retirement pay, to the extent included in federal adjusted gross income, if you are a retired member of the armed forces of the United States or the National Guard (retired military member) or if you are a beneficiary receiving survivor benefits under an option or election made by a deceased retired military member. Payments received by a former spouse of a retired military member, under a final decree of divorce ... do not qualify." Also: "Military retirement pay and Tier 1 and Tier 2 railroad retirement benefits are fully exempt from Connecticut income tax." OLR 2025-R-0152 (Sept. 22, 2025): "Taxpayers may deduct 100% of their military retirement pay."
VA disability compensation: excluded from federal gross income under federal law, so it never enters federal AGI and is not taxed by Connecticut; no separate CT form line is needed. (DRS instructions apply the same principle to other benefits "not included in your federal adjusted gross income.")
2026: PA 26-35 §§18-19 and PA 26-68 §277 add deductions for honor-guard funeral detail pay, National Guard active-service pay, and USPHS Commissioned Corps retirement pay (effective July 1, 2026, varying tax years).

## 6. State employment preference — CGS 5-224
Source: https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_067.htm. Quote: "Any veteran who served in time of war, if such veteran is not eligible for disability compensation or pension from the United States Department of Veterans Affairs, or the spouse of such veteran who by reason of such veteran's disability is unable to pursue gainful employment, or the unmarried surviving spouse of such veteran, and if such person has attained at least the minimum earned rating on any examination held for an original appointment ... shall have five points added to his or her earned rating. Any such veteran ... if such person is eligible for such disability compensation or pension ... shall have ten points added to his or her earned rating. Any veteran who has served in a military action for which such person received or was entitled to receive a campaign badge or expeditionary medal, shall have five points added ... [and] any person who is a member of the armed forces ... in the final year of an enlistment contract ... shall have five points added." Credits are "based upon examinations with a possible rating of one hundred points."
The DAS "Military Talent" page (https://portal.ct.gov/das/services-for-state-employees/statewide-human-resources/military-talent, no date) describes a "Tailored recruitment process allowing veterans and spouses to voluntarily claim veterans' status" and veteran-only postings, but does not explain how the 5/10 points are applied under the current (largely exam-less) hiring process. Flagged for verification.

## 7. Other / unique to Connecticut

Skilled nursing admissions: 860-616-3708.
