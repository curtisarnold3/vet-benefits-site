## 1. Property tax exemption — first $150,000 of assessed value (50%+)

**Source:** AS 29.45.030 Required exemptions — https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#29.45.030 (Alaska Statutes 2025; no page-level revision date shown).

Quoted threshold language, AS 29.45.030(e):
> "The real property owned and occupied as the primary residence and permanent place of abode by a resident who is (1) 65 years of age or older; (2) a disabled veteran; or (3) at least 60 years of age and the widow or widower of a person who qualified for an exemption under (1) or (2) of this subsection is exempt from taxation on the first $150,000 of the assessed value of the real property. A municipality may by ordinance approved by the voters grant the exemption under this subsection to the widow or widower under 60 years of age of a person who qualified for an exemption under (2) of this subsection or to a resident who is the widow or widower of a person who dies from a service-connected cause sustained while serving as a member of the United States armed forces or as a member of the National Guard. A municipality may, in case of hardship, provide for exemption beyond the first $150,000 of assessed value in accordance with regulations of the department. ... In this subsection, 'widow or widower' means a person whose spouse has died and who has not remarried."

Definition, AS 29.45.030(i)(1):
> "'disabled veteran' means a disabled person (A) separated from the military service of the United States under a condition that is not dishonorable who is a resident of the state, whose disability was incurred or aggravated in the line of duty in the military service of the United States, and whose disability has been rated as 50 percent or more by the branch of service in which that person served or by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs; or (B) who served in the Alaska Territorial Guard, who is a resident of the state, whose disability was incurred or aggravated in the line of duty while serving in the Alaska Territorial Guard, and whose disability has been rated as 50 percent or more"

Application / deadline, AS 29.45.030(f):
> "An exemption may not be granted under (e) of this section except upon written application for the exemption. Each municipality shall, by ordinance, establish procedures and deadlines for filing the application. The governing body of the municipality for good cause shown may waive the claimant's failure to make timely application ... The assessor ... shall require a disabled veteran claiming an exemption under (e) of this section to provide evidence of the disability rating. The assessor may require proof under this subsection at any time."
Also (f): a municipality "may by ordinance require that an individual also meet" PFD eligibility for the same or preceding year.

(i)(2): "'real property' includes but is not limited to mobile homes."

**Who qualifies among 100% schedular / P&T / TDIU:** the statute tests only (a) the percentage ("rated as 50 percent or more"), (b) line-of-duty incurrence/aggravation, (c) non-dishonorable separation, (d) Alaska residency, (e) owner-occupied primary residence. P&T status and TDIU are not mentioned. Any veteran whose rating letter shows 50% or more meets the state threshold. Boroughs may require an annual letter if the rating is not marked permanent (see FNSB below).

**Surviving spouse:** widow/widower must be at least 60 and unremarried; municipalities may extend by voter-approved ordinance to under-60 spouses and to spouses of members who died from a service-connected cause.

**Administered by municipalities — examples (varies by borough/city):**
- OVA Taxes and Land page, https://veterans.alaska.gov/benefits/additional-benefits/taxes-and-land/ : "Property tax exemptions are at the discretion of the municipality the Veteran resides in. This exemption may transfer to a spouse if the Veteran passes away and the spouse is at least 60 years old. The amount of the exemption, how to apply for the exemption, and transfer applicability for the exemption are all set by the municipality. If you have questions about this benefit, contact the tax assessor's office in your municipality."
- Municipality of Anchorage, Property Appraisal Exemptions, https://www.muni.org/Departments/finance/property_appraisal/Exemption/pages/default.aspx : "Disabled Veteran Exemption: Up to $150,000 of the assessed value may be exempt for; Property owned and occupied as the primary residence and permanent place of abode by a Disabled Veteran, or A resident at least 60 years old who is the widow or widower of a person who qualified as a Disabled Veteran. Application deadline; March 15 of the applicable year." Phone (907) 343-6770.
- Fairbanks North Star Borough, "Disabled Veteran/Widow Widower Property Tax Exemption – Facts Sheet," https://www.fnsb.gov/DocumentCenter/View/24432/Facts-for-Disabled-Veterans-Exemption : "Timely filed applications are accepted through February 14th." "Applicants must have a service-connected disability rating of 50% or more. A current letter from the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs stating your disability rating percentage is required. The rating must be effective before January 1 of the tax year you are applying for." "Applications must be filed in person." "If your disability rating has not been determined permanent by the VA, you must submit a current disability rating letter annually by February 14." Widow/widower "is at least 60 years old by January 1 of the assessment year" and "must not be remarried." FNSB 2027 Homeowner Exemptions Enrollment Facts (https://www.fnsb.gov/DocumentCenter/View/27106/2027-Homeowner-Exemptions-Enrollment-Facts): "Open Enrollment is July 1, 2026 through February 14, 2027."

**State reimbursement note:** AS 29.45.030(g) says the state "shall reimburse" municipalities for the lost revenue but only "If appropriations are ... sufficient"; this does not change the veteran's benefit.

**2025–2026 law change check:** HB 169 (34th Legislature) would raise the $150,000 to $250,000; BASIS shows "Current Status (H) CRA Then FIN, Status Date 04/02/2025" — not enacted (https://www.akleg.gov/basis/Bill/Detail/34?Root=HB169). The Alaska Statutes 2025 text still reads $150,000.

## 2. Optional municipal exemption above $150,000

**Source:** AS 29.45.050(i) — https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#29.45.050
> "A municipality may by ordinance approved by the voters exempt from taxation the assessed value that exceeds $150,000 of real property owned and occupied as a permanent place of abode by a resident who is (1) 65 years of age or older; (2) a disabled veteran, including a person who was disabled in the line of duty while serving in the Alaska Territorial Guard; or (3) at least 60 years old and a widow or widower of a person who qualified for an exemption under (1) or (2) of this subsection."
Varies by municipality; voter approval required.

## 3. Vehicle: disabled veteran plates and free registration

**Statute:** AS 28.10.181(d) — https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#28.10.181
> "Upon the request of a person with a disability that limits or impairs the ability to walk, as defined in 23 C.F.R. 1235.2, the department shall (1) register one motor vehicle in the name of the person without charge; and (2) issue a specially designed registration plate that displays (A) recognition of the disabled veteran if the applicant's disability originated from the applicant's service with the Alaska Territorial Guard or the armed forces of the United States; (B) the international symbol of accessibility (the wheelchair logo) ... A person who is not otherwise qualified under this subsection, but who meets the qualifications of a disabled veteran under AS 29.45.030(i), may register one motor vehicle without charge, and the department shall issue a specially designed registration plate that displays recognition of the disabled veteran that does not display the international symbol of accessibility and does not carry with it special parking privileges."

**DMV page:** Alaska DMV, Disability Parking Permits & Plates — https://dmv.alaska.gov/vehicle-services/disability-parking-permits-plates/ (no last-updated date shown):
> "Disabled Veteran w/ Parking Privileges — $0 / $0 — Meet Permanent Disability Requirements (Form 861) and Proof of service-related disability of combined 50% or more from VA — Submit Application Online"
> "Disabled Veteran w/o Parking Privileges — $0 / $0 — Proof of service-related disability of combined 50% or more from VA — Submit Application Online"
> "Having a permanent disability allows a registered owner to have 1 free registration. This exemption is transferrable to another vehicle. ... You get one vehicle registration for free, but if you want disability plates on more vehicles, you'll need to pay the usual fees for each additional one."

OVA Passes, Permits & Plates page (https://veterans.alaska.gov/benefits/additional-benefits/license-and-license-plates/): "Disabled Veterans License Plates do not automatically come with parking privileges. To obtain a disabled Veterans license plate with parking privileges please make sure you submit a form 861 with your online application."

Threshold: 50% combined. Vehicles: one free registration. Parking: only with Form 861 (mobility disability).

**Veteran designation on license/ID:** DMV Military & Veteran Services, https://dmv.alaska.gov/credential-services/military-veteran-services/ : "If you are a retired veteran, or a veteran of the armed forces discharged under honorable conditions, you are eligible for a veteran designation on your license or ID card." Apply with Form D1 plus DD-214/DD-215, NGB-22/22A, VA letter, or retiree military ID. OVA: "There is no additional fee for the Veteran designation."

Veteran branch/unit plates: https://dmv.alaska.gov/vehicle-services/veteran-license-plates/ (proof of service required; not disability-based).

## 4. Hunting, fishing, trapping — free permanent ID card (50%+)

**Statute:** AS 16.05.400(b) — https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#16.05.400
> "Upon request, and without charge, the commissioner shall issue a permanent sport fishing, hunting, or trapping identification card to a resident who completes the forms required by the commissioner ... and who ... (2) has been discharged from military service under honorable conditions, is eligible for a loan under AS 18.56.101, and is certified by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs as having incurred a 50 percent or greater disability during military service; or (3) has served in the Alaska Territorial Guard ... and incurred a 50 percent or greater disability while serving in the Alaska Territorial Guard."
(c): the card or a license must be in the person's possession while hunting/fishing/trapping. Note: AS 16.05.341 now covers only active Guard/reserve members; the disabled-veteran benefit was moved to the permanent ID card by ch. 4 SLA 2023 (SB 10), which also removed the waterfowl conservation tag requirement for card holders (AS 16.05.340(a)(17)(A)(iv)).

**ADF&G page:** Military Licenses, "Alaska Resident Disabled Veteran License" — https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=license.military (no date shown). Per the page: a "complimentary permanent identification card" for residents who "meet the Department of Fish and Game's residency definition" and are "certified 50% disabled or greater"; "must be physically present in Alaska to apply"; card "void immediately if the holder is no longer an Alaska resident"; apply on the ADF&G online store and send the "letter of disability certification" to adfg.license@alaska.gov, fax (907) 465-2440, or ADF&G Licensing Section, P.O. Box 115525, Juneau, AK 99811-5525. Disqualifiers listed: resident license, voter registration, homestead exemption, or residency-based benefits in another state.

OVA Hunting, fishing, and trapping page (https://veterans.alaska.gov/benefits/additional-benefits/hunting-fishing-and-trapping/): "complimentary permanent identification card in order to hunt, sport fish, or trap to those who meet the Department of Fish and Game's residency definition and who are certified 50-percent disabled or greater. Applicants must be physically present in Alaska to apply." Also: resident disabled veterans fishing in annual-limit fisheries "must obtain and carry on them a free Sport Fishing Harvest Record Card."

## 5. Alaska State Parks — free disabled veteran camping pass

**Statute:** AS 41.21.026(d) — https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#41.21.026
> "The department shall issue free of charge to disabled veterans of this country an annual state park developed campsite permit. The permit shall allow a disabled veteran to use a state park developed campsite for personal recreation use without a charge or fee under (a)(2) of this section."

**DNR page:** Disabled Veteran's Annual Camping Pass — https://dnr.alaska.gov/parks/asp/vetpass.htm (no date shown):
> "Per AS 41.21.026 (d), Disabled Veterans (DAV) may receive one Alaska State Park Camping Pass free of charge. The DAV Camping Pass is valid in all developed Alaska State Park campgrounds ... The 2023 -2027 DAV Camping pass is available and can be used for camping in individual electric or non-electric campsites (DOES NOT INCLUDE GROUP CAMPING AREAS) ... the veteran must be physically present in the campsite each night ... There is no DAV pass for boat launch or day use, nor can the DAV camping pass be used to purchase a boat launch or day use pass. To receive a free DAV camping pass, an eligible disabled veteran must present proof of a service-connected disability at either the Anchorage or Fairbanks DNR Public Information Centers ... either: a Veteran's Administration Patient Data Card ... imprinted with 'service connected disability' ... or a current year letter from the US Department of Veteran's Affairs, which states the person has a service-connected disability ... AND a photo ID."
Replacement decal $10 (with remains) or $100 (lost, one time). Anchorage PIC 907-269-8400; Fairbanks PIC 907-451-2705.

DNR Fact Sheet "Veteran's Programs and the Department of Natural Resources" (September 2025), https://dnr.alaska.gov/mlw/cdn/pdf/factsheets/veterans-programs.pdf : "The percentage of disability on the letter does not influence qualifying." Threshold therefore = any service-connected disability.

**2025–2026 law change check:** SB 30 (CSSB 30(RES)) would make the permit lifetime and add developed-facility parking and restrooms; BASIS shows "Current Status (S) FIN, Status Date 02/19/2025" — not enacted (https://www.akleg.gov/basis/Bill/Detail/34?Root=SB30).

## 7. Education — University of Alaska tuition waiver (survivors only)

**Statute:** AS 14.43.085 — https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#14.43.085
> "(a) A person enrolled as a student in good standing in a state supported educational institution in this state is entitled to a waiver of undergraduate tuition and fees if the person was the spouse or dependent child of a bona fide resident of the state who was (1) a member of the armed services and who died in the line of duty or who died as a result of injuries sustained while in the line of duty for the state or federal government or who was listed by the United States Department of Defense as a prisoner of war or as missing in action"
(b)(6): "'resident' means a person who resided in the state for at least one year before serving in the armed services and who lists Alaska as the home state for purposes of military records". (b)(7): spouse means a person married to the member "at the time of the ... member's death."

UA Board of Regents Policy P05.10.080 (https://www.alaska.edu/bor/policy-regulations/chapter-05-10-tuition-student-fees.php, dated 11-10-22): "Consistent with AS 14.43.085 ... undergraduate resident or nonresident tuition and fees of for-credit courses, mandatory student fees will be waived for an eligible child or spouse of: a member of the Alaska National Guard, Alaska Naval Militia, or the armed services of the United States who died in the line of duty or who died as a result of injuries sustained while in the line of duty or who was listed by the United States Department of Defense as a prisoner of war or missing in action."

OVA Education page (https://veterans.alaska.gov/education/): "Spouse and dependents of armed service members who were Alaska residents and died in the line of duty, listed by the Department of Defense as a Prisoners of War, or were Killed in Action are entitled to a waiver of all undergraduate tuition and fees through the University of Alaska system."

**Important:** The hint that the waiver covers dependents of "100% disabled" veterans is NOT supported by the current statute or UA policy. HB 88 (2025) would add "permanent total disability" but shows "Current Status (H) EDC Then FIN, Status Date 02/10/2025" — not enacted (https://www.akleg.gov/basis/Bill/Detail/34?Root=HB88). No state tuition waiver for the disabled veteran himself/herself was found; OVA lists Alaska National Guard tuition assistance (up to 100% at UA) for current Guard members only.

## 8. Income tax and sales tax

**Statute:** AS 43.20.012(a) — https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#43.20.012
> "The tax imposed by this chapter does not apply to (1) an individual; (2) a fiduciary ..."
Alaska's Net Income Tax Act (AS 43.20) therefore taxes only corporations; there is no state personal income tax, so military retired pay and VA compensation are untaxed by the state and no state return exists.

**Sales tax:** No statute imposes a statewide general sales tax. AS 29.45.650(a) (https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#29.45.650): "a borough may levy and collect a sales tax on sales, rents, and services provided in the borough" — i.e., sales taxes are local only. (The Tax Division site https://www.tax.alaska.gov/ rendered no usable text; see Could not verify.)

## 9. State employment preference

**Statute:** AS 39.25.159 — https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#39.25.159
> "(a) A person who possesses the necessary qualifications for a position ... is entitled to an employment preference as follows: (1) in an assessment using numerical ratings, (A) points equal to five percent of the points available ... shall be added to the passing score if the person is in active service, on furlough from active service, or discharged under honorable conditions from service in the armed forces of the United States, a reserve unit ..., the Alaska Territorial Guard, the Alaska Army National Guard, the Alaska Air National Guard, or the Alaska Naval Militia; or (B) points equal to 10 percent of the points available ... shall be added to the passing score if the person is (i) a disabled veteran; (ii) a former prisoner of war; (2) in an assessment not using numerical ratings, (A) consideration shall be afforded to (i) a person under (1)(A) ...; (ii) the spouse of a person under (1)(A) ...; (iii) a dependent child or ... the surviving spouse of a person under (1)(B) ... who died within the 10-year period ...; (B) an opportunity to interview for the position shall be afforded to a disabled veteran or a former prisoner of war."
(d): "If the recruitment for a position is limited to state employees, preference under (a) of this section may not be counted." (f)(3): "'disabled veteran' means a veteran who is entitled to compensation under laws administered by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs, a person who was honorably discharged or released from active duty because of a service-connected disability, or a person who was disabled in the line of duty while serving in the Alaska Territorial Guard".

OVA Veteran Preference page (https://veterans.alaska.gov/employment/veteran-preference/): "Ten-point Veterans, AS 39.25.159 (a) and (c) required qualified Veterans having 10 Veterans' preference points be offered an interview for all open competitive vacancies (vacancies for All Alaska Residents). This preference does not extend to All Department Employees or All State Employees recruitments." "Workplace Alaska automatically identifies 10-poin[t] Veterans. If you are the selected applicant as a 10-point Veteran, you are required to provide a copy of the DD-214, DD-215, or a letter from the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs." Division of Personnel "Employment Considerations" guidance (rev. 6/20/13, https://doa.alaska.gov/dop/fileadmin/StatewideHR/pdf/EmploymentConsiderationsGuidance.pdf): "Except in limited (pre-approved) circumstances the State of Alaska does not use validated, written entrance exams with numerical ratings."

Note: the OVA page and 2013 DOP guidance describe the pre-2022 statute (the 8-year Guard rule and subsection (c) were repealed by ch. 54 SLA 2022, which added spouse/survivor consideration). The statute text above is current.

## 10. Veterans land discount and preference auction

**Statute:** AS 38.05.940 — https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#38.05.940
> "(a) An eligible veteran is entitled to a discount of 25 percent on the purchase price of state land sold under this title and classified ... for a use other than commercial or industrial use. (b) ... the veteran (1) is 18 years of age or older on the date of sale; (2) has been a state resident for a period of not less than one year immediately preceding the date of sale; and (3) has served (A) in the Alaska Territorial Guard for at least 90 days, unless the service was shortened due to a service connected disability; or (B) on active duty in the U.S. Armed Forces at least 90 days, unless tenure was shortened due to a service connected disability or due to receiving an early separation upon return from a tour of duty overseas, and has received an honorable discharge or a general discharge under honorable conditions. (c) A veteran is entitled to only one discount under this section during the veteran's lifetime."
DNR Fact Sheet (September 2025, https://dnr.alaska.gov/mlw/cdn/pdf/factsheets/veterans-programs.pdf): "eligible veterans can receive a 25 percent discount, minus development costs ... (see AS 38.05.940 and 11 AAC 67.891 - .897) ... Applications for the discount must be made at the time of purchase or before entering land sale contract." Veteran's Preference Auction (AS 38.05.067): parcels "five acres or less and residentially zoned"; once per lifetime; cannot be combined with the discount. **Discrepancy:** the OVA Taxes and Land page still says "a 20% discount"; the statute and DNR say 25%.

## 13. Permanent Fund Dividend — military allowable absence

**Statute:** AS 43.23.008(a)(3) — https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp#43.23.008 : an otherwise eligible individual absent during the qualifying year remains eligible if absent "serving on active duty as a member of the uniformed services of the United States or accompanying, as that individual's spouse, minor dependent, or disabled dependent, an individual who is (A) serving on active duty ...; and (B) eligible for a current year dividend". (b): "An individual may not claim an allowable absence under (a)(1) — (16) of this section unless the individual was a resident of the state for at least six consecutive months immediately before leaving the state." (d): after more than 180 days absent in each of five preceding years the department presumes non-residency, rebuttable by clear and convincing evidence including 30 cumulative days physically present in the past five years.

PFD Division pages: Absence Guidelines, https://pfd.alaska.gov/Eligibility/Absence-Guidelines ("You must report ALL absences over 90 days in a calendar year"; general 180-day rule); Military Eligibility, https://pfd.alaska.gov/Eligibility/Military-Eligibility ("you (unless you qualify for a waiver), your spouse and dependent children must return to Alaska for at least 72 consecutive hours every two years and provide proof of travel"; LES required to prove Alaska state of legal residence; establishing residency requires arrival and claiming Alaska as legal residence by December 31 of the year before the qualifying year).
