## 1. Property tax (filed with the county property appraiser; deadline March 1)

Florida's veteran property-tax benefits are set by state statute and are uniform statewide; only the forms/documentation handling differs by county property appraiser.

### 1a. Total homestead exemption — service-connected total and permanent disability (F.S. 196.081)
- Source: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.081.html ("The 2026 Florida Statutes")
- Threshold language: "Any real estate that is owned and used as a homestead by a veteran who was honorably discharged with a service-connected total and permanent disability and for whom a letter from the United States Government or United States Department of Veterans Affairs or its predecessor has been issued certifying that the veteran is totally and permanently disabled is exempt from taxation, if the veteran is a permanent resident of this state on January 1 of the tax year for which exemption is being claimed or was a permanent resident of this state on January 1 of the year the veteran died."
- TDIU / 100% schedular / 100% P&T: the statute keys on a VA letter certifying the veteran is "totally and permanently disabled." 100% P&T clearly qualifies. A 100% schedular rating that is not designated permanent does not match the statutory wording. TDIU is not mentioned anywhere in the statute; a TDIU veteran whose VA letter carries the permanent-and-total designation would present the same letter, but no official Florida source addresses TDIU by name.
- Proof: "The production by a veteran or the spouse or surviving spouse of a letter of total and permanent disability from the United States Government or United States Department of Veterans Affairs ... before the property appraiser ... is prima facie evidence."
- Surviving spouse (sub. 3): "If the totally and permanently disabled veteran predeceases his or her spouse and if, upon the death of the veteran, the spouse holds the legal or beneficial title to the homestead and permanently resides thereon ... the exemption from taxation carries over to the benefit of the veteran's spouse until such time as he or she remarries or sells or otherwise disposes of the property. If the spouse sells the property, the spouse may transfer an exemption not to exceed the amount granted from the most recent ad valorem tax roll to his or her new residence, as long as it is used as his or her primary residence and he or she does not remarry."
- Surviving spouse of a veteran "who died from service-connected causes while on active duty" also gets a total homestead exemption (sub. 4), same carryover/remarriage rules.
- Apply before the VA letter arrives (sub. 5): exemption "shall be granted as of the date of the original application, and the excess taxes paid shall be refunded," limited to the 4-year period in s. 197.182(1)(e).
- Mid-year purchase (sub. 1(b)): a veteran/surviving spouse who acquires a home between January 1 and November 1 gets "a refund, prorated as of the date of transfer, of the ad valorem taxes paid for the newly acquired property" if the exemption is granted for the next tax year.
- 2025–2026 bills: SB 290 (2025) and SB 450 / HB 393 (2026), which would have removed the cap on the surviving spouse's portable amount, all died in committee (flsenate.gov bill pages, last action 6/16/2025 and 3/13/2026). No change enacted.

### 1b. $5,000 exemption — 10% or more service-connected (F.S. 196.24)
- Source: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.24.html
- Threshold language: "Any ex-servicemember ... who is a bona fide resident of the state, who was discharged under honorable conditions, and who has been disabled to a degree of 10 percent or more by misfortune or while serving during a period of wartime service ... Property to the value of $5,000 of such a person is exempt from taxation."
- Surviving spouse: "The unremarried surviving spouse of such a disabled ex-servicemember is also entitled to the exemption."
- DOR PT-109 (R. 08/24), https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt109.pdf: "may be entitled to a $5,000 reduction in his or her property's assessed value. This exemption is not limited to homestead property."
- Hillsborough County Property Appraiser (https://www.hcpafl.org/Property-Info/Homestead-Other-Exemptions) describes it as "Available to any service connected disability of less than 100%," requires "A current letter from US Government or Veteran's Affairs with percent of disability and award date," and states "This exemption is automatically renewed." Form DR-501.

### 1c. Combat-related disability discount, age 65+ (F.S. 196.082; Form DR-501DV)
- Source: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.082.html (last amended ch. 2024-101)
- Threshold language: "Each veteran who is age 65 or older and is partially or totally permanently disabled shall receive a discount from the amount of the ad valorem tax otherwise owed on homestead property that the veteran owns and resides in if: (a) The disability was combat-related; and (b) The veteran was honorably discharged upon separation from military service. (2) The discount shall be in a percentage equal to the percentage of the veteran's permanent, service-connected disability as determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs."
- Filing: "an applicant must submit to the county property appraiser by March 1: (a) An official letter from the United States Department of Veterans Affairs which states the percentage of the veteran's service-connected disability and evidence that reasonably identifies the disability as combat-related; (b) A copy of the veteran's honorable discharge; and (c) Proof of age as of January 1." Late filers "may file an application for the discount and may file ... a petition with the value adjustment board."
- Surviving spouse carryover (sub. 3): same hold-title/reside/remarry rule; a dollar amount "not to exceed the dollar amount granted from the most recent ad valorem tax roll may be transferred to his or her new residence."
- Mechanics (sub. 6): other exemptions are deducted first, then the disability-percentage share of the remaining value is subtracted.

### 1d. Wheelchair / quadriplegic / paraplegic total exemptions (F.S. 196.091 and 196.101)
- 196.091, http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.091.html: homestead of "an ex-servicemember who has been honorably discharged with a service-connected total disability and who has a certificate ... certifying that the ex-servicemember is receiving or has received special pecuniary assistance due to disability requiring specially adapted housing and required to use a wheelchair for his or her transportation is exempt from taxation." Spouse carryover only if the homestead was held "as an estate by the entirety."
- 196.101, http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.101.html: "(1) Any real estate used and owned as a homestead by any quadriplegic is exempt from taxation. (2) Any real estate used and owned as a homestead by a paraplegic, hemiplegic, or other totally and permanently disabled person ... who must use a wheelchair for mobility or who is legally blind, is exempt from taxation." Subsection (2) only is subject to a household gross-income cap (statutory base $14,500, indexed annually; "gross income" includes VA and Social Security benefits). Proof: certificate from two Florida licensed doctors or from the VA (Form DR-416, R. 01/25). I did not retrieve the current-year indexed income figure.

### 1e. Deployed servicemember exemption (F.S. 196.173) — 2026 law change
- Source: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.173.html (amended ch. 2026-239)
- Additional homestead exemption for servicemembers "deployed during the preceding calendar year on active duty outside the continental United States, Alaska, or Hawaii in support of" listed operations (new 2026 additions include Operation Southern Spear, 2025). Amount = taxable value x days deployed / days in year. Normal deadline March 1 (Form DR-501M26). Session-law note: "For the 2026 ad valorem tax roll, the deadline ... is August 1, 2026," and for the newly added operations a servicemember "may include ... the total number of days he or she was on qualifying deployments during the 2023, 2024, and 2025 calendar years."

### 1f. Deadlines, forms, where to file
- F.S. 196.011(1)(a): "shall, on or before March 1 of each year, file an application for exemption with the county property appraiser ... Failure to make application ... on or before March 1 of any year shall constitute a waiver of the exemption privilege for that year, except as provided in subsection (8) or subsection (9)." (Hillsborough PA: late applications accepted "until the 25th day after the yearly TRIM notice is mailed (typically August)".)
- DOR Forms page (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Forms.aspx): DR-501 "Original Application for Homestead and Related Tax Exemptions" (R. 01/26); DR-501DV (R. 11/21); DR-501M26 (provisional); DR-416 (R. 01/25).
- DOR Exemptions page (https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/Taxpayers_Exemptions.aspx): applications go to "the property appraiser in the county where the property is located." No last-updated date shown.
- County-level caveat: the Hillsborough PA page says the total exemption "is inherited by the surviving spouse so long as she/he had been married to the veteran" for 5 years at death. No such 5-year marriage requirement appears in s. 196.081. Treat county web summaries with care and rely on the statute.

## 2. Vehicles, driver license, parking, tolls

### 2a. Free "DV" license plate (F.S. 320.084) and parking-fee exemption (F.S. 316.1964)
- Source: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0320/Sections/0320.084.html (last amended ch. 2025-155 and ch. 2026-39)
- Threshold language: "One free disabled veteran motor vehicle license number plate shall be issued ... for use on any motor vehicle owned or leased by any disabled veteran who has been a resident of this state continuously for the preceding 5 years or has established a domicile in this state as provided by s. 222.17(1), (2), or (3), and who has been honorably discharged ... upon application, accompanied by proof that: (a) A vehicle was initially acquired through financial assistance by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs ... specifically for the purchase of an automobile; (b) The applicant has been determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs ... to have a service-connected 100-percent disability rating for compensation; or (c) The applicant has been determined to have a service-connected disability rating of 100 percent and is in receipt of disability retirement pay."
- Note the threshold is a 100% rating for compensation; "permanent and total" is NOT required for the plate (unlike the property tax exemption and driver-license fee waiver).
- One free plate only. FLHSMV Procedure RS-17 (Revision Date 07/26, https://www.flhsmv.gov/pdf/proc/rs/rs-17.pdf): "Only one tax-free Disabled Veteran license plate or Disabled Veteran Wheelchair license plate can be issued to each disabled veteran with a 100 percent service-connected disability. Additional Disabled Veteran license plates may be issued by charging the regular registration tax and applicable fees." Even the free plate carries small service/branch/decal fees.
- Renewal: sub. (4)(c) requires "a certified statement as to the continued eligibility of the applicant."
- Option (sub. 6, from SB 736 (2024); FLHSMV Technical Advisory RSTL 25-014, 05/19/25): a DV-eligible veteran "may be issued, in lieu of the disabled veteran license plate, a military license plate for which he or she is eligible or a specialty license plate embossed with the initials 'DV'" but "must pay all applicable fees related to such license plate." FLHSMV: "The disabled veteran may only receive one free plate."
- FLHSMV Military page (https://www.flhsmv.gov/military/): "100 percent disabled veterans are eligible for a 'DV' license plate at no charge to the applicant." FLHSMV parking FAQ (https://www.flhsmv.gov/motor-vehicles-tags-titles/disabled-person-parking-permits/frequently-asked-questions/): "disabled veterans with a 'DV' license plate are exempt from paying parking fees statewide."
- F.S. 316.1964(1): no state agency, county or municipality "may ... exact any fee for parking on the public streets or highways or in any metered parking space from the driver of a vehicle that displays ... (b) A license plate issued under s. 320.084, s. 320.0842 ..." and "Such exemptions apply only if the vehicle is transporting the person who has a disability and to whom the ... license plate was issued." Sub. (8) also exempts DV-plated vehicles from fees in timed parking lots. Event venues may still charge (sub. 3).
- Purple Heart / Pearl Harbor plate (F.S. 320.089(1)): "an applicant for a Pearl Harbor Survivor license plate or a Purple Heart license plate who also qualifies for a disabled veteran's license plate under s. 320.084 shall be issued the appropriate special license plate without payment of the license tax imposed by s. 320.08."

### 2b. DV wheelchair plate (F.S. 320.0842)
- Source: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0320/Sections/0320.0842.html
- "the department shall issue to such person a free motor vehicle license plate, similar in all respects to the plate issued under s. 320.084, with the exception that the designation 'DV' is replaced by the international symbol of accessibility." Must be DV-eligible and offer "proof that due to a service-connected disability he or she permanently uses a wheelchair or proof ... that he or she qualifies for a disabled parking permit." "The license plate entitles the person to all privileges afforded by a parking permit issued under s. 320.0848." (The $28 fee FLHSMV lists is for the non-veteran wheelchair plate; RS-17 says the first DV wheelchair plate is tax-free.)

### 2c. Tolls — NO DV-plate toll exemption found
- F.S. 338.155, http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0338/Sections/0338.155.html: the only disability toll exemption is sub. (3): "A person with a disability who has a valid driver license, who operates a vehicle specially equipped for use by persons with disabilities, and who is certified by a physician ... or by the Adjudication Office of the United States Department of Veterans Affairs ... as being severely physically disabled and having permanent upper limb mobility or dexterity impairments that substantially impair the person's ability to deposit coins in toll baskets shall be allowed to pass free through all tollgates."
- FDOT Toll Permit page (https://www.fdot.gov/ctd/toll-permit) lists the same criteria and makes no mention of DV or wheelchair plates. The commonly repeated claim that a DV plate with wheelchair symbol exempts a veteran from tolls is not supported by any official source I found.

### 2d. Free driver license / ID card for 100% P&T (F.S. 322.21(7)) and "Veteran" designation (F.S. 322.14(1)(d))
- Source: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0322/Sections/0322.21.html (amended ch. 2026-39)
- "Any veteran honorably discharged from the Armed Forces who has been issued a valid identification card by the Department of Veterans' Affairs in accordance with s. 295.17, has been determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs ... to have a 100-percent total and permanent service-connected disability rating for compensation, or has been determined to have a service-connected total and permanent disability rating of 100 percent, is in receipt of disability retirement pay ... and who is qualified to obtain a driver license under this chapter is exempt from all fees required by this section." (This section sets both driver-license and ID-card fees.)
- FLHSMV Military page: 100% service-connected disabled veterans "may get a no-fee driver license (with endorsements) or ID card" on showing a VA ID card showing "a 100% total and permanent service-connected disability rating."
- "Veteran" designation: F.S. 322.14(1)(d) — "The word 'Veteran' must be exhibited on the driver license of a veteran upon the presentation of a copy of the person's: a. DD Form 214 ... b. Veteran health identification card ... c. Veteran identification card ..." FLHSMV: replacement is free when the designation is the only change.

### 2e. Sales tax on VA automobile-grant purchases (F.S. 212.08(7)(nn))
- "When a veteran of the armed forces purchases an aircraft, boat, mobile home, motor vehicle, or other vehicle from a dealer pursuant to the provisions of 38 U.S.C. s. 3902(a) ... the amount that is paid directly to the dealer by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs is not taxable. However, any portion of the purchase price which is paid directly to the dealer by the veteran is taxable."
- 2026 bill: CS/HB 665 (sales-tax exemption on new vehicles for 100% service-connected veterans) "Died in State Affairs Committee" 3/13/2026 (https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/665). No such exemption exists.

## 3. Hunting, fishing, state parks

### 3a. No-cost Persons with Disabilities Resident Hunting/Fishing License (F.S. 379.353(1))
- Statute: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0379/Sections/0379.353.html (amended ch. 2024-251)
- "(1) The commission shall issue without fee hunting, freshwater fishing, and saltwater fishing licenses and permits to any resident who is certified or determined to be: (a) ... totally and permanently disabled ... by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs ... or who holds a valid identification card issued under s. 295.17 ... (c) A disabled veteran of the United States Armed Forces who was honorably discharged upon separation from service and who is certified by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs ... as having a service-connected disability percentage rating of 50 percent or greater ... Any license issued under this paragraph after July 1, 2024, expires after 5 years and must be reissued, upon request, every 5 years thereafter."
- FWC page (https://myfwc.com/license/accessibility/hunt-fish/, copyright 1999–2026, no update date): "a no-cost license"; 5-year term for veterans with "service-connected disability percentage of 50 percent or greater"; covers "Saltwater Fishing, Freshwater Fishing, and Hunting licenses; and Deer, Wildlife Management Area, Archery, Muzzleloading Gun, Crossbow, Turkey, Florida Waterfowl, Snook, and Lobster permits"; does not cover the Federal Duck Stamp/Migratory Bird Permit, tarpon tag, statewide alligator hunt, or quota hunts without exemptions. Apply at GoOutdoorsFlorida.com (FWC "will review the information provided and respond within 10 business days") or at a county tax collector's office with proof of Florida residency.
- Military Gold Sportsman's License (https://myfwc.com/license/recreational/military-gold/): "reduced-fee $20 annual license" for "active duty and retired members of the United States Armed Forces," reserves, Florida National Guard and Coast Guard "stationed in Florida or list Florida as their primary residence." Not a disabled-veteran benefit; non-retired veterans are not eligible.
- Residents in the Armed Forces "not stationed in this state, when home on leave for 30 days or less" need no license (379.353(2)(c)).

### 3b. State park passes (F.S. 258.0145)
- Source: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0200-0299/0258/Sections/0258.0145.html
- "(1) Active duty members and honorably discharged veterans of the United States Armed Forces, National Guard, or reserve components thereof shall receive a 25-percent discount on annual entrance passes. (2) Honorably discharged veterans who have service-connected disabilities shall receive lifetime family annual entrance passes at no charge. (3) Surviving spouses and parents of deceased members ... who have fallen in combat shall receive lifetime family annual entrance passes at no charge."
- No minimum disability percentage in the statute. floridastateparks.org pages were blocked; search snippets from that site said passes must be issued in person at a park ranger station with DD-214 plus VA/DoD disability documentation — not independently verified.
- County and city parks: ch. 2016-196 (SB 1202, https://laws.flrules.org/2016/196) created s. 125.029 and s. 166.0447 requiring county and municipal park departments to "provide a partial or a full discount on park entrance fees" to active duty, honorably discharged veterans, veterans with a service-connected disability, and surviving spouses/parents of combat dead. The size of the discount varies by county/city.

## 4. Education

### 4a. Congressman C.W. "Bill" Young Veteran Tuition Waiver (F.S. 1009.26(13)) and transcript-fee waiver (1009.26(17))
- Source: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=1000-1099/1009/Sections/1009.26.html
- "A state university, Florida College System institution, career center ... or charter technical career center shall waive out-of-state fees for a person who is: 1. An honorably discharged veteran ... who physically resides in this state while enrolled in the institution; or 2. Entitled to and uses educational assistance provided by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs ... who physically resides in this state while enrolled." "Tuition and fees charged ... may not exceed the tuition and fees charged to a resident student."
- (17): institutions "shall waive the transcript fee for a person who is an active duty member or an honorably discharged veteran ... and his or her spouse and dependents."

### 4b. Purple Heart / combat decoration tuition waiver (F.S. 1009.26(8))
- "shall waive tuition for undergraduate college credit programs and career certificate programs for each recipient of a Purple Heart or another combat decoration superior in precedence who ... (b) Is currently, or was at the time of the military action ... a resident of this state; and (c) Submits ... the DD-214." Good for "110 percent of the number of required credit hours."

### 4c. Disabled veteran GI Bill gap waiver (F.S. 295.011)
- Source: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0200-0299/0295/Sections/0295.011.html
- "disabled veteran" = Florida resident under s. 1009.21 who is "(a) Determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs to have a service-connected 100-percent total and permanent disability rating for compensation; (b) ... 100 percent and is in receipt of disability retirement pay ...; or (c) Issued a valid identification card ... in accordance with s. 295.17." Such a veteran "enrolled in a program of education approved for educational assistance under 38 U.S.C. s. 3313 [Post-9/11 GI Bill] ... who does not qualify for the 100-percent eligibility tier under federal law is eligible to receive a waiver for tuition and fees. The waiver amount is equal to the difference between the portion of tuition and fees paid in accordance with federal law and the full amount of tuition and fees."

### 4d. Scholarships for Children and Spouses of Deceased or Disabled Veterans (CSDDV) (F.S. 295.01–295.04)
- Statute: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0200-0299/0295/Sections/0295.01.html (amended ch. 2023-279). Fact sheet: https://www.floridastudentfinancialaidsg.org/PDF/factsheets/CSDDV.pdf (labeled "2026-27 Fact Sheet").
- Qualifying veteran: "1. Died as a result of service-connected injuries, disease, or disability sustained while on active duty; or 2. Has been: a. Determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs ... to have a service-connected 100-percent total and permanent disability rating for compensation; b. ... 100 percent and is in receipt of disability retirement pay ...; or c. Issued a valid identification card by the Department of Veterans' Affairs in accordance with s. 295.17." POW/MIA children also covered (295.015).
- Residency: child eligible if "Immediately preceding the occurrence of the servicemember's disability, Florida was listed as the servicemember's official home of record in the Defense Enrollment Eligibility Reporting System (DEERS) database; or ... The child qualifies as a resident for tuition purposes under s. 1009.21 and the servicemember is a resident of this state."
- Child age: F.S. 295.02 — "between the ages of 16 and 22 years"; a child reaching 22 mid-course "may continue the course."
- Spouse: of a disabled servicemember, must "have been married to each other for 1 year," benefit lasts "only during the duration of the marriage"; of a deceased servicemember, must be unremarried and apply "within 5 years after the servicemember's death."
- Award (295.04 / fact sheet): at a public institution "an award equal to the amount required to pay tuition and registration fees"; at a private institution the average public tuition/fees at the comparable level.
- Deadline (fact sheet): "submit a completed, error-free Florida Financial Aid Application (FFAA) by April 1, 2027, for priority consideration of an initial award. The FFAA is available beginning on October 1, 2026." FDVA certifies the veteran; documents go to FDVA's portal.

## 5. State income tax
- Florida has no personal income tax. DOR FAQ #1466 (https://floridarevenue.com/faq/Pages/FAQDetails.aspx?FAQID=1466): "Florida does not impose a personal income tax, so there are no filing requirements," citing Article VII, Section 5 of the Florida Constitution. Military retired pay, VA compensation, SBP/DIC and all other income are therefore untaxed at the state level. (Florida does levy a 5.5% corporate income tax, ch. 220.)

## 6. State employment preference (F.S. 295.07, 295.08, 295.085)
- Sources: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0200-0299/0295/Sections/0295.07.html (amended ch. 2025-22); http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0200-0299/0295/Sections/0295.08.html
- Who: (1)(a) "disabled veterans ... [who] have established the present existence of a service-connected disability that is compensable" (any percentage), or receiving VA/DoD compensation, disability retirement or pension; (b) spouse of a person with "a total disability, permanent in nature, resulting from a service-connected disability and who, because of this disability, cannot qualify for employment" (and spouses of MIA/POW); (c) wartime veterans; (d) unremarried widow/widower of a veteran who died of a service-connected disability; (e) parents/guardian/unremarried widow(er) of a member who died in the line of duty under combat-related conditions; (f) any veteran; (g) current reserve/Guard members.
- Points (295.08): "20 points must be added to the earned ratings of a person included under s. 295.07(1)(a) or (b), 15 points ... under s. 295.07(1)(c), (d), or (e), and 10 points ... under s. 295.07(1)(f) or (g)." And: "the names of all persons who are qualified to receive a 20-point preference whose service-connected disabilities have been rated ... to be 30 percent or more must be placed at the top of the appropriate register or employment list."
- Non-scored positions (295.085): "first preference ... to a person included under s. 295.07(1)(a) or (b), and second preference ... (c), (d), (e), (f), or (g)."
- Covers "the state and its political subdivisions" (counties, cities, school districts), Career Service positions; exempt positions listed in 295.07(5). Educational-requirement waiver available (295.07(2)). FDVA form VP-1 / People First application section is the claim mechanism (DMS page could not be fetched).

## 7. Other Florida-specific benefits
- FDVA 100% P&T identification card (F.S. 295.17): issued to a permanent resident "adjudged ... to have a 100-percent, service-connected permanent and total disability rating for compensation"; "may be used by the veteran as proof of eligibility for any benefit provided by state law for 100-percent, service-connected permanently and totally disabled veterans except those benefits provided by ss. 196.081, 196.091, and 196.24" (property tax still needs the VA letter).
- DBPR professional license fee waiver (F.S. 455.213(13), http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0400-0499/0455/Sections/0455.213.html): "The department shall waive the initial licensing fee, the initial application fee, and the initial unlicensed activity fee for a military veteran or his or her spouse at the time of discharge, if he or she applies ... within 60 months after the veteran is discharged ... the veteran must have been honorably discharged." No disability-percentage requirement (the old 50% rule is gone).
- Local business tax exemption (F.S. 205.055): "A veteran of the United States Armed Forces who was honorably discharged upon separation from service, or the spouse or unremarried surviving spouse of such a veteran" is "entitled to an exemption from a business tax and any fees imposed under this chapter"; must sign a "Request for Fee Exemption" furnished by the county/city; a business the veteran majority-owns with fewer than 100 employees is also exempt. Administered locally.
- State Veterans' Nursing Homes (F.S. 296.36, http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0200-0299/0296/Sections/0296.36.html): applicant must "(a) Be in need of nursing home care. (b) Be a resident of the state at the time of application ... (e) Have been approved as eligible for care and treatment by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs"; spouses and surviving spouses are eligible (ch. 2024-250); priority first to Florida-resident veterans, then those with service-connected disabilities. FDVA locations page (https://floridavets.org/locations/state-veterans-nursing-homes/) could not be fetched.
- Homestead protections for servicemembers: renting out the homestead while serving does not abandon the exemption (s. 196.061); next of kin may file on the servicemember's behalf (s. 196.071) (PT-109).
