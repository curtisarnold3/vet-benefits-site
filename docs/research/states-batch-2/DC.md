# District of Columbia (DC) — Disabled Veteran Benefits Research

Verified: 2026-10-16. Official sources only (DC Code via code.dccouncil.gov, OTR, MOVA/communityaffairs.dc.gov, DC DMV, DOEE, DDOT, DFHV, UDC, OCFO). Web search budget ran out near the end of research; the "Could not verify" list notes what was affected.

**Official veterans agency:** Mayor's Office of Veterans Affairs (MOVA), within the Mayor's Office of Community Affairs. One Judiciary Square, 441 4th St NW, Suite 707 North, Washington, DC 20001. (202) 724-5454. https://communityaffairs.dc.gov/mova (ova.dc.gov now 301-redirects here). No last-updated date shown.

## 1. Property tax

### 1a. Disabled Veterans Homestead Deduction — $445,000 off assessed value

**Statute:** DC Code § 47-850(a-2) — https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/47-850 (fetched 2026-10-16; no page date shown). Exact text:

> "(a-2)(1) For purposes of levying the real property tax during a tax year, the Mayor shall deduct from the assessed value of real property that qualifies for the homestead deduction and is owned by a veteran or eligible spouse the amount of $445,000; provided, that the: (A) Veteran has been classified (or, in the case of an eligible spouse, had been classified at the time of the veteran's death) by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs as having a total and permanent disability as a result of a service-incurred condition or service-aggravated condition or is paid (or, in the case of an eligible spouse, had been paid at the time of the veteran's death) at the 100% disability rating level as a result of unemployability; and (B) Veteran's or eligible spouse's household is an eligible household as defined in § 47-863(a)(1A)(A); except, that § 47-863(a)(1A)(A)(iii)(I)(aa) and (II) shall not apply."

> "(3)(A) To qualify for and receive the deduction ... the veteran or eligible spouse ... shall complete and file with the District of Columbia Office of Veterans Affairs an application, in a form prescribed by the Mayor ..."

> "(4) ... the term 'eligible spouse' means the surviving spouse or domestic partner of a deceased veteran whose real property was validly receiving the deduction provided under this subsection at the time of the veteran's death or whose real property would have been eligible for such deduction at the time of the veteran's death had the veteran applied for such deduction."

Deadline language, § 47-850(c): "If a properly completed and approved application is filed during the period October 1 through March 31 of the tax year, the real property shall receive the deduction for the entire tax year. ... if a properly completed and approved application is filed during the period April 1 through September 30, the real property shall receive 1/2 of the deduction for the second installment only."

Threshold analysis: qualifies = VA "total and permanent disability" (100% P&T) OR "paid at the 100% disability rating level as a result of unemployability" (TDIU). A 100% schedular rating that is NOT permanent does not meet the statute's wording. The cross-reference to § 47-863(a)(1A)(A) imports the ownership (at least 50%), 5-or-fewer-units, Class 1A/1B property, and household income rules, but strikes the age-65 and SSA-disability requirements.

**Surviving spouse (2025–2026 law change):** D.C. Law 26-83, "Veteran Spouse Homestead Deduction Amendment Act of 2025" — https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/laws/26-83 — "To amend section 47-850 ... to allow a surviving spouse or domestic partner of a disabled veteran to claim the disabled veteran's homestead deduction." OCFO Fiscal Impact Statement (Oct 14, 2025, Bill 26-10): "The bill makes the expansion to eligible spouses effective October 1, 2025." — https://app.cfo.dc.gov/services/fiscal_impact/pdf/spring09/FIS%20Veteran%20Spouse%20Homestead%20Deduction%20Amendment%20Act%20of%202025.pdf

**OTR page:** "Real Property Tax Reliefs, Credits and Deductions" — https://otr.cfo.dc.gov/am/page/real-property-tax-reliefs-credits-and-deductions (no date shown). Quotes: "is eligible for reduction in assessed value of $445,000" (effective October 1, 2022); "The total household federal adjusted gross income for 2024 must be less than $163,500.00 for tax year 2026."; "Effective October 1, 2025, residential real property owned by a disabled veteran's eligible surviving spouse may qualify for a $445,000 reduction in assessed value."; "To apply online must visit communityaffairs.dc.gov/page/disabled-veterans-homestead-exemption-application."; properties receiving it "are not eligible for the Homestead Deduction, Senior Citizen/Disabled Tax Relief or the Assessment Cap Credit." Cooperative properties are not eligible.

**MOVA application page:** https://communityaffairs.dc.gov/node/4111 (no date shown). Requirements as stated: VA classification "totally and permanently disabled from a service-incurred or service-aggravated condition" or "paid at the 100% rating level due to unemployability"; "no more than five dwelling units"; principal residence; "at least 50% ownership, as shown by deed"; "domiciled in D.C."; household income under the Senior/Disabled Tax Relief limit, "$163,500 for tax year 2026". Property in an irrevocable trust (other than a special needs trust) or owned by a corporation/LLC is ineligible. Active-duty members must submit DD-2058 designating DC as domicile. Apply via the online "Veterans Homestead Tax Deduction Application" (Smartsheet). If you move, file Form ASD-105 on MyTax.DC.gov.

**OTR 2022 release:** https://otr.cfo.dc.gov/release/veterans-homestead-deduction-starts-october-1-2022 (dated Sep 30, 2022): same eligibility; filed Oct 1–Mar 31 = full year; Apr 1–Sep 30 = half on second-half bill; questions to MOVA (202) 724-5454 / OVA@DC.gov.

**Pending bill (not law):** Bill 26-476, "Disabled Veterans Complete Property Tax Exemption Amendment Act of 2025." OCFO testimony for the Committee of the Whole hearing July 10, 2026: "The Bill would provide for a 100% real property tax exemption for a home owned by a veteran who is wholly disabled as a result of service in the armed forces ... The income threshold applicable to senior and disabled tax relief is removed." — https://cfo.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/ocfo/release_content/attachments/OCFO%20Testimony-McKeon-Disabled%20Veterans%20Property%20Tax%20Exemption%20Act-B26-476.pdf. I could not confirm any vote or enactment (LIMS requires an access token). As of this research, the law is still the $445,000 deduction with the income cap.

County variation: none — DC is a single jurisdiction.

### 1b. Senior Citizen / Disabled Property Owner Tax Relief — 50% (not veteran-specific)

**Statute:** DC Code § 47-863(b)(1): "an eligible household shall be eligible for a 50% deduction in computing real property tax liability." Eligible household (§ 47-863(a)(1A)(A)(iii)): owned at least 50% by an individual who "Is 65 years of age or older" with household AGI under the indexed cap, OR who "Has been determined to have a permanent and total disability by the Social Security Administration, is receiving Supplemental Security Income or Social Security Disability, is receiving railroad retirement disability benefits, or is receiving federal or District of Columbia government disability payments" with household AGI "less than $125,000, increased annually, beginning October 1, 2014, by the senior or disabled cost-of-living adjustment." — https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/47-863

**OTR:** "This tax relief reduces a qualified property owner's property tax by 50 percent." "The total household federal adjusted gross income for 2024 must be less than $163,500.00 for tax year 2026." Owner can apply once eligible. Cannot be combined with the Disabled Veterans Homestead Deduction. Whether VA disability compensation alone counts as "federal ... government disability payments" is not stated on the OTR page — flagged for verification.

### 1c. Standard Homestead Deduction
OTR: "This deduction reduces your real property's assessed value by $91,950.00 for tax year 2026." Not available on a property receiving the $445,000 veterans deduction (the veterans deduction replaces it).

## 2. Vehicles

### 2a. No-fee tags for veterans with a service-connected permanent and total disability
**Source:** DC DMV, "Specialty Vehicle Tags" — https://dmv.dc.gov/service/specialty-vehicle-tags (no date shown). Quotes: veterans with a "service-connected permanent and total disability" may receive "one set of tags (i.e., DAV, Veteran, passenger, etc) at no fee for a non-commercial vehicle"; "the 'no fee' is only applicable to annual registration fees"; proof is a Department of Veterans Affairs letter indicating "Total and Permanent." Under the Woman Veteran tag section the page states such veterans "shall not be charged the application fee, registration fee or renewal fee for one set of tags," which "does not apply to commercial vehicles." Limit: one vehicle/one set of tags. I could not find the statutory basis in DC Code § 50-1501.03 (the fee schedule) — the waiver appears to be administrative/regulatory (18 DCMR); see "Could not verify."

### 2b. DC Veteran specialty tags (any honorably discharged veteran or spouse)
**Statute:** DC Code § 50-1501.02a — https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/50-1501.02a. "The Mayor shall issue a registration certificate and identification tags for a passenger motor vehicle ... to an individual who is a veteran or the spouse of a veteran, is a District resident ... Upon the death of a veteran that is a holder of identification tags issued under this section, the identification tags shall be transferred, upon application, to the surviving spouse for the spouses's lifetime or until he or she remarries." Fees: "The application fee shall be $52 and the display fee shall be $26." Veteran defined as active duty or Guard/Reserve called to active duty or 20 years' service, "discharged or released under conditions other than dishonorable." DMV page: proof is DD 214, WD AGO, or DD 256; tags only for passenger cars, pickups, vans. Annual registration fees still apply unless the P&T no-fee rule (2a) applies.

### 2c. Disabled American Veteran (DAV) tags
**Source:** DMV Specialty Vehicle Tags page. DAV tags "are available for a normal registration fees" to veterans with a passenger vehicle who are "certified as a member of the District of Columbia Disabled American Veterans by the Commander of the District of Columbia Disabled American Veterans"; also requires "the Department of Veterans Affairs authorization letter" and "an approved DC DMV Disability Tag/Placard application." DC Code § 50-1501.03(a)(3)(C) exempts DAV from the $100 organizational-tag application fee. No disability percentage is stated on the DMV page; eligibility runs through DAV membership plus DMV disability-tag approval.

### 2d. Purple Heart tags (new 2026 law)
**Statute:** DC Code § 50-1501.02o, added by D.C. Law 26-114, effective May 21, 2026 — https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/50-1501.02o. "A resident ordering a Purple Heart award recipient motor vehicle identification tag shall pay a one-time application fee of $10 ... There shall be no annual display fee." Available to the recipient "or as the surviving spouse of a Purple Heart recipient; provided, that the surviving spouse has not remarried." Proof: DD-214/DD-215, WD-AGO 53-55, award orders, or award certificate.

### 2e. Disability parking placards/tags and parking privileges (not veteran-specific)
**Sources:** DMV, "Apply for Disability Parking Placards and Permits" — https://dmv.dc.gov/service/apply-for-disability-parking-placards-and-permits: "There is no charge for a disability placard/permit."; "one placard and one set of vehicle tags may be issued for each applicant who is a DC resident." DMV placard info page https://dmv.dc.gov/node/1697081: holders may "Park for double the posted time in any metered spaces (unless prohibited by DDOT's Red Top Meter Program)" and "Park in any parking space designated for a disabled person." DDOT https://ddot.dc.gov/node/538322: "Park for double the posted time in red top metered spaces, after paying the established meter rate indicated on the meter." No DC source gives disabled veterans free metered parking or any veteran-specific parking privilege. Disability tags cost $10 per the DMV application PDF.

### 2f. Veteran designation on driver license / ID
**Source:** https://dmv.dc.gov/page/veteran-designation-0. "To be eligible for this designation, you must have been honorably discharged from a branch of the US armed forces." Provide "DD 214 form, WD AGO form, or DD 256 form" in person at a DMV service center. No separate fee listed.

## 3. Recreation

- **Fishing license:** DOEE, "Get a Fishing License" — https://doee.dc.gov/service/get-fishing-license (footer © 2023). "Purchase for District resident: $10.00"; "Purchase for non-resident: $13.00". Exemptions listed: "Persons under sixteen (16) years of age;" and "Persons sixty-five (65) years of age or older." No disabled-veteran or disability exemption appears. 14-day licenses discontinued January 2022.
- **Hunting:** Hunting is not permitted in the District; there is no DC hunting license program.
- **State parks:** DC has no state park system or park pass; DC Department of Parks and Recreation facilities are municipal. No veteran pass exists to verify.

## 4. Education

### 4a. UDC in-state tuition for GI Bill users living in DC
**Source:** UDC "Policy on Student Residency Classification" PDF — http://files-do-not-link.udc.edu/docs/admissions/Residency%20Policy.pdf (linked from https://www.udc.edu/military/). The policy grants the "District of Columbia in-state residency rate for tuition purposes, and the requirement for living in-state for 12 months is waived" for: "A veteran using educational assistance under either chapter 30 (Montgomery G.I. Bill – Active Duty Program) or chapter 33 (Post-9/11 G.I. Bill), of title 38, United States Code, who lives in the District of Columbia (regardless of his or her formal State of residence), and enrolls in the University of the District of Columbia within three years of discharge or release from a period of active duty service of 90 days or more"; also "Anyone using transferred Post-9/11 GI Bill benefits (38 U.S.C. § 3319) who lives in the District of Columbia ... within three years of the transferor's discharge"; and anyone above "while he or she remains continuously enrolled." Policy states it is to be read as amended to comply with 38 U.S.C. 3679(c). Contact: Office of Military and Veteran Student Services, (202) 274-6221 / 202-274-6099.

**No DC tuition waiver for disabled veterans or their dependents was found** on UDC or DC government sites. DC does not operate a state-funded dependents' tuition waiver like many states.

### 4b. DC National Guard Tuition Assistance
**Statute:** DC Code § 49-807 — https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/49-807: "Any funds contributed by the District of Columbia to the District of Columbia National Guard Tuition Assistance Program may be utilized ... for tuition assistance benefits for all members or for new recruits; provided, that the member or new recruit is a resident of the District of Columbia." Dollar caps are in a DC National Guard policy memo (dc.ng.mil) that I could not retrieve (403). Needs verification of amounts.

## 5. Income tax

- **DC has an income tax.** DC gross income starts from federal gross income (DC Code § 47-1803.02(a)). VA disability compensation is excluded from federal gross income, so it is not taxed by DC; DC has no separate statutory line for it (OTR testimony: "all income ... is included in federal gross income and, therefore, is included in District income tax absent a specific statutory exclusion").
- **Military retirement pay is fully taxable in DC.** DC Code § 47-1803.02(a)(2)(N)(i): the $3,000 exclusion for "Pension, military retired pay, or annuity income received from the District of Columbia or the federal government by persons who are 62 years of age or older" applies only "for taxable years beginning before January 1, 2015." — https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/47-1803.02. OTR testimony (Mar. 14, 2022, Bill 24-71): "the Fiscal Year 2015 Budget Support Act of 2014, effective February 26, 2015 (D.C. Law 20-155), effectively repealed this exclusion for all tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2015." — https://cfo.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/ocfo/release_content/attachments/OTR%20Testimony%20on%20Pension%20Exclusion%20and%20Restoration%20Act%20of%202021.pdf. The 2025 D-40 booklet (https://otr.cfo.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/otr/publication/attachments/2025_D40_Book_082026_v1.pdf) contains no military retirement subtraction line.
- **Survivor benefits:** § 47-1803.02(a)(2)(N)(ii) excludes "Survivor benefits received from the District of Columbia or the federal government by persons who are 62 years of age or older." 2025 D-40 instructions, Line 12: "If you are an annuitant's survivor and 62 years of age or older as of December 31, 2025, enter the total survivor benefits (do not include Social Security survivor benefits)." Whether DIC/SBP qualifies is not spelled out — needs verification.
- **Disability income exclusion (not veteran-specific):** § 47-1803.02(a)(2)(V): "Income derived from any source, not to exceed $10,000, for a person who has been determined to have a permanent and total disability by the Social Security Administration, is receiving Supplemental Security Income or Social Security Disability, is receiving railroad retirement disability benefits, or is receiving federal or District of Columbia government disability payments; and, whose household adjusted gross income ... is less than $100,000." Claimed on Form D-2440 (D-40 Schedule I, Line 2). Whether VA compensation counts as "federal ... government disability payments" for this purpose is not stated by OTR — needs verification.

## 6. Employment preference (DC government)

**Statute:** DC Code § 1-607.03 — https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/1-607.03.
- (a): persons who "served on active duty in the armed forces of the United States for more than 180 consecutive days ... and have separated from the armed forces under honorable conditions may receive an additional 5 points on any register."
- (b): "an additional 5 points if he or she has separated from the armed forces under honorable conditions, and has established the presence at the time of appointment of a service-connected disability or is receiving compensation, disability retirement benefits, or pensions because of a public law administered by the Veterans Administration or a military department."
- (e): "no person shall receive any appointment preference after 5 years from the date of separation from the armed forces" (except spouse/30%+/Vietnam provisions in (h)–(k)).
- (f): "No person entering the armed forces of the United States after October 14, 1976, shall receive any preference unless the person served in the armed forces of the United States during time of war."
- (g): "No person retiring from the armed forces of the United States shall receive any preference."
- (h)/(i): unremarried surviving spouse/domestic partner, and spouse/domestic partner of a service-connected veteran with a disability, get the federal-equivalent preference. (j): "A person classified as 30 percent or more disabled under subsection (b) of this section shall receive an appointment preference as provided in that subsection."
- Applies to Career Service and Educational Service appointments (subchapters VIII and VIII-A). No residency requirement in this section.

**Separate residency preference:** DC Code § 1-515.02 — https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/1-515.02: "an individual who is a District resident at the time of application shall be awarded a 10-point hiring preference over a nonresident applicant; provided, that the individual claims the preference," with a written agreement "to remain a District resident for a period of 7 consecutive years." This is for all residents, not a veteran benefit.

- **VetsRide (free rides):** DFHV — https://dfhv.dc.gov/page/vetsride: "Program eligibility is restricted to veteran residents of Washington, DC with annual income less than $45,000" "or recently unemployed." "Cost to riders: Free." "Book a ride or register: (202) 745-5555." Trip limits not on the page.
- **Veteran claims assistance:** MOVA District Veteran Service Officers "Assist DC veterans and their family members with their federal VA claims and benefits" — https://communityaffairs.dc.gov/page/veteran-claims-assistance.
- **Veterans Navigator Office (DC Superior Court):** Search results showed a dccourts.gov page ("Veterans Navigator Office," Moultrie Courthouse Room 120B, 202-879-1259) but the URL now returns "Page Not Found" after the courts' site redesign. I could not confirm that DC Superior Court operates a formal Veterans Treatment Court docket.
