# Alabama (AL) — State Benefits for Disabled Veterans

Researched 2026-10-16. Official sources only: Alabama Department of Veterans Affairs (ADVA, va.alabama.gov), Alabama Department of Revenue (ALDOR, revenue.alabama.gov), Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources (outdooralabama.com / alapark.com), State Personnel Department (personnel.alabama.gov), and enrolled bills on the Alabama Legislature site (alison.legislature.state.al.us).

Primary compendium used: ADVA, "Alabama Laws Affecting Veterans," May 2025 (PDF), https://va.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/AL-Laws-Affecting-Veterans-Act-2025-37-Draft.pdf. Note: the file name says "Draft"; it is hosted on ADVA's site and dated May 2025. Each benefit below also cites the implementing agency page where one exists.

## 1. Property tax — Homestead / principal residence exemption for permanently and totally disabled persons (Code of Ala. 40-9-19, 40-9-21, 40-9-21.1)

**What you get.** Full exemption from ALL state, county and municipal ad valorem (property) taxes on the principal residence and up to 160 acres, with no income limit and no assessed-value cap.

**Exact language.**
- ALDOR Homestead Exemptions page (no last-updated date shown): "A homestead is defined as a single-family owner-occupied dwelling and the land thereto, not exceeding 160 acres." Owner must occupy the home "as their primary residence on the first day of the tax year." H-3 (Disabled): "Taxpayer is permanently and totally disabled – exempt from all ad valorem taxes." Principal Residence Exemption table (40-9-21): "Permanently and totally disabled, any age — No maximum [assessed value] — [county school tax collected:] No — [income limit:] None." "Visit your local county office to apply for a homestead exemption."
  URL: https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/property-tax/homestead-exemptions/
- ALDOR Administrative Rule 810-4-1-.23 (effective Feb 7, 2014): "(7)(b) The principal residence of any taxpayer in this state who is permanently and totally disabled regardless of age or income is exempt from all property taxes levied by the state, any county or any municipality." Proof: "(7)(c) Proof of the taxpayer being totally and permanently disabled may include the receipt of a pension or annuity due to disability from a private company or a state or federal governmental agency or the written certification (Form PT-PA-1) ... from two physicians licensed to practice medicine in Alabama." Deadline: "(8) ... the owner of any homestead property as of the October 1 lien date must successfully apply for the exemption and submit all supporting documentation to the local tax assessing official in the county where their homestead property is located. Application may be made between October 1 and December 31 for the exemption to be applied for the current year or application may be made at any time during the year for the exemption to be applied for the following year." Annual verification: "(9) Any person who is permanently and totally disabled ... shall not be required to annually claim such exemptions after the initial qualification, but shall be allowed to verify such condition each year by mail on a form affidavit provided by the county assessing official." Penalty for false claims: "twice the amount of any ad valorem tax which would have been due retroactive for a period of up to 10 years plus interest at a rate of 15 percent per annum."
  URL: https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/810-4-1-.23.pdf (also https://admincode.legislature.state.al.us/api/rule/810-4-1-.23)
- ADVA Laws Affecting Veterans (May 2025), p.18: "Exemption is provided from ad valorem taxation of the home and an adjacent 160 acres of any person who is totally and permanently disabled or is 65 years of age or older. (ALA. CODE §§ 40-9-19 (a), 40-9-21)"
- 2026 enrolled HB77 ties the VA 100% P&T rating to this exemption: a tax assessing official issues a tentative certificate on "Documentation from the United States Department of Veterans Affairs ... indicating that the veteran has a 100 percent service-connected, permanent, and total disability" for a veteran "who may claim an exemption under Section 40-9-19 or 40-9-21 ... for permanent and total disability."

**Key points.** Alabama has NO rating-tiered (10%–90%) property tax break. The test is "permanently and totally disabled," not a VA percentage. Apply at the county Revenue Commissioner / Tax Assessor, Oct 1–Dec 31 for the current year. No surviving-spouse continuation is stated in the official sources for 40-9-21 (a spouse would have to qualify on his or her own age/disability). Whether TDIU alone satisfies "permanently and totally disabled" is NOT addressed in any official source found; counties decide what proof to accept (varies by county in practice). The VA 100% P&T letter is the documentation the 2026 act names.

## 2. Property tax — Homes acquired under the VA Specially Adapted Housing grant (Code of Ala. 40-9-20)

- ADVA Laws Affecting Veterans (May 2025), p.18: "The home of any veteran which is or was acquired by him/her pursuant to the provisions of Public Law 702, 80th Congress (specially adapted housing grant) as amended by (38 U.S.C. § 701 and Chapter 12), regardless of its value, shall be exempt, as long as the same is owned and occupied as a home by the veteran or his/her unremarried widow/er. (ALA. CODE § 40-9-20)"
- URL: https://va.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/AL-Laws-Affecting-Veterans-Act-2025-37-Draft.pdf
- Not keyed to a VA percentage; keyed to the SAH grant. Explicit unremarried surviving spouse continuation. Claimed at the county assessing office. I could not retrieve the current codified text of 40-9-20 from the Legislature's code database directly (only the ADVA summary).

## 3. 2026 law changes — HB155 (no annual re-verification for P&T veterans) and HB77 (tentative certificate for mortgage debt-to-income)

- **HB155 (2026 Regular Session), enrolled.** Amends 40-7-2.1 and 40-9-21.1. New 40-9-21.1(b)(2): "a veteran of the United States Armed Forces who has been determined to be permanently and totally disabled by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs and who qualifies for a homestead exemption under Section 40-9-19(d) or Section 40-9-21, shall not be required to annually claim the exemption nor verify eligibility for the exemption after the initial qualification. This release from the annual verification requirement shall terminate immediately upon the death of the qualifying, permanently and totally disabled veteran or upon the establishment of a new homestead." "Section 3. This act shall become effective on October 1, 2026." Passed House 10-Feb-26, Senate 07-Apr-26.
  URL: https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB155-enr.pdf
- **HB77 (2026 Regular Session), enrolled — "Disabled Veterans Property Tax Debt-To-Income Ratio Exemption Act."** "A tax assessing official shall issue a veteran who has received a 100 percent disability rating from the United States Department of Veterans Affairs and who may claim an exemption under Section 40-9-19 or 40-9-21 ... a tentative certificate of permanent and total disability prior to purchasing a homestead" on an affidavit, the purchase agreement, and VA documentation of "a 100 percent service-connected, permanent, and total disability." Certificate issued "within 20 days." "A settlement agent or loan closing officer may not consider ad valorem taxes for a homestead when calculating the debt-to-income ratio" of a borrower who provides the certificate. Effective October 1, 2026. Passed House 20-Jan-26, Senate 10-Mar-26.
  URL: https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB77-enr.pdf
- Caveat: I verified the enrolled texts and passage dates on the Legislature's site but could not pull the Act numbers / governor-signature record from an official page (search budget exhausted). Flagged needs_verification.

## 4. Vehicle — Registration fee / license tax exemption for service-connected disabled veterans (10% and up) and the Disabled Veteran plate

- ADVA Laws Affecting Veterans (May 2025), p.6: "Service-connected disabled veterans who are rated at 10% or higher and are residents of Alabama at the time the exemption is claimed shall be exempt from the payment of the privilege or license tax and registration and issuance fees now and hereinafter to be levied on automobiles and motor vehicles by the State of Alabama. (Note that local taxes and fees may still be charged.) Veterans rated as less than 50% service-connected disabled are limited to one exemption and one license plate. ... (ALA. CODE §§ 40-12-244, 40-12-254, 40-12-271.1)" 60%+/special-controls plate: "The registration fee for these tags is $5.00, unless otherwise exempted by law ... All veterans being issued one of these plates shall be exempt from the state issuance fee. Disabled Veterans plates are not transferable. (ALA. CODE §§ 32-6-130 ...)"
- ALDOR Disabled Veteran plate page (no date shown): Eligibility "A resident of Alabama who is a service-connected disabled veteran." "Disability rating of 10% to 50%: The first plate is exempt from registration fees and license plate issuance fees. The registrant is NOT exempt from ad valorem taxes. Registrant must pay all applicable registration fees and license plate issuance fees for subsequent license plates." "Disability rating of 51% and higher or vehicle has special mechanical control devices: The first plate is exempt from the registration fees and license plate issuance fees. The registrant is NOT exempt from ad valorem taxes. Registrant must pay a $5 annual fee for subsequent license plates." "Veterans qualifying for the $5 plate (disability rating 51% or higher) may display the plate on any gross vehicle weight pleasure motor vehicle." "Disabled veterans are not required to obtain the Disabled Veteran distinctive license plate in order to claim the registration fee(s) exemption/reduction." Documentation: "a U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs disability rating certification" plus military ID, DD214, or ADVA affidavit. "The surviving spouse of a veteran fitting these criteria may not obtain nor retain the plate." Parking: "The Application for Disability Access Parking Credentials must be provided to obtain the disability access license plate decal."
  URL: https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/license-plates/disabled-veteran/ (branch versions e.g. https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/license-plates/disabled-veteran-us-army/)
- ALDOR Rule 810-5-1-.201 (amended eff. May 24, 2018): "(1) Anyone claiming a partial or full registration fee exemption ... must claim the exemption at the time of registration or re-registration of the motor vehicle. (2) Anyone failing to claim ... will not be entitled to a refund of registration fees subsequently claimed."
  URL: https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/810-5-1-.201.pdf
- Trap: the ADVA guide labels the $5 plate tier "60% or Higher" while ALDOR's page says "51% and higher"; both derive from 32-6-130 ("exceeds 50%"). Ad valorem (property) tax on the vehicle is still owed unless the vehicle was VA-grant funded (item 5). No official source found for a 100%-rating vehicle ad valorem exemption.

## 5. Vehicle — Vehicle paid for in whole or part by a VA grant: no license fees and no ad valorem tax (Code of Ala. 40-12-254)

- ADVA Laws Affecting Veterans (May 2025), p.19: "Any disabled veteran ... who owns an automobile which has been, is or shall hereafter be all or partly paid for with funds furnished for such purposes by the USDVA ... is entitled to exemptions from all license fees and ad valorem taxes on such motor vehicle provided the veteran keeps the vehicle only for private use. (ALA. CODE § 40-12-254)" Also p.6: "If the veteran qualifies due to USDVA funding for their vehicle, he/she is limited to one (1) license plate which shall be exempt from license fees and ad valorem taxes."
- ALDOR plate page: "Vehicle all or partly paid for by U.S. Department of Veteran Affairs: All license plates are exempt from registration fees and ad valorem taxes (Section 40-12-254)."
- URLs as in item 4.

## 6. Vehicle — Purple Heart (and Medal of Honor, POW, Legion of Valor) plates; Honored Veteran Parking Permit

- ALDOR Purple Heart page (no date shown): "The registrant is exempt from registration fees (but not ad valorem taxes) for the first plate. All applicable registration fees and ad valorem taxes must be remitted for any additional plates obtained." "The surviving spouse of a veteran fitting this criteria may retain the plate." Required: "DD214; Certification from the Department of Veterans Affairs as provided by §32-6-350(d) ... Affidavit from Veteran Affairs." Statutes "32-6-250, 32-6-250.1, and 32-6-231.1."
  URL: https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/license-plates/purple-heart/
- ADVA guide p.8–9: "Medal of Honor - The registrant is exempt from registration fees and ad valorem taxes for the first plate." "Prisoner of War - The registrant is exempt from registration fees and ad valorem taxes for the first plate." These plate holders "may keep the tag for life and do not need to re-register each year. Upon the veteran's death, the surviving spouse shall be entitled to retain the tag, at no fee or tax. Exception: the surviving spouse of Purple Heart Medal recipients shall pay ad valorem taxes." "Honored Veteran Parking Permit - Those who would otherwise qualify for one or more of the above listed license plates may be issued an Honored Veteran Parking Permit placard ... To apply for a placard, please visit your ADVA county veterans service office."

## 7. Recreation — Disabled Military Veteran's Appreciation hunting licenses (50% and 100%) and lifetime hunting license (40%)

- DCNR license form "100% DISABLED MILITARY VETERAN'S APPRECIATION HUNTING LICENSE, Valid: Sept 1 – Aug 31, Resident" (Revised 09/22): "Applicant must show certification from Veteran's Administration showing a disability of 100% or more." "COST: $3.30." "Allows qualifying resident disabled military veteran to hunt all legal game in state during open season." Purchase "at your local Probate Office or License Commissioner," the Montgomery office, or by mail. Residency: "You are considered a Non-Resident IF you have an Alabama Driver's License or Non-Driver's ID and you possess a valid driver's license from another state."
  URL: https://www.outdooralabama.com/sites/default/files/Licenses/Recreational%20-%20Current/100Percent_Vet_Hunt.pdf
- DCNR form "50% DISABLED MILITARY VETERAN'S APPRECIATION HUNTING LICENSE" (Revised 09/22): "disability of 50% or more." "COST: $15.65."
  URL: https://www.outdooralabama.com/sites/default/files/Licenses/Recreational%20-%20Current/50Percent_Vet_Hunt.pdf
- DCNR License FAQs (© 2026, no date): "Residents can obtain the Annual Military Veteran's Appreciation Hunting License to veterans with a minimum of 50% disability or those resident veterans who are 100% disabled as certified by the Administration. ... Must show VA letter stating level of disability."
  URL: https://www.outdooralabama.com/licenses/license-faqs
- ADVA guide p.17: "A special hunting license is available to 100% service-connected veterans at a cost of $2 plus the applicable issuance fee ... to 50% service-connected veterans at half of the usual cost plus the applicable issuance fee. Such veterans may also purchase an annual resident nighttime feral swine and coyote hunting license by paying the issuance fee ... (ALA. CODE §§ 9-11-44, 9-11-235)". Lifetime: "Veterans who are Alabama residents and have a disability rating ... of 40% or more may purchase a lifetime disabled military veterans appreciation hunting license ... The cost of the lifetime license varies based upon whether the veteran is at least 50 years old and whether he or she is rated 100% service-connected totally and permanently disabled. (ALA. CODE §§ 9-11-65.3)"
- Note: prices on the 2022-revised forms ($3.30 / $15.65) may have been adjusted; I could not load the current lifetime-license price packet. Nonresident disabled veterans get no discount (DCNR FAQ: residency "is determined by the state of the driver's license").

## 9. Recreation — Free state park admission for Alabama resident veterans (Code of Ala. 9-14-8); 15% lodging discount

- ADVA guide p.22: "Active duty and honorably discharged veterans who are residents of Alabama are granted free admission to state parks on days where the park is open to the general public. Residents may prove qualifying service by presenting their active or retired military identification card, an Alabama driver's license with a veteran designation, or their DD Form 214. ... Active and retired military personnel who are not residents of Alabama are granted free admission to state parks on state and federal holidays ... (ALA. CODE § 9-14-8)"
- Alabama State Parks "Parks for Patriots" (© 2026): "Alabama State Parks provides Veterans and active-duty military personnel free admission to parks that charge entrance or day-use area fees (#ParksforPatriots). Veterans and active-duty military personnel should ask for complimentary admission at gated parks or day-use facilities. You may be asked to show ID or documentation."
  URL: https://www.alapark.com/ParksForPatriots
- Gulf State Park / Oak Mountain discount pages: "15% daily discount on overnight accommodations managed and operated by the State Park System" for "Veterans and active military personnel" and "People with disabilities as defined by the ADA"; "Identification is required at check-in for all reservations"; "not available in conjunction with other group rates, employee pricing, special packages, weekly or monthly rates, or discounts." Oak Mountain: "All active military, retired military and veterans are FREE" (entry).
  URLs: https://www.alapark.com/parks/gulf-state-park/discounts ; https://www.alapark.com/parks/oak-mountain-state-park/rates-and-discounts
- Not disability-based; any honorably discharged resident veteran qualifies. Some park pages limit free entry to the veteran only (not dependents).

## 10. Education — Alabama G.I. Dependents' Scholarship Program (Code of Ala. 31-6-1 to 31-6-17) — for children, stepchildren, spouses, unremarried widow(er)s

- ADVA guide pp.10–12 and ADVA pamphlet (Nov 2024). Disability: "The veteran must be rated 40% or more by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs due to service-connected disabilities or have held the qualifying rating at the time of death, be a current or former prisoner of war (POW), be declared missing in action (MIA), have died as the result of a service-connected disability, or have died while on active military service in the line of duty." Grandfather note: "Veterans whose disability rating was less than 20% prior to May 23, 2017 but who had a claim for compensation pending prior to that date which resulted in a final award ... of at least 20% may be eligible if the dependent applies for benefits within six months of that final adjudication."
- Service: "honorably discharged with at least 90 days of continuous active federal military service" (or less if discharged for service-connected disability).
- Veteran residency: "permanent civilian resident of the State of Alabama for at least one year immediately prior to ... initial entry into active military service" AND one of: "Current resident for at least two years immediately prior to the date of this application or date of veteran's death; or ... discharged within the last 12 months; or ... Filed a resident Alabama income tax return for the past 10 consecutive years." OR "Permanently service-connected veterans rated at 100% who did not enter service from Alabama may qualify after establishing at least five years of permanent residency in Alabama immediately prior to filing of an application." Pamphlet: "This applies to 100% permanent and total ratings only."
- Student: "Must be a current resident of the State of Alabama at the time of application"; FAFSA each year; SAP; FERPA release; child/stepchild "must initiate training under our program prior to his/her 26th birthday. In certain situations ... up to the age of 30."
- Entitlement: children/stepchildren "five standard academic years (10 semesters)"; spouses/widow(er)s of a veteran "rated as 100% permanently and totally disabled" 10 semesters; spouses/widow(er)s of a veteran "rated 40-90% disabled" "three standard academic years (6 semesters)." Pamphlet: "Students have 8 years from the date the application is received ... to use their 10 semesters" (6 years for the 6-semester spouse benefit). "Alabama G.I. Dependent Scholarship Program benefits cannot be granted retroactively."
- Caps: "Tuition is limited to a maximum of four hundred dollars ($400) per semester hour and required textbooks and applicable fees will be limited to a combined maximum one thousand dollars ($1,000) total per student for each semester. ... All scholarships and grants paid directly to the school must be applied to required education expenses first." Private colleges qualify for enrollments "JUNE 1, 2022 OR AFTER."
- Termination: divorce ends a former spouse/stepchild's eligibility "effective the date of the divorce"; "The widow(er) and any associated stepchildren forfeit all entitlements upon remarriage."
- Apply through the ADVA portal: https://va.alabama.gov/dependents-scholarship/
- URLs: https://va.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/AL-Laws-Affecting-Veterans-Act-2025-37-Draft.pdf ; https://va.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/GI-Bill-Pamphlet-2024-November-2024.pdf (dated November 2024); qualifying schools list (January 2026): https://va.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Qualifying-schools-for-the-Alabama-G.I.-Dependent-Scholarship-Program.pdf
- Note: This is a dependents' benefit; there is no state tuition waiver for the disabled veteran personally (other than the Purple Heart waiver below).

## 11. Education — Purple Heart tuition waiver (Code of Ala. 16-1-43)

- ADVA guide p.13: "Public institutions of higher learning in the State of Alabama ... may waive tuition and fees for recipients of the Purple Heart Medal for undergraduate studies. Contact the public institution that the recipient plans to attend." Discretionary ("may"); not rating-based.

## 12. Income tax — Military retirement pay and VA benefits fully exempt

- ALDOR "Income Exempt from Alabama Income Taxation" (no date shown): exempt list includes "Military retirement pay" and "Disability retirement payments (and other benefits) paid by the Veterans Administration" and combat-zone compensation.
  URL: https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/individual-corporate/income-exempt-from-alabama-income-taxation/
- ADVA guide p.19: "Compensation received as retirement benefits by any person retired from the military service of the United States of America and survivor benefits derived there from are exempt from any state, county or municipal income tax or similar tax. (ALA. CODE § 40-18-20)"
- Alabama DOES have a state income tax; these items are simply excluded. Active-duty base pay of residents is taxable (only allowances and combat pay are exempt).

## 13. Employment — State classified service preference points (Code of Ala. 36-26-15(b))

- ADVA guide p.15: "All persons who have been honorably discharged ... shall have 5 points added to any earned ratings in examination for entrance to the state classified service. Honorably discharged veterans with the present existence of a service-connected disability and entitled to pension, compensation or disability allowance under existing laws shall have 10 points added to any earned ratings. Wives and widows of certain disabled or deceased veterans may have the 10-point preference extended to them."
- State Personnel Department FAQ (© 2026): Veteran 5 points — DD214, "Must be active duty for other than training purposes." Disabled Veteran 10 points — DD214 plus "V.A. letter of disability dated with the last 6 months"; "Letter must be kept updated until register is established or you lose the extra 5 points." Deceased veteran's spouse 10 points (not if remarried); disabled veteran's spouse 10 points only while married to a veteran who "because of this disability is not qualified." Job announcements: "Veteran's credits are NOT allowed on promotional examinations."
  URL: https://www.personnel.alabama.gov/Faq
- Any compensable service-connected rating qualifies for 10 points (no percentage floor stated).

## 14. Other — Business and occupational license tax exemption for disabled veterans (Code of Ala. 40-12-340 to 40-12-352)

- ADVA guide p.16: "Any bona fide permanent resident of Alabama who is an honorably discharged veteran ... when the United States was, is or shall be engaged in hostilities with any foreign state and who has physical disabilities of 25% or more, whether service-connected or not, may be exempt from business or occupational license taxes. A veteran who conducts his/her business as a means of livelihood through his/her personal efforts and has no more than one employee shall be entitled to licenses ... upon the payment of license taxes that shall not exceed $25.00. However, a veteran whose property (both real and personal) is valued at $5,000.00 or more or whose net income is $2,500.00 or more is not entitled to these business and occupational license exemptions."
- Practically very narrow because of the $5,000 property / $2,500 income caps. Claimed through the county probate judge / license commissioner (local license issuer); statutory text not independently retrieved.
