# California (CA) — Disabled Veteran State Benefits Research

Researched 2026-10-16. Sources limited to California state agencies (CalVet, BOE, FTB, DMV, CDFW, State Parks, CalHR/CalCareers) and the official statute site (leginfo.legislature.ca.gov). State veterans agency: California Department of Veterans Affairs (CalVet), https://www.calvet.ca.gov.

Method note: calvet.ca.gov and leginfo.legislature.ca.gov block automated fetchers (captcha / Cloudflare), so those pages were read in a real browser session. BOE and FTB PDFs were downloaded and text-extracted. The web-search budget for this session ran out partway through; everything below that is marked verified was read directly on the official page.

## 1. Disabled Veterans' Property Tax Exemption (Rev. & Tax. Code 205.5)

**Statute (verified):** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=RTC&sectionNum=205.5 — "(Amended by Stats. 2025, Ch. 549, Sec. 3. (SB 663) Effective October 10, 2025.)"

Exact threshold language:

> "(a) Property that constitutes the principal place of residence of a veteran, that is owned by the veteran, the veteran's spouse, or the veteran and the veteran's spouse jointly, is exempted from taxation on that part of the full value of the residence that does not exceed one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000), as adjusted for the relevant assessment year ... if the veteran is blind in both eyes, has lost the use of two or more limbs, or if the veteran is totally disabled as a result of injury or disease incurred in military service. The one-hundred-thousand-dollar ($100,000) exemption shall be one hundred fifty thousand dollars ($150,000), as adjusted ... in the case of an eligible veteran whose household income does not exceed the amount of forty thousand dollars ($40,000), as adjusted ..."

> "(e) ... being totally disabled means that the United States Department of Veterans Affairs or the military service from which the veteran was discharged has rated the disability at 100 percent or has rated the disability compensation at 100 percent by reason of being unable to secure or follow a substantially gainful occupation."

So: **100% schedular qualifies; TDIU (compensated at the 100% rate for unemployability) qualifies; P&T status is not required by the statute.** Blindness in both eyes or loss of use of two or more limbs also qualify regardless of rating.

Veteran definition trap, 205.5(b)(1)(A): the veteran must have "served either in time of war or in time of peace in a campaign or expedition for which a medal has been issued by Congress, or in time of peace and because of a service-connected disability was released from active duty, and who has been determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs to be eligible for federal veterans' health and medical benefits." Discharge must be "under other than dishonorable conditions."

Surviving spouse, 205.5(c)(1): "the unmarried surviving spouse of a deceased veteran" qualifies if "(A) The deceased veteran during their lifetime qualified for the exemption ... (B) The veteran died from a disease that was service connected as determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs." Also (c)(2) covers the unmarried surviving spouse of a veteran who "died while on active duty in military service" from a service-connected injury or disease. Remarriage ends eligibility (must be "unmarried").

Principal residence: confinement to a hospital/care facility does not break residency if the home is not rented to a third party (b)(2)(A); a home destroyed in a Governor-declared disaster keeps the exemption if the veteran intends to rebuild and reoccupy (b)(2)(B). Assessor must accept an electronically generated VA letter of service-connected disability (f).

**Current dollar amounts (verified, BOE Letter To Assessors No. 2026/019, dated May 22, 2026):** https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta26019.pdf

> "Applying this factor to the 2026 exemption amounts of $180,671 and $271,009 results in 2027 exemption amounts of $185,889 for the basic exemption and $278,836 for the low-income exemption. Applying this factor to the 2026 household income limit of $81,131 results in a 2027 household income limit of $83,474 for those claiming the low-income exemption."

Table from the same letter: Lien date 2026 — basic $180,671, low-income $271,009, income limit $81,131 (factor 1.03065). Lien date 2027 — basic $185,889, low-income $278,836, income limit $83,474 (factor 1.02888). The forms "BOE-261-G and BOE-261-GNT ... will be available to Assessors for the January 1, 2027, lien date."

**Filing rules (verified, BOE "Disabled Veterans' Exemption" page):** https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/dv_exemption.htm (no last-updated date shown; accessibility certification dated June 2025)

- "An unmarried surviving spouse of a qualified veteran may also claim the exemption."
- "the property must be used as the claimant's principal place of residence."
- Low-income claims: "The claim form must be filed annually between January 1 and on or before February 15." Claims filed after February 15 through December 10 receive 90 percent of the exemption for that year; after December 10, 85 percent.
- Form BOE-261-G, "Claim for Disabled Veterans' Property Tax Exemption," filed with the county assessor. Basic exemption is a one-time filing; low-income exemption must be re-filed every year.

**CalVet page (verified):** https://www.calvet.ca.gov/VetServices/Pages/Property-Tax-Exemptions.aspx (no date shown)

> "In order to receive 100 percent of the basic exemption in the first year claimed, the claim form must be filed between the date the property or veteran qualifies and on or before the following January 1, or 90 days after the date of qualification, whichever is later. A one-time filing is only required for the $100,000 basic exemption; however, annual filing is required for the $150,000 low-income exemption ... (claim form must be filed between January 1 and February 15)."
> "The veteran must file the form with the county assessor of the county in which the property is located."

Same CalVet page also describes the separate, much smaller **Veterans' Exemption** (RTC 205): "exemption of property not to exceed $4,000" for veterans who own property worth no more than $5,000 (single) / $10,000 (married). RTC 205.5(g) says the disabled veterans' exemption "shall be in lieu of the veteran's exemption" — you cannot stack them.

County variation: the amounts and rules are statewide, but the claim is filed with and processed by each county assessor, so forms, processing times, and "preliminary eligibility" letters (205.5(k)) vary by county.

## 2. State income tax: military retirement pay and VA compensation

California has a personal income tax. Military retirement pay is taxable to residents, with a new limited exclusion starting tax year 2025.

**Statute (verified):** RTC 17132.9, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=RTC&sectionNum=17132.9 — "(Added by Stats. 2025, Ch. 17, Sec. 12. (SB 132) Effective June 27, 2025. Repealed as of December 1, 2030, by its own provisions.)"

> "(a) For taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2025, and before January 1, 2030, gross income shall not include retirement pay received by a qualified taxpayer during the taxable year, not to exceed twenty thousand dollars ($20,000), from the federal government for service in the uniformed services."
> "(1) 'Qualified taxpayer' means a taxpayer that satisfies either of the following: (A) In the case of a surviving spouse or spouses filing a joint return, adjusted gross income, as required to be shown on the federal tax return for the same taxable year, does not exceed two hundred fifty thousand dollars ($250,000). (B) In the case of any other individual, adjusted gross income ... does not exceed one hundred twenty-five thousand dollars ($125,000)."

The enacting bill was **SB 132 (2025 budget trailer bill)**, not AB 53 or SB 1 (those were the stand-alone bills; the budget trailer bill carried the final language).

RTC 17132.10 (verified, same site, sectionNum=17132.10) gives a parallel exclusion of up to $20,000 for "annuity payments ... pursuant to a United States Department of Defense Survivor Benefit Plan," same AGI limits, same 2025–2029 window.

**FTB page (verified, "Last updated: 06/11/2026"):** https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/personal/filing-situations/military.html

> "If you're a resident, your military retirement pay is taxable. This includes all military pension income, regardless of where you were stationed or domiciled while on active duty."
> "For taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2025, and before January 1, 2030, California allows qualified taxpayers to exclude from gross income up to $20,000 of retirement pay from the federal government for service in the uniformed services or annuity payments received pursuant to a U.S. Defense Department Survivor Benefit Plan."
> "VA disability benefits and 'lump-sum' retroactive payments are: Tax-exempt; Not included in income; Not reported on IRS Form 1099-R."
> "CRDP pay is: Included in income; Even if there is a VA disability determination." / "CRSC payments are tax-exempt and not included in gross income."

FTB Pub. 1032 (2025), https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-1032-publication.pdf: "not to exceed $20,000 for each type of payments. The qualified taxpayer's federal adjusted gross income (AGI) must not exceed $125,000 (or $250,000 for a surviving spouse or spouses filing a joint return)." Claimed on Schedule CA (540) / (540NR).

2026 pending law: FTB posted analyses of **SB 1407 (2026)**, which would raise the exclusion (versions at $80,000 and $40,000) — https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/law/legislation/2025-2026/SB1407-040926.pdf. I could not confirm from an official source whether it was enacted; as of the June 2026 FTB page the law is still the $20,000 exclusion.

## 3. Disabled Veteran (DV) license plates and registration fee exemption

**Definition (verified):** Vehicle Code 295.7, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&sectionNum=295.7

> "A 'disabled veteran' is any person who, as a result of injury or disease suffered while on active service with the armed forces of the United States, suffers any of the following: (a) Has a disability which has been rated at 100 percent by the Department of Veterans Affairs or the military service from which the veteran was discharged, due to a diagnosed disease or disorder which substantially impairs or interferes with mobility. (b) Is so severely disabled as to be unable to move without the aid of an assistant device. (c) Has lost, or has lost use of, one or more limbs. (d) Has suffered permanent blindness, as defined in Section 19153 of the Welfare and Institutions Code."

Key trap: a 100% rating alone is **not** enough — the 100% rating must be for a condition that "substantially impairs or interferes with mobility." TDIU is not mentioned in 295.7.

**Plates (verified):** Vehicle Code 5007, same site, sectionNum=5007: "The department shall, upon application and without additional fees, issue a special license plate or plates ... to ... (2) A disabled veteran." Subdivision (c)(3): "For a disabled veteran, the department shall accept, in lieu of the [medical] certificate ... a certificate from a county veterans service officer, the Department of Veterans Affairs, or the United States Department of Veterans Affairs that certifies that the applicant is a disabled veteran as described in Section 295.7." Plates carry the wheelchair symbol and must be returned within 60 days of the veteran's death.

**Fee exemption (verified, DMV Vehicle Industry Registration Procedures Manual sec. 21.095 "Disabled Veteran License Plates and Fees Exemption (VC §§5007 and 9105)"):** https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/vehicle-industry-registration-procedures-manual-2/special-plates/disabled-veteran-license-plates/ (no revision date shown)

- "A qualified disabled veteran is exempt from the payment of all fees" except duplicate certificates, plates, and stickers.
- "The fee exemption in VC §9105 can be used for one vehicle only." Vehicle must be a passenger vehicle, motorcycle, or commercial vehicle of 8,000 lbs unladen or less, not used for hire.
- DV plates expire 12/31 and are renewed annually at no fee. Plate owner must be driver or passenger to use disabled-person parking privileges; a disabled veteran who chooses other plates must display a DP placard. Must carry the REG 3060 certification card.

**Form (verified):** REG 256A (Rev. 1/2021), https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/uploads/2020/12/reg256a.pdf — "I understand that disabled veteran exempt registration is valid for one vehicle only." Lists the four 295.7 conditions as checkboxes; accepts certification by a medical professional, a County Veterans Service Officer, CalVet, or USDVA; mail to DMV Special Processing Unit, MS D238, P.O. Box 932345, Sacramento, CA 94232-0001.

Note: I could not open the Vehicle Code 9105 text itself on leginfo (the site returned a "multiple results" picker for 9105 because of a 2022 renumbering); the DMV manual quotes and applies it.

## 4. Hunting and fishing licenses (CDFW)

**Reduced-fee hunting license (verified):** https://wildlife.ca.gov/Licensing/Hunting/Reduced-Fee (no date shown)

> "any honorably discharged veteran of the US Armed Forces with a service–connected disability rating of 50% or greater." Price shown: Disabled Veteran Hunting License $9.79. Prequalify by submitting a VA letter (Benefit Summary Letter) showing honorable discharge and 50%+ rating plus a copy of driver license and GO ID — upload to the online customer record, mail to a CDFW license sales office, or fax (916) 419-7585; "DO NOT" email. After CDFW updates the record, buy anywhere licenses are sold.

**Reduced-fee sport fishing license (verified):** https://wildlife.ca.gov/Licensing/Fishing/Reduced-Fee (fee table labeled 2026 fees)

> "honorably discharged disabled veteran with a 50 percent or greater service-connected disability." Price: $10.04 at CDFW offices, $10.54 from license agents. Residents and nonresidents both qualify. Valid 365 days from purchase.

There is **no free** hunting or fishing license for disabled veterans in California — only the reduced fee. The reduced fee covers the base license only; tags, validations, and report cards are extra.

## 5. California State Parks Distinguished Veteran Pass

**Verified:** https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=30958 (page shows "© Copyright 2026"; application form DPR 619 Rev. 4/2026, https://www.parks.ca.gov/pages/1012/files/DPR619.pdf)

> "Honorably discharged veterans who have California residency, and one of the following three criteria/criterion": "An overall/combined service-connected disability at a rating of 50% or greater with service during periods of wartime or operations against a foreign power"; "Held as prisoners of war by forces hostile to the United States."; "Recipient of the Medal of Honor."

DPR 619: "California residents that are qualified 1) disabled veterans of wars or operations against foreign powers, 2) former prisoners of war, or 3) recipients of the Medal of Honor are eligible to receive a Distinguished Veteran Pass, which entitles the holder to the use of all basic State Park System operated facilities, at no further charge. No fee is required to obtain this pass." Requires "A current letter from the United States Department of Veterans Affairs dated within one year of application stating the veteran has an overall/combined rating at 50% or greater", proof of wartime/foreign-operations service (DD-214), and a CA driver license/ID showing a current California address. "This is a lifetime pass ... the pass holder's passcard will need to be renewed every five years." Covers vehicle day use (one vehicle, 9 persons or less), family camping (the site occupied by the pass holder), and boat use for one vessel; not valid for group sites, special events, supplemental fees, or concessionaire/local/federal parks. Apply online through ReserveCalifornia (fastest) or by mail (allow 8–10 weeks).

Trap: the 50% path requires **wartime or foreign-operations service** — a peacetime-only veteran at 50%+ does not qualify.

## 6. Education

### 6a. College Fee Waiver for Veteran Dependents (CalVet) — verified
https://www.calvet.ca.gov/VetServices/Pages/College-Fee-Waiver.aspx (no date shown; states AY 2026-27 figure)

> "waives mandatory system-wide tuition and fees at any State of California Community College, California State University, or University of California campus. This program does not cover the expense of books, parking or room and board."

**Plan A:** "The unmarried child of a Veteran who is totally service-connected disabled or whose death was officially rated as service-connected is eligible. The child must be over 14 years old and under 27 years old to be eligible. If the child is a Veteran, then the age limit is extended to age 30"; "The spouse of a wartime Veteran who is totally service-connected disabled is eligible. There are no age limit restrictions"; "The unmarried surviving spouse of a wartime Veteran whose death has been rated as service-connected is eligible." "The Veteran must have served at least one day of active duty during a period of war as declared by the U.S. Congress, or during any time in which the Veteran was awarded a campaign or expeditionary medal. Concurrent receipt of benefits under Plan A and VA Chapter 35 benefits is prohibited." "There are no income restrictions under this plan." The qualifying event "must have occurred prior to the child's 21st birthday."

**Plan B:** "The child of a Veteran who has a service-connected disability, or had a service-connected disability at the time of death, or died of service-related causes is eligible. The child's annual income, which includes the child's adjusted gross income, plus the value of support provided by a parent, may not exceed the annual income limit." "Under Plan B, wartime service is not required and there are no specific age requirements. Children are the only dependents eligible under this plan. There is no prohibition against receiving concurrent VA Chapter 35 benefits." "The new income limit for eligible students under Plan B for academic year (AY) 2026-2027 is $22,941."

Plan C: dependents of California National Guard members killed/disabled in state active service. Plan D: Medal of Honor recipients and their children under 27 (undergraduate only).

Notes: "All applicants must meet California residency requirements as per the schools requirements." Self-supported/extension programs are not covered. Not retroactive, except when an application was denied only because the VA claim was still pending and the VA later grants service connection (Mil. & Vet. Code 890.3(a)(1)) — you must have applied every academic year. Apply with form DVS-40 at a County Veterans Service Office; re-apply each academic year.

### 6b. Nonresident tuition exemption for veterans and GI Bill users — verified
Education Code 68075.7, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EDC&sectionNum=68075.7 (amended Stats. 2022, Ch. 465):

> "a student enrolled at a campus of the California Community Colleges or the California State University who meets all of the following requirements shall be exempt from paying nonresident tuition ... (1) The student resides in California. (2) The student meets the definition of 'covered individual,' as that term is defined in Section 3679(c)(2) of Title 38 ... (3) The student is eligible for education benefits under the federal Montgomery GI Bill–Active Duty program ..., the Veterans Readiness and Employment program ..., the Post-9/11 GI Bill program ..., or the Survivors' and Dependents' Educational Assistance program."

Education Code 68075.5 (same site): a veteran "stationed in this state on active duty for more than one year immediately prior to being discharged" is exempt from nonresident tuition at CCC/CSU for up to one year while establishing residency (use within two years of discharge); dishonorable/bad-conduct discharges excluded. The statute names only CCC and CSU; UC is "requested" to conform.

### 6c. Cal Grant
Cal Grant is a general need-based state aid program with no veteran-specific eligibility; I found no official veteran-specific Cal Grant rule and did not include it as a benefit.

## 7. State employment — Veterans' Preference (top rank, not points)

**Statute (verified):** Government Code 18973.1, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=18973.1 — "(Repealed and added by Stats. 2013, Ch. 75, Sec. 7. (AB 372) Effective January 1, 2014.)"

> "Whenever any veteran, widow or widower of a veteran, or spouse of a 100 percent disabled veteran achieves a passing score on an entrance examination, he or she shall be ranked in the top rank of the resulting eligibility list. This section shall not apply to any veteran who has been dishonorably discharged or released."

Gov. Code 18973.5 (verified): "an entrance examination is any open competitive examination" and "No veterans' preference under this article shall be awarded to permanent civil service employees." Gov. Code 18978 (the old 10-point / 15-point section) now displays as empty on leginfo — the point system was replaced in 2014. The "10/15 points" hint is outdated.

**CalHR form 1093 (verified, Rev 07/2025):** https://www.calhr.ca.gov/Documents/calhr-1093.pdf — "all individuals who achieve a passing score on an entrance examination shall be certified in rank 1 of the eligibility list, regardless of score." Requirements: "(1) Have never achieved permanent civil service status; AND (2) Have served in the U.S. Armed Forces ...; AND (3) Qualify as veterans" (38 U.S.C. 101(2), other than dishonorable). "'100 percent disabled veteran' means any veteran ... who is currently declared by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs to be 100 percent disabled." Spouse of a 100% disabled veteran must attach the DD-214, marriage certificate, and "US Dept VA Disability Award Letter substantiating 100% disablement." Mail to CalHR, ATTN: Selection Division, 1515 S Street, North Building, Suite 500, Sacramento, CA 95811-7258. "Individuals who received VP are not eligible for Career Credits." Active-duty members within six months of separation may apply with CalHR 1094.

CalCareers Veterans' Information page (verified): https://www.calcareers.ca.gov/CalHRPublic/Landing/Jobs/VeteransInformation.aspx — applies "only to open exams"; passing score "70 or above"; "Veterans' Preference does not guarantee you will be selected for an interview or for a position."

Note: there is no extra preference for a disabled veteran over any other veteran — all qualifying veterans go to rank 1. The disability rating only matters for the **spouse** of a 100% disabled veteran.

## 8. Other California-specific benefits

### 8a. CalVet Home Loans — verified
https://www.calvet.ca.gov/HomeLoans/Pages/New-Customer.aspx (no date shown)
> "Any veteran can apply for a CalVet Home Loan when purchasing an owner-occupied home in California." "To be eligible, you must: Have served on active duty a minimum of 90 days during wartime or peacetime (excluding training). Have a discharge under honorable conditions. Not have a current CalVet Home Loan." "We have no prior residency rules, meaning you do not have to be a 'California veteran' to qualify." "Most of our loan products have no down payment requirements." No disability rating required; reusable.

### 8b. Veterans Homes of California — verified
https://www.calvet.ca.gov/VetHomes/Pages/apply.aspx (no date shown)
> "To be eligible for admission, you must be: 55 years or older, homeless, or disabled. A veteran, having served on active duty. A California resident. Discharged or released from active duty under conditions other than dishonorable. Covered under a medical insurance policy." Priority: "Medal of Honor recipients. Former POWs. Veterans experiencing homelessness. Veterans with 70% or more service-connected disability ratings." Eight homes (Barstow, Chula Vista, Fresno, Lancaster, Redding, Ventura, West LA, Yountville); levels from independent living to skilled nursing and memory care. Spouses/domestic partners may apply jointly (married/cohabiting 1+ year).

### 8c. Business license, tax and fee waiver for veteran vendors — verified, varies by locality
CalVet: https://www.calvet.ca.gov/VetServices/Pages/Business-License-Tax-and-Fee-Waiver.aspx — "waives municipal, county, and state business license fees, taxes, and fees for veterans who hawk, peddle, or vend any goods, wares, or merchandise owned by the veteran, except ... intoxicating liquor." "Honorably discharged veterans who engage in sales (not services) activities may be eligible. Eligibility criteria will differ based upon local jurisdiction." Statute: Bus. & Prof. Code 16102, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=16102 ("without payment of any license, tax or fee whatsoever, whether municipal, county or State, and the board of supervisors shall issue ... without cost, a license therefor"). Apply to the city/county licensing authority with proof of honorable discharge.

### 8d. Veterans' Exemption ($4,000) — RTC 205
Covered on the CalVet property tax page. Only for veterans whose total property is worth $5,000 or less ($10,000 married) — rarely usable, and cannot be combined with the Disabled Veterans' Exemption (RTC 205.5(g)).
