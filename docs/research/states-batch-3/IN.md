# Indiana (IN) — State Benefits for Disabled Veterans

Research date: 2026-10-17. Official agency: Indiana Department of Veterans Affairs (IDVA), https://www.in.gov/dva/.

Sourcing note: Everything below comes from in.gov sites: IDVA (in.gov/dva), the Department of Local Government Finance (DLGF, in.gov/dlgf, including its June 17, 2026 Veterans Deductions FAQ posted Aug. 12, 2026 and State Form 12662 R20 6-26), the Bureau of Motor Vehicles (in.gov/bmv, State Form 52870), the Department of Natural Resources (in.gov/dnr), the Commission for Higher Education (in.gov/che), the Department of Revenue (in.gov/dor, Information Bulletins #6 and #27, June 2025), the Indiana Veterans' Home (in.gov/ivh), and events.in.gov. The Indiana General Assembly site (iga.in.gov) is a JavaScript app and returned no statute text to any fetch, so statutory language is quoted from the DLGF FAQ and the DLGF-prescribed State Form 12662, which reproduce the statutory tests. No in.gov page shows a "last updated" date; all show only "Copyright 2026 State of Indiana." Several older in.gov URLs returned 404 or redirected (noted below). The web search budget ran out before I could find the state employment preference statute or a 2025 DLGF legislative memo.

## 1. Property tax (filed with the county auditor; deadline January 15)

Big picture: House Enrolled Act 1210 (2026), signed March 12, 2026 (P.L. 157-2026 per bill trackers; not confirmed on iga.in.gov), rewrote Indiana's veteran property tax benefits starting with the 2026 assessment date (taxes payable in 2027). The old structure (IC 6-1.1-12-13 $24,960 wartime deduction; IC 6-1.1-12-14 $14,000 deduction for total disability or age 62 with 10%, capped at $240,000 assessed value) is replaced by: a 100% deduction under IC 6-1.1-12-14 with no cap; a $350 credit under new IC 6-1.1-51.3-6; and a $250 credit under new IC 6-1.1-51.3-5. The IC 6-1.1-12-14.5 donated-homestead deduction continues. These benefits are uniform statewide; only the county auditor's processing differs.

Pre-2026 figures for reference (IDVA applicant letter, https://www.in.gov/dva/files/Property-Tax-Approved.pdf, Braun/Adams letterhead, undated): "Section 14 : $14,000 If you served on active duty for at least 90 days during any period, received an honorable discharge AND have a Permanent and Total disability (Paid at 100% Rate) if under age 62 OR a 10% disability if age 62 years or older. To receive this deduction, the assessed value of your property must be less than $240,000.00 beginning the 2024 assessment year. Section 13: $24,960 If you served during a recognized period of wartime service, received an honorable discharge, AND are service-connected disabled 10% or higher. Combination of Section 13 AND section 14: Both deductions may be taken if eligibility requirements are met for both section 13 and 14." The DLGF 2024 memo (https://in.gov/dlgf/files/2024-memos/240618-Shackle-Memo-Legislation-Affecting-Deductions-and-Exemptions.pdf) confirms HEA 1120-2024 raised the cap "from $200,000 to $240,000" for 2024 pay 2025.

### 1a. 100% deduction for totally disabled veterans (IC 6-1.1-12-14, as amended by HEA 1210-2026)
- Sources: DLGF FAQ https://www.in.gov/dlgf/files/2026-memos/Veterans-Deductions-FAQ_DLGF_08122026-1.pdf (dated June 17, 2026); State Form 12662 (R20 / 6-26) https://forms.in.gov/Download.aspx?id=16787; IDVA page https://www.in.gov/dva/divisions/training-and-services/disabled-veteran-property-tax-deduction/
- Threshold language (SF 12662, Section I): "A. Applicant served in the military or naval forces of the United States for at least ninety (90) days (not necessarily during war time). B. Applicant was honorably discharged. C. Applicant has a total disability. D. Applicant's disability is evidenced by: Certificate of eligibility (Section VIII) from the Indiana Department of Veterans Affairs ('IDVA') ...; Pension certificate from the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs; or Award of compensation from U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. E. Applicant has resided in Indiana for at least one (1) year before the assessment date for which this deduction is claimed. ... G. Applicant uses the property on which the deduction is claimed as the applicant's homestead (principal place of residence)."
- IDVA page: "An individual may have 100% of the assessed value deducted from their principal place of residence."
- DLGF FAQ: "Section 14 provides a deduction equal to 100% of the assessed value of real property, a mobile home not assessed as real property, or a manufactured home not assessed as real property that the qualifying veteran owns (or is buying under a qualifying recorded contract) and uses as the veteran's principal place of residence." Safe harbor: "the dwelling, up to one (1) acre of immediately surrounding land, and the qualifying residential improvements."
- TDIU / 100% schedular / 100% P&T: the statute (as reproduced on SF 12662) requires only "a total disability." It does not say "permanent," "100 percent," or "individual unemployability." DLGF FAQ: "IC 6-1.1-12-14 does not state that the veteran's total disability must be service-connected. Section 14 requires a total disability evidenced by a VA pension certificate or award of compensation, or an IDVA certificate of eligibility." Also: "A 10% disability rating by itself does not satisfy the total disability requirement." So 100% schedular qualifies; P&T qualifies; non-service-connected total disability (VA pension) qualifies. TDIU is not named anywhere. The pre-2026 IDVA letter described the old deduction as requiring "Permanent and Total disability (Paid at 100% Rate)," which suggests IDVA has historically read "total disability" as the 100% pay rate, but no official page addresses TDIU. Flagged.
- Assessed value cap: DLGF FAQ Q9: "HEA 1210-2026 removed the assessed value eligibility limitation from IC 6-1.1-12-14. Beginning with the 2026 assessment date (2026 Pay 2027), there is no maximum assessed value for real property receiving the section 14 deduction" (a separate limit in IC 6-1.1-12-40.5 applies to mobile/manufactured homes not assessed as real property).
- Deadline, office, forms: SF 12662: "This form must be completed, signed, and filed by January 15 of the calendar year in which the property taxes are first due and payable. File with the county auditor of the county where the property is located." DLGF FAQ: "If the application is mailed, it must be postmarked on or before the filing deadline. A person who fails to apply by the statutory deadline may not apply for the deduction or credit retroactively." Required documents (IDVA): DD214 and U.S. DVA Disability Award Letter. IDVA notes "SF 51186 is no longer used."
- Transition: DLGF FAQ Q5: "For 2026 Pay 2027 only, if a taxpayer received a deduction under IC 6-1.1-12-14 ... the county auditor must not carry over that former deduction and must instead apply the $250 credit under IC 6-1.1-51.3-5. A veteran who qualifies for the amended 100% deduction may file for that deduction instead. Therefore, a new filing is required to receive the amended 100% deduction for 2026 Pay 2027."
- No stacking with credits: SF 12662: "an individual who claims a deduction under Section I may not claim a credit under Section II or Section III or a deduction under Section V for that same assessment date. (IC 6-1.1-12-14(d); IC 6-1.1-12-14.5(d))." DLGF: "This prohibition applies to the individual receiving the section 14 deduction even if taxable assessed value or property tax liability remains on the same property or on other property owned by that individual."
- Surviving spouse (SF 12662 I.F): "Applicant is the surviving spouse of an individual who would have qualified for the deduction under this section when he or she was alive and the applicant has not subsequently remarried." DLGF: "Yes, if the veteran satisfied IC 6-1.1-12-14(a)(1) through (a)(4) at the time of death and the surviving spouse owns or is buying the property under a qualifying contract when the deduction statement is filed. The spouse is no longer eligible if the spouse subsequently remarries."
- Residency / ownership: one year of Indiana residency before the assessment date; principal place of residence; owner or recorded contract buyer. Trusts: "HEA 1210 removed the 100% disabled veteran deduction as a deduction to which a trust may be entitled." County summaries (Hamilton County, via news) also say LLC-titled homes are ineligible; not verified on an official page.
- Income cap: none.
- Annual refiling: "Generally, no."
- Sale: deduction stays on the assessment for one year after a conveyance (IC 6-1.1-12-45).
- County variation: none in amount or eligibility; auditor processes.
- 2025-2026 changes: HEA 1210-2026 as above. No 2025 veteran change found (SEA 1-2025 was the general property tax reform; I found no veteran-specific provision in it, but the DLGF 2025 memo could not be located before the search budget ran out).

### 1b. $350 credit, wartime service and 10%+ service-connected disability (IC 6-1.1-51.3-6)
- Sources: DLGF FAQ (above); SF 12662 Section II; IDVA page.
- Threshold language (SF 12662 Section II): "A. Applicant served in the military or naval forces of the United States during any of its wars. B. Applicant was honorably discharged. C. Applicant has a service-connected disability of at least 10% or more. D. Applicant's disability is evidenced by: Certificate of eligibility ... from the Indiana Department of Veterans Affairs ...; Pension certificate from the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs; or Award of compensation or disability compensation check from U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. E. Applicant is the surviving spouse of an individual who would have qualified for the credit under this section when he or she was alive."
- IDVA wartime periods listed for this credit: WWII Dec 7, 1941 to Dec 31, 1946; Korea June 27, 1950 to Jan 31, 1955; Vietnam Nov 1, 1955 to Aug 4, 1964 (in-country) or Aug 5, 1964 to May 7, 1975; Gulf War Aug 2, 1990 to a date set by law or Presidential Proclamation.
- Amount: "Credit not to exceed $350." DLGF: "A single $250 or $350 credit may be split among multiple qualifying properties; however, it is not a separate credit for each parcel ... any unused credit may not be carried forward, carried back, or refunded."
- Residency: DLGF: "The $250 and $350 credits do not contain an Indiana-residency requirement."
- Surviving spouse: see E above; no remarriage bar is stated for this credit on the form.
- Transition from the old $24,960 deduction: "Not by statute. IC 6-1.1-51.3-6 requires an application and does not contain an automatic conversion provision. DLGF recommends that counties automatically transfer taxpayers receiving the former $24,960 deduction to the new $350 credit if the taxpayer remains eligible."
- Trusts may receive this credit. Stackable with the $250 credit and Over 65 / Blind-Disabled credits ("Credits are stackable," IDVA).
- Deadline/office: January 15, county auditor, SF 12662.

### 1c. $250 credit, age 62+ and 10%+ disability (IC 6-1.1-51.3-5)
- Threshold language (SF 12662 Section III): "A. Applicant served in the military or naval forces of the United States for at least ninety (90) days (not necessarily during war time). B. Applicant was honorably discharged. C. Applicant is at least age 62 with at least 10% disability. D. [same VA/IDVA evidence] E. Applicant is the surviving spouse of an individual who: (1) would have qualified for the credit under this section when he or she was alive; or (2) was killed in action, died while serving on active duty, or died while performing inactive duty training."
- The 10% disability here is not stated as service-connected (contrast the $350 credit). DLGF: "A 33-year old veteran with a 10% disability does not qualify for the $250 credit because IC 6-1.1-51.3-5 requires the veteran to be at least 62 years old."
- Transition: automatic for 2026 pay 2027 for anyone who had the old section 14 deduction (IC 6-1.1-51.3-5(g)).
- Amount "not to exceed $250"; same splitting, no-carryforward, no-residency rules as the $350 credit.

### 1d. Homestead donated to veteran deduction (IC 6-1.1-12-14.5)
- Source: SF 12662 Section V; IDVA page.
- Threshold language: "A. Applicant served in the military or naval forces of the United States for at least ninety (90) days. B. Applicant was honorably discharged. C. Applicant has a disability of at least 50%. ... E. Applicant's homestead was conveyed without charge to the applicant who is the owner of the homestead by an organization that is exempt from income taxation under the federal Internal Revenue Code." Amount: "If the applicant is totally disabled, the deduction is equal to 100% of the assessed value of the homestead. If the applicant has a disability of at least 90% but is not totally disabled ... 90% ... 80% ... 70% ... 60% ... at least 50% but less than 60% ... 50%." "A veteran who claims this deduction for an assessment date may not also claim the Totally Disabled Veteran Deduction under IC 6-1.1-12-14 for that same assessment date. Moreover, an unused portion of this deduction may NOT be applied to excise taxes." Applicant "must own the property" (no contract purchase).
- This is Indiana's closest analog to an SAH-type benefit; it is tied to a charitable conveyance, not to a federal SAH grant. No Indiana SAH-grant-based deduction was found.

### 1e. Surviving spouse of WWI veteran (IC 6-1.1-12-16)
- SF 12662 Section IV: "Deduction not to exceed $18,720" for the surviving spouse of a veteran who served before November 12, 1918 and was honorably discharged. Listed for completeness; not in the JSON.

## 2. Vehicles, driver license, parking

### 2a. Disabled Hoosier Veteran (DHV) plate and placard (IC 9-18.5-5-1)
- Sources: IDVA https://www.in.gov/dva/divisions/training-and-services/veteran-license-plates/ ; IDVA approval letter https://secure.in.gov/dva/files/DHV-PLATE-Approved-New-letterhead-4-2.pdf ; BMV https://www.in.gov/bmv/registration-plates/drivers-with-disabilities/
- Threshold language (IDVA, paraphrasing IC 9-18.5-5-1): service-related (1) "Loss of sight in both eyes, or permanent vision impairment in both eyes, to the extent of service-connected compensation eligibility"; (2) loss or permanent loss of use of one or both feet; (3) loss or permanent loss of use of one or both hands; (4) a VA disability rating for a physical condition that "precludes the individual from walking without pain or difficulty"; (5) "rated by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs as being at least fifty percent (50%) disabled and receiving service related compensation. At least sixty percent (60%) of the disability rating under this subdivision must be attributable to a mobility disability." Application "must include a certificate from the United States Department of Veterans Affairs or the appropriate branch of the armed forces." "Veterans can obtain a plate or placard for each registered vehicle they own."
- Process: IDVA approves (State Form 32584; "Disabled Veteran applications require a VA claim number"; "Most applications are approved within two days"); take stamped form to BMV. BMV: "A permanent disability parking placard may also be issued to a person who has been issued or is otherwise eligible to receive a Disabled Hoosier Veteran (DHV) license plate." Form 42070 for BMV disability plates/placards.
- Parking: IDVA letter: "This entitles the veteran to park in any marked handicapped space, including parking meters designated only for handicapped persons, but the privilege is assigned to the disabled veteran, not to the vehicle. The Disabled Hoosier Veteran license plate and placard allows for free parking at metered spaces in Indiana. However; a person other than the veteran is not entitled to the free parking privilege."
- Fees: no fee waiver stated; normal registration fees and excise tax apply. Exact DHV plate fee not found on BMV pages.
- No TDIU / rating-only path: a 100% rating with no mobility component does not by itself qualify.

### 2b. Vehicle excise tax credit for veterans without property (IC 6-6-5-5.2) and unused-deduction carryover (IC 6-6-5-5)
- Sources: BMV State Form 52870 (R3 / 12-24) https://forms.in.gov/download.aspx?id=11539 ; DLGF FAQ; IDVA letter.
- SF 52870: "An eligible veteran who is not a property owner may claim Vehicle Excise Tax or Recreational Vehicle Excise Tax credit. Must be submitted with affidavit from County Auditor stating that the claimant does not own qualifying property. Credit may be claimed for up to 2 vehicles. Credit may not exceed the amount of excise tax due, and is limited to $70 per vehicle, per registration year." Affirmation: "I do not own property to which a property tax deduction may be applied under Indiana Code 6-1.1-12-13, Indiana Code 6-1.1-12-14, or Indiana Code 6-1.1-12-16."
- DLGF FAQ: "Yes, if the requirements of IC 6-6-5-5.2 are met and the individual does not own property to which the specified veteran property tax deduction may be applied. The credit is the lesser of the vehicle excise tax liability or $70 per vehicle, and it may be claimed for no more than two (2) vehicles. An affidavit from the county auditor is required." On IC 6-6-5-5: "the 100% deduction under amended IC 6-1.1-12-14 generally will not generate a remaining deduction amount for that purpose."
- Flag: the BMV form predates HEA 1210 and references IC 6-1.1-12-13, which is no longer a veteran deduction. How the $350/$250 credits map to the excise credit is not spelled out on any official page.

### 2c. Hoosier Veteran plate, Purple Heart plate (IC 9-18.5-6)
- IDVA: Hoosier Veteran plate: "Any state resident honorably separated from the active Armed Forces may buy one"; "Cost is $15, plus $45 for personalization"; bring DD-214 to BMV; "No application form is required." Purple Heart plate: for a recipient "or to a surviving spouse who has not remarried"; modified plate with accessibility symbol for recipients eligible for a disability placard/plate; fee not stated. Gold Star plate: no cost beyond regular registration fees (SF 54181).

## 3. Hunting, fishing, parks

### 3a. Disabled veteran hunting and fishing license (IC 14-22-12-1.5)
- Sources: DNR https://www.in.gov/dnr/fish-and-wildlife/licenses-and-permits/disabled-veterans/ ; IDVA https://www.in.gov/dva/divisions/training-and-services/disabled-veteran-discounted-hunting-and-fishing-license-and-golden-hoosier-passport/
- DNR: "Indiana residents who have served in the armed forces and have a documented service-connected disability are eligible." License "sells for $2.75"; 10-year DAV license $27.50 covering "a total of 10 hunting/fishing seasons." Covers small game hunting and fishing; "Gamebird, Waterfowl, and Trout and Salmon stamps must be bought separately at the regular price"; does not cover deer or turkey.
- IDVA: eligibility includes "disability retirement benefits administered under Department of Defense laws"; State Form 50833; "DNR will not process a faxed copy." DNR's own page lists fax 317-232-8150 and email INHuntFish@dnr.IN.gov. Conflict noted.
- Process: one-time DNR approval, then buy at GoOutdoorsIN.com, DNR property, or vendor.
- No minimum percentage: any service-connected disability.

### 3b. State park passes (IC 14-19-3-4; IC 9-18.5-5)
- Source: DNR https://www.in.gov/dnr/state-parks/rates-and-fees/entrance-fees/
- "$7 In-State Daily Entrance"; "$15 Out-of-State Daily Entrance"; "$50 Annual Entrance Pass"; "$120 Non-resident Annual Entrance Pass." Disabled Hoosier Veteran pass $25: "Persons eligible for a Disabled Hoosier Veteran license plate under IC 9-18.5-5 may purchase this pass." Golden Hoosier Passport $25 "For Indiana residents who are at least age 65 years" or eligible for Social Security disability. "Indiana residents who are former POWs or their surviving spouses are entitled to a free Indiana Golden Hoosier Passport" (mailed automatically). Gold Star family members (Indiana residents) get a free Annual Entrance Pass. The page says veterans generally do not get free admission otherwise.
- IDVA: pass is "not valid for historic sites, museums, memorials, and other Department of Natural Resources (DNR) facilities." Buy online (stores.innsgifts.com/entrance) or at DNR locations with DHV registration or SF 32584.
- Veterans Day: events.in.gov listing "Free admission to DNR properties for veterans, active-duty military, Nov. 11" (https://events.in.gov/event/free-admission-to-dnr-properties-for-veterans-active-duty-military-nov-11, posted Nov. 6, 2024): "All veterans and active-duty military personnel, and everyone in their vehicle, will be admitted free" to "DNR-managed state parks, reservoir properties, state forest recreation areas, and off-road state recreation areas" plus the Falls of the Ohio Interpretive Center; "simply let the gate attendant at the property they visit know." No 2025 or 2026 listing found. Free hunting/fishing days: not researched (budget).

## 4. Education and tuition

### 4a. Tuition and Fee Exemption for Children of Disabled Veterans (IC 21-14-4)
- Source: CHE https://www.in.gov/che/state-financial-aid/state-financial-aid-by-program/tuition-and-fee-exemption-children-of-disabled-veterans ; IDVA https://www.in.gov/dva/divisions/training-and-services/tuition-and-fee-exemption/ (cites "Indiana Code 21-14-4-1")
- Veteran: served "during a war, or in duty 'equally hazardous' that earned a service or campaign medal"; "service-connected death or disability as determined by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs"; discharge "other than dishonorable." Child: biological or adopted (adoption before 18).
- Residency: veteran "listed Indiana as home of record at enlistment, or lived in Indiana at least five years before the student's first application"; student eligible for resident tuition.
- Amount: enlisted before July 1, 2011: "100% of tuition and regularly assessed fees at the undergraduate resident rate." Enlisted on or after July 1, 2011: "20% + the veteran parent's VA disability percentage."
- Limits: "up to 124 semester credit hours"; eight academic years; SAP required since July 1, 2020; student "must first apply ... before turning thirty-three (33) years old."
- Private nonprofits: "up to $5,000" a year for students who graduated high school on or after January 1, 2023.
- Apply: one-time ScholarTrack.IN.gov application; FAFSA each year; IDVA reviews.
- The 2023 change: private-school option for 2023+ graduates. The 2011 change: percentage formula for post-June 30, 2011 enlistees.

### 4b. Indiana Purple Heart Recipient exemption; children of Purple Heart recipients
- Source: https://www.in.gov/che/state-financial-aid/state-financial-aid-by-program/tuition-and-fee-exemption-indiana-purple-heart-recipient
- Veteran "Received the Purple Heart decoration or was wounded as a result of enemy action"; "entered active duty from a permanent home address in Indiana"; discharge other than dishonorable; up to 100% of tuition and fees for 124 credit hours at Indiana public institutions; eight academic years. Children of Purple Heart recipients have a parallel exemption with the same 2011 percentage rule (CHE page fetched).

### 4c. National Guard Tuition Supplement Grant
- Source: https://www.in.gov/che/state-financial-aid/state-financial-aid-by-program/national-guard-tuition-supplement-grant
- Indiana National Guard member "in active drilling status," not AWOL in prior 12 months, seeking first associate, bachelor's, or master's degree; covers tuition and regularly assessed fees; "up to $5,000" at private nonprofits; unit commander certifies each semester. Not a disabled-veteran benefit.

### 4d. In-state tuition for veterans: not researched (budget). CHE pages list no separate veteran in-state tuition program.

## 5. State income tax
- Sources: DOR Information Bulletin #27 (June 2025, effective Jan. 1, 2025) https://www.in.gov/dor/files/ib27.pdf ; Bulletin #6 (June 2025) https://www.in.gov/dor/files/ib06.pdf
- IB27: "Military retirement pay received by an Indiana resident is deductible in full for Indiana tax purposes. ... This deduction is available for the military member and the miliary member's surviving spouse. However, if a divorced spouse receives a portion of a service member's military retirement pay as a result of a divorce decree or similar agreement, that portion of the pay is not deductible for the spouse. The individual need not have been an Indiana resident during active military service to qualify for this adjustment."
- IB06 phase-in: "For 2019, the percentage will be 25 percent. For 2020 ... 50 percent. For 2021 ... 75 percent. For 2022 and later, the percentage will be 100 percent." No age requirement since 2018. Claimed on IT-40 Schedule 2.
- Military Service Deduction: "military personnel on active duty or in the active reserves may deduct up to $5,000 of their military pay"; from 2023 only active components; National Guard and reserve wages fully deductible (2023 and later).
- VA disability compensation: not addressed by name in either bulletin. IB27 defines "wages" to exclude "retirement or disability income from military service." Because Indiana AGI starts from federal AGI and VA compensation is excluded federally, it is not taxed; this is an inference, not a quoted DOR statement.
- 2025 change: Space Force, USPHS Commissioned Corps, and NOAA Corps added effective Jan. 1, 2025. No 2026 change found.

## 8. 2025-2026 law changes checked
- HEA 1210-2026 (signed March 12, 2026; ceremonial signing June 2026): 100% deduction for totally disabled veterans with no assessed value cap; $350 and $250 credits (IC 6-1.1-51.3-5, -6) replace the $24,960 and $14,000 deductions; trusts lose the 100% deduction; no stacking of the deduction with any 51.3 credit; effective 2026 assessment / 2027 payable; January 15, 2027 filing deadline for the new deduction. Source: DLGF FAQ; SF 12662 R20 6-26.
- HEA 1120-2024: raised the IC 6-1.1-12-14 cap from $200,000 to $240,000 for 2024 pay 2025 (DLGF 2024 memo). Now moot.
- 2025 legislation: DOR bulletins updated for Space Force/USPHS/NOAA Corps (Jan. 1, 2025). SEA 1-2025 (property tax reform): no veteran-specific provision identified; DLGF 2025 memo not located.
- DNR fees: resident annual pass $50, DHV pass $25 (current DNR page).
