## 1. Property tax (parish assessor; constitutional, uniform statewide amounts)

Louisiana's veteran exemption is in the state constitution, so the dollar tiers are the same in every parish. Only paperwork and the treatment of municipal (city) taxes vary locally. Louisiana assesses residential property at 10 percent of fair market value (Const. Art. VII Sec. 18(B); not separately fetched this session), so $7,500 of assessed value is roughly $75,000 of market value.

### 1a. Base homestead exemption (Const. Art. VII Sec. 20(A)(1))
- Source: https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=206550
- Text: "The bona fide homestead ... owned and occupied by any person or persons owning the property in indivision, shall be exempt from state, parish, and special ad valorem taxes to the extent of seven thousand five hundred dollars of the assessed valuation." Also applies to a mobile home used as the primary residence even if the owner does not own the land.
- Note the list: "state, parish, and special" taxes. Municipal taxes are not listed, which is why the East Baton Rouge assessor says city taxes and fees still appear on the bill even for 100% veterans.
- 2025 HB 271 (constitutional amendment to let parishes raise the homestead exemption by up to $5,000 and re-word 21(K)) "Failed House final passage" (legis.la.gov BillInfo, 25RS HB271). No change.

### 1b. Full exemption, 100% unemployability or totally disabled (Const. Art. VII Sec. 21(K)(1)(c))
- Source: https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=206551 (page shows history "Acts 2022, No. 172, §1, approved Nov. 8, 2022, eff. Dec. 13, 2022" and a note that a revised (K)(1) takes effect "Jan. 1, 2027, upon ratification of the const. amend. proposed by Acts 2026, No. 39")
- Threshold language: "In addition to the homestead exemption authorized pursuant to the provisions of Section 20 of this Article, which applies to the first seven thousand five hundred dollars of the assessed valuation of property, the remaining assessed valuation of property receiving the homestead exemption that is owned and occupied by a veteran with a service-connected disability rating of one hundred percent unemployability or totally disabled by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs shall be exempt from ad valorem taxation."
- TDIU / 100% schedular / 100% P&T: the constitution says "one hundred percent unemployability or totally disabled." TDIU is therefore expressly covered by name ("unemployability"). "Totally disabled" covers a 100% schedular rating. The words "permanent" or "permanent and total" do not appear, so P&T is not required by the text. No official Louisiana source found adds a permanence requirement. The Jefferson Parish assessor paraphrases the tier as "For 100% disability or total unemployability." East Baton Rouge: "Service connected disability rating of 100% will be eligible to be exempt from the full assessed value of the property. (city taxes and fees will still be due on your tax bill each year)."
- Amount: all remaining assessed value of the homestead (state, parish, special taxes). LDVA: "100%: exempt from all ad valorem tax, except certain municipal assessments."
- Deadline / office / forms: no statewide deadline or state form found. Apply at the parish assessor's office. East Baton Rouge mails homestead cards each September or October to confirm the exemption is still in place. Jefferson Parish requires appearing in person with a recorded act of sale and photo ID. No parish page I reached lists the exact VA document required; OMV and LDWF require a VA rating letter or an LDVA-17 affidavit for their benefits, and the same documents are the practical proof here.
- Effective: tax year 2023 (Act 172 of 2022 / voter approval Nov. 8, 2022; Jefferson Parish: "On November 8, 2022, voters approved an additional exemption for disabled veterans").
- Residency / ownership: must be "owned and occupied" by the veteran and must be property "receiving the homestead exemption" (bona fide homestead, one per household). No separate length-of-residency rule. No income cap.
- County (parish) variation: amounts uniform; procedures and whether city taxes still apply vary.
- (K)(2): the exempt value "shall not be treated as taxable property for purposes of any subsequent reappraisal and valuation or millage adjustment," and implementing the exemption "shall neither trigger nor be cause for a reappraisal of property or an adjustment of millages."

### 1c. 70% to 99%: additional $4,500 assessed value (Const. Art. VII Sec. 21(K)(1)(b))
- Threshold language: "the next four thousand five hundred dollars of the assessed valuation of property owned and occupied by a veteran with a service-connected disability rating of seventy percent or more but less than one hundred percent by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs shall be exempt from ad valorem taxation. ... If property eligible for the exemption provided for in this Subsubparagraph has an assessed value in excess of twelve thousand dollars, ad valorem property taxes shall apply to the assessment in excess of twelve thousand dollars."
- Total exempt: $12,000 assessed value (about $120,000 market). EBR example: "7,500 assessed value + 4,500 assessed value = 12,000 assessed value exempted."

### 1d. 50% to 69%: additional $2,500 assessed value (Const. Art. VII Sec. 21(K)(1)(a))
- Threshold language: "the next two thousand five hundred dollars of the assessed valuation of property receiving the homestead exemption that is owned and occupied by a veteran with a service-connected disability rating of fifty percent or more but less than seventy percent by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs shall be exempt from ad valorem taxation. ... If property eligible for the exemption provided for in this Subsubparagraph has an assessed value in excess of ten thousand dollars, ad valorem property taxes shall apply to the assessment in excess of ten thousand dollars."
- Total exempt: $10,000 assessed value (about $100,000 market).

### 1e. Surviving spouse (all tiers) and the pending 2026 amendment
- Current text (each tier): "The surviving spouse of a deceased veteran with a service-connected disability rating of [tier] by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs shall be eligible for this exemption if the surviving spouse occupies and remains the owner of the property, whether or not the exemption was in effect on the property prior to the death of the veteran."
- No remarriage bar in the text. Spouse must keep owning and living in the same home; nothing transfers to a new home today.
- Act 39 of 2026 (SB 180, Foil), https://legis.la.gov/legis/BillInfo.aspx?s=26RS&b=SB180&sbi=y; Resume Digest: "Proposed constitutional amendment removes the requirement that the surviving spouse occupy and remain the owner of the property ... once the surviving spouse ... has taken the exemption, if the surviving spouse then acquires a different property which qualifies for the homestead exemption, the surviving spouse shall be entitled to a one-time transfer of the exemption on that subsequent homestead. The exemption shall be limited in value to the amount of the exemption claimed on the prior homestead in the last year for which the exemption was claimed." "Proposed constitutional amendment is applicable to taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2027. ... Specifies submission of the amendment to the voters at the statewide election to be held on November 3, 2026." New (K)(1)(d) text is already posted on the legis.la.gov constitution page with the Jan. 1, 2027 effective note. Outcome unknown as of research date.

### 1f. Specially adapted housing / other veteran property tax items
- None found. Louisiana has no separate SAH, wheelchair, or paraplegic property tax exemption and no age-65 veteran discount. (The general Art. VII Sec. 18(G) special assessment level freeze for seniors, disabled persons, and 50%+ disabled veterans exists but was not researched this session; see Could not verify.)

## 2. Vehicles, driver license, parking

### 2a. 100% DAV military honor plate (R.S. 47:490.31; OMV Policy 160.00)
- Source: https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=1017799 ("Acts 2016, No. 194, §1"); OMV Policy 160.00 (Effective 06/26/2017, Revised 08/28/2017), https://public.powerdms.com/ladpsc/documents/366091
- Threshold: a "qualified disabled veteran" is a Louisiana resident "with a service-connected disability of one hundred percent as determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs." No P&T requirement; TDIU not addressed (the plate statute keys on the 100% rating, so TDIU at the 100% rate is unaddressed and should be confirmed with OMV).
- Plate shows "100% DAV" and the international symbol of accessibility; "No fee shall be charged for the license plates"; not subject to renewal; one plate "for each vehicle registered in the applicant's name"; holder gets "the same privileges as holders of license plates for persons with mobility impairments"; free accessibility hang tag, exempt from renewal; replacement of a lost tag carries the R.S. 47:463.4(C) reissuance fee.
- OMV Policy 160.00: "This is a free lifetime plate. $3.00 - fee to transfer the plate to another vehicle. If the plate is lost or stolen ... $10.00 ... $8.00 - handling fee." Required: vehicle registration copy and "An affidavit (LDVA-17) signed by a Veteran Affairs service officer." "If the applicant qualifies for a Disabled Veteran License Plate, he may also be issued a handicap placard (permanent), upon request, at no charge."

### 2b. 50%+ disabled veteran plate, mobility-impaired plate, Purple Heart DV plate, airport parking
- LDVA State Benefits page (https://vetaffairs.la.gov/benefits/state, no date shown): "Free specialty plates: Veterans rated 50% or higher may get a disabled veterans plate or a military honors plate at no charge. Veterans rated 100% may get the 100% DAV plate."
- OMV Policy 14.00 Mobility Impaired License Plates (Revised 07/21/2026), https://public.powerdms.com/ladpsc/documents/366704: "Mobility impaired persons who have a permanent impairment or veterans who have a 50% or more service connected disability are eligible for the mobility impaired license plates." "Disabled Veteran - In the case of a disabled veteran, an affidavit from the Veterans Affairs Office attesting to the veteran's disability is required ... A disabled veteran is considered to have a permanent impairment."
- R.S. 47:490.32 (https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=1148936, "Acts 2019, No. 6"): Purple Heart recipients "who have a service-connected disability of fifty percent or more and who are residents of this state" get a "DV"-prefixed military honor plate; "No fee shall be charged for the license plates authorized by this Section"; free accessibility hang tag.
- The statute creating the general (non-Purple-Heart) 50%+ disabled veteran plate was not located (search budget exhausted); row flagged.
- Airport and cruise parking (LDVA State Benefits page): "at least 50%" service-connected rating; free airport parking for any length with a Veterans Assistance Counselor (VAC) certified form and itinerary (requirements vary by airport); 7 days free at the Port of New Orleans cruise terminal with VAC form and a military honor plate. Act 86 of 2025 (HB 508) limits the cruise terminal benefit to Louisiana resident disabled veterans (LDVA June 20, 2025 release).
- Auto insurance (LDVA page): a 25% premium discount for retirees and 50%+ veterans "at the carrier's discretion"; not a state benefit, not included in the table.
- Vehicle sales/use tax: no disabled-veteran exemption exists. New residents pay no more than $90 use tax on a vehicle they already owned (R.S. 47:302(D)(1), LDR RIB 25-011, Feb. 2025); LDVA lists this as a veteran benefit but it applies to all new residents. A 2025 bill to exempt veterans from vehicle sales tax (reported as SB 545) returned no bill record at legis.la.gov.

### 2c. Driver license / ID card fee exemption (R.S. 32:412.2; OMV Policy 2.01)
- Source: https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=452291 ("Acts 2007, No. 123, §1; Acts 2010, No. 402, §1; Acts 2012, No. 95, §1")
- Text: a veteran is exempt from driver license and special ID fees "under this Chapter" if the veteran (1) "Was honorably discharged," (2) "Has a service-related disability of at least fifty percent," (3) "Receives compensation from the United States government because of such disability," and (4) "Is not subject to the registration requirements of Chapter 3-B of Title 15" (sex offender registry).
- OMV Policy 2.01 (Effective 10/23/2007, Revised 08/31/2021), https://public.powerdms.com/LADPSC/documents/364297: covers "fees (including the handling and parish fees) for the issuance (initial, renewal, duplicate, amended, etc.)." Documents: "DD-214, certification from Veteran's Administration, or decision letter"; "Statement or form from the Department of Veteran's Affairs" for the 50% rating; a 100% statement to add the "100% DAV" indicator "under their portrait."
- "Veteran" designation: OMV Policy 2.02 (per search summary, not fetched) requires honorable discharge documentation; no fee if the current credential is surrendered; normal fees if added at renewal. LDVA: waivers "may not apply at privatized OMV offices."

## 3. Hunting, fishing, parks

### 3a. Disabled Veterans Hunting and Fishing License (R.S. 56:3000(H))
- Statute: https://legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=1240979 (history "Acts 2021, No. 356, §3, eff. June 1, 2022; Acts 2022, No. 229, §1, eff. June 1, 2022; Acts 2024, No. 348, §3; Acts 2025, No. 321, §1")
- Threshold language: "Upon identification and proof of disability satisfactory to the department, the following people may be issued a Disabled/Special Needs Hunting and Fishing license: ... A person who is a veteran of the United States Armed Forces, including the Louisiana Army National Guard or the Louisiana Air National Guard, having a permanent service connected disability classification of fifty percent or more." Also "Any resident of Louisiana who is the surviving spouse of a member of the United States Armed Forces ... who was killed in action while in a combat zone."
- LDWF Special Licenses page (https://www.wlf.louisiana.gov/page/special-licenses-and-permits, no date shown): "For veterans who have a permanent service-connected disability classification of 50% or more. Free (The Louisiana Wildlife and Fisheries Foundation covers the $4 fee for this license). Includes Basic and Saltwater Fishing Licenses; Basic, Deer, Waterfowl, and Turkey Hunting Licenses; and the WMA Access Permit. Does not include the Federal Duck Stamp." Apply in person at 2000 Quail Drive, Baton Rouge, by email to lifetime@wlf.la.gov, or by mail with a copy of your driver license/ID and proof of disability. LDVA: "Licenses expire yearly and must be renewed."
- LDWF news release, Aug. 7, 2025 (https://www.wlf.louisiana.gov/news/ldwf-offering-commemorative-durable-hard-cards-free-to-resident-and-nonresident-disabled-veterans): "Those qualifying for the cards and disabled hunting/fishing licenses include any resident or non-resident veteran of the U.S. Armed Forces, including the Louisiana Army or Air National Guard, having a permanent service connected disability classification of 50% or more." Hard cards authorized by Act 321 of 2025.
- Note the word "permanent": LDWF requires a permanent 50%+ rating; a temporary rating may be refused.

### 3b. Other veteran licenses (R.S. 56:3000(F), (G))
- Honorably discharged resident veteran combo (Act 321 of 2025, eff. Aug. 1, 2025): "Any bona fide resident who is an honorably discharged member of the United States Armed Forces, including the Louisiana Army National Guard or the Louisiana Air National Guard" qualifies for the Senior/Retired Military/Honorably Discharged Veteran license; LDWF: "Honorably discharged military veteran residents may purchase a recreational hunting and fishing combination license for $20." DD-214 or NGB-22 is satisfactory proof. Retired military born in Louisiana or bona fide residents: $20 (resident or nonresident).
- Purple Heart recipients: "may be issued an annual hunting and fishing license at the resident rate" (any residency). Honorably discharged members who enlisted while residing in Louisiana: resident rates.

### 3c. State park day-use permit (R.S. 56:1694)
- Source: https://legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=104938 ("Acts 1980, No. 827, §1; Acts 1991, No. 372, §1")
- Who: Louisiana residents who are veterans and who have had a limb amputated, received a federal automobile allowance, or are rated by the Social Security Administration or the VA as totally and permanently disabled or at least 50% disabled; they "shall be exempt from paying the day use entrance fee to any Louisiana state park." Application to the assistant secretary of the Office of State Parks; permit issued after LDVA verifies eligibility; everyone in the same single private non-commercial vehicle is also exempt. Permit holders may also buy a Golden Pelican Permit.
- Louisiana State Parks Special Rates page (https://www.lastateparks.com/special-ratesdiscounts, copyright 2026): "Free admission to all sites is provided for Disabled Veterans ... with State Park Permit. Eligible veterans may apply for the permit by contacting their local parish veterans service office." Active duty: 50% off day use with military photo ID; active and retired military, Guard and reservists: "15% off all rentable facilities" with code MILITARY. No camping or cabin discount for disabled veterans.
- LDVA: "Disabled State Park Pass: Veterans rated 50% or higher who apply for this pass do not pay the day-use entrance fee at any Louisiana state park."

## 4. Education and tuition

### 4a. Title 29 dependents' tuition exemption (R.S. 29:288)
- Source: https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=85513 (most recent amendments "Acts 2023, No. 46, eff. June 1, 2023" and "Acts 2024, No. 74")
- Qualifying veteran (A(1)(a), B(1)): killed in action, died in active service from other causes, missing in action, prisoner of war, "died of a service-connected disability incurred during a wartime period," or "rated ninety percent or above" service-connected disabled or "determined to be unemployable as a result of a service-connected disability" before death. Living veterans (A(1)(b)): rated 90 to 100 percent or determined unemployable, resident of Louisiana at least twelve months before the child enrolls.
- Residency: veteran "resided in Louisiana for a period of not less than twelve months" before entering service or before death, or was permanently assigned and reported for duty at an active, Guard, or Reserve installation in Louisiana (HB 385 / Act 74 of 2024 shortened this from 24 months per the search digest). LDVA Title 29 Residency Requirements PDF (dated 6.20.25) lists acceptable proof: death certificate, DD-214 with Louisiana home of record, Louisiana diploma or transcripts, or three documents such as a Louisiana driver license, voter registration, vehicle registration, homestead exemption form, or Louisiana resident tax return.
- Children: "not less than sixteen nor more than twenty-five years of age"; exempt from "tuition, matriculation, registration" and laboratory, athletic, medical, nonresidency, and other special fees at any public postsecondary institution in Louisiana. Spouses and surviving spouses: same exemptions, "within ten years from the date of eligibility." No duplication with R.S. 17:1686 benefits (C).
- Process (LDVA Title 29 page): contact the parish veterans service office; a Veterans Assistance Counselor determines eligibility; LDVA headquarters mails a certificate; the student takes it to the school.
- TDIU: the statute's "determined to be unemployable as a result of a service-connected disability" expressly includes TDIU.

### 4b. In-state tuition for veterans and dependents
- LSU Registrar residency page (https://www.lsu.edu/registrar/academics/residency.php, copyright 2026): "A Veteran of the Armed Forces, their spouse, and their dependents qualify as residents for tuition and fee purposes" if the student is "eligible to receive educational assistance administered by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs through any provision of the United States Code," and "this classification shall remain valid for as long as the student remains continuously enrolled." Statutory cite (Title 17) not retrieved; LDVA also lists "In-State Tuition Status" for veterans, spouses, children and active duty eligible for federal education benefits. Row flagged.

### 4c. Louisiana National Guard tuition exemption (R.S. 29:36.1)
- Source: https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=85550
- "Any student enrolled or who may enroll in a public institution of higher learning of this state who is seventeen years of age or over and who is serving in the Louisiana National Guard shall be exempt from all or a portion of tuition charges ... if the applicant for free tuition is presently domiciled in Louisiana. This exemption may be claimed for five separate academic years or until the receipt of a degree." Mobilized students get an extension equal to active duty time; exemption forfeited if Guard service ends or the student is placed on scholastic probation.

### 4d. 2025-2026 education changes
- Act 355 of 2026 (SB 105): veterans may use a DD-214 as proof of eligibility for TOPS, effective Aug. 1, 2026 (KEDM summary of the session; not independently fetched from legis.la.gov).
- Act 130 of 2025 (HB 54): "Purple Star Campus" designation for veteran-friendly colleges (LDVA release).
- Act 241 of 2025 (HB 116): MFA Board may "pay up to $1,000 of educational benefits for children of certain disabled veterans" (LDVA release).

## 5. State income tax
- R.S. 47:44.2 (https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=102134, "Acts 1989, No. 812"): "any income received by an individual pursuant to a retirement system for retirees of the United States Government ... shall be exempt from the state income tax." LDR FAQ (https://revenue.louisiana.gov/...retirement-system-benefits..., no date): "Federal retirement benefits received by federal retirees, both military and nonmilitary, may be excluded from Louisiana taxable income. Federal benefits received from a military survivor benefit plan may also be excluded."
- VA disability compensation: not addressed on the LDR page; it is excluded from federal AGI, which is Louisiana's starting point, so it is not taxed (general tax-law statement, not a quoted official source).
- Act 11 of the 2024 Third Extraordinary Session, LDR Revenue Information Bulletin 25-012 (March 7, 2025): "Act 11 repeals the graduated rates and brackets for individuals in favor of a flat 3% tax rate applicable to all taxable income" for "tax periods beginning on or after January 1, 2025"; standard deduction "from $4,500 to $12,500" single and "$9,000 to $25,000" joint, indexed to CPI-U "with the first adjustments occurring on January 1, 2026"; the age-65 retirement income exemption rises "from $6,000 to $12,000 per person." "The additional exemptions for dependents, blind persons, and persons over age 65 were repealed."
- 2025 HB 402 (Knox), "Authorizes an income tax deduction for veterans with certain disabilities": "Current Status: Pending Senate Finance" (legis.la.gov BillInfo 25RS HB402). Not enacted.
- The March 29, 2025 constitutional amendment election (Amendment 2, the tax and budget rewrite) was not researched; see Could not verify.

## 6. Employment preference
- Const. Art. X Sec. 10(A)(2), https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=206619: "The state and city civil service departments shall accord a five-point preference in original appointment to each person who served honorably in the armed forces of the United States during a war declared by the United States Congress; or in a peacetime campaign or expedition for which campaign badges are authorized; or for at least ninety days after September 11, 2001, for reasons other than training; or during war period dates or dates of armed conflicts as provided by state law ... The state and city civil service departments shall accord a ten-point preference in original appointment to each honorably discharged veteran who served either in peace or in war and who has one or more disabilities recognized as service-connected by the Veterans Administration; to the spouse of each veteran whose physical condition precludes his or her appointment to a civil service job in his or her usual line of work; to the unremarried widow of each deceased veteran who served in a war period ... or to the unremarried widowed parent of any person who died in active wartime or peacetime service or who suffered total and permanent disability ... However, only one ten-point preference shall be allowed in the original appointment to any person enumerated above. ... any such preference may be given only to a person who has attained at least the minimum score required on each test." (A)(3) gives layoff retention preference to "preference employees."
- War periods: R.S. 29:251.3 (not fetched).
- State Civil Service HR Handbook Procedure 22 (Revised 3/22/18), https://www.civilservice.louisiana.gov/files/HRHandbook/Procedure/22-Veterans%20Preference%20Information.pdf: "Veterans points are only added in NEW hiring situations – 'probational' or 'job appointments.'" "Persons claiming eligibility for 10 veterans points must submit a copy of the DD214 in addition to a letter from the Veterans Administration documenting service connected disability and it must be dated within the last six months of the application." Spouses, unremarried widows/widowers, or parents "must submit Form C.S. 295" with DD-214, VA statement, marriage certificate, and death or birth certificate. "Veterans preference cannot be verified without a DD214."
- Private employers may adopt a veteran hiring preference (R.S. 23:1001, Act 190 of 2016, LDVA-hosted copy).
- Act 160 of 2026 (HB 450): 180-day minimum service for veterans' points in fire and police civil service, eff. Aug. 1, 2026 (KEDM summary).
- Business: Louisiana Economic Development's Veteran Initiative and Veterans First Business Initiative (certification for 51% veteran-owned businesses) are listed by LDVA; no license fee waiver statute found.

## 7. Other (homes, cemeteries, grants, bonuses)
- Veterans Homes (https://vetaffairs.la.gov/homes, no date; admissions application dated 2025): homes in Jackson, Monroe, Bossier City, Reserve, Jennings. Eligible: "honorably discharged, during wartime or peacetime, and served 90 days of active duty," "eligible National Guard/Reservist," "spouses of veterans," "Gold Star parents." "Veterans with a VA service-connected rating of 70% or higher" or admitted "with a diagnosis related to their service connection at any level" may live there "free of charge." Others: "a monthly fee of $2,424" that "changes annually," offset by VA Aid and Attendance; spouses, Gold Star parents, Guard/Reservists: "a monthly fee of $5,500," "subject to change." Act 132 of 2025 (HB 60): Guard veterans and retirees who did not deploy under federal orders become eligible; Act 191 of 2025 (HB 387): LDVA may adjust fees for spouses and Gold Star families (LDVA June 20, 2025 release, https://vetaffairs.la.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/062025RegSessionFinal.pdf).
- Veterans Cemeteries (https://vetaffairs.la.gov/cemeteries): Leesville, Rayville, Keithville, Slidell, Jennings. "There is no interment, or burial, fee for eligible veterans." Spouses and dependent children pay a fee equal to "the federal VA plot allowance at the time of their death." Pre-need application optional. Guard/drilling reserve members must have been "a Louisiana resident at the time of death."
- Military Family Assistance Fund (https://vetaffairs.la.gov/military-family-assistance-fund): "up to $10,000 for one need-based claim in a 12-month period"; applicants must be "current Louisiana residents" with "a discharge of honorable or under honorable conditions," meeting minimum active-duty service; covers rent/mortgage, utilities, vehicle payments and insurance, food, clothing, some medical; excludes home purchase, credit cards, fines, cable, cosmetic procedures, taxes; "fund of last resort," reviewed at monthly board meetings. Act 241 of 2025 (HB 116, eff. Aug. 1, 2025): indigent veteran burials, up to $1,000 education benefits for children of certain disabled veterans, grants to veteran organizations.
- Louisiana National Guard Disability Benefit (LDVA page): $100,000 or $50,000 state benefit for Guard members permanently and totally disabled on state or federal active duty on or after Sept. 11, 2001 (R.S. 29:26.1, not fetched).
- Louisiana Veterans Honor Medal (LDVA): for all eligible Louisiana veterans.
- Act 558 of 2026 (HB 609): health care providers must waive fees for health records a veteran requests for a VA or LDVA disability claim, one time per facility, eff. Aug. 1, 2026 (legis.la.gov BillInfo 26RS HB609: "Signed by the Governor - Act 558"; "Effective date: 08/01/2026").
- No state veteran bonus exists.
