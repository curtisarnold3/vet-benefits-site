Research date: 2026-10-17. Official agency: Hawaii Office of Veterans' Services (OVS), State Department of Defense, https://dod.hawaii.gov/ovs/.

Sourcing note: Statutes were read from the Legislature's data mirror (data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/...), because www.capitol.hawaii.gov is behind Cloudflare and blocked every fetch. ## 1. Property tax

Hawaii has no state property tax and no state-law veteran exemption. Each of the four counties sets its own rule under its own code. OVS's Benefits page only says the exemption covers a home "owned and occupied by a totally disabled Veteran or their widow(er)" and that the amount "varies by island," then links to the four county offices. None of the four county rules names TDIU or "permanent and total."

### 1a. City and County of Honolulu: Totally Disabled Veteran's Exemption (ROH § 8-10.5)
- Source: https://realproperty.honolulu.gov/tax-relief-and-forms/exemptions/totally-disabled-veterans/ (page © 2026; forms E-8-10.5 "rev-08_2026" and E-8-10.5A "rev-july-2026"); RPAD Information Guide rev. 2026-06-26, https://realproperty.honolulu.gov/media/a0onfffn/information-guide-rev-2026-06-26-85x14-v2.pdf
- Threshold language (guide): "Exemption for real property owned & occupied by a veteran who is 100% totally disabled due to service related injuries while on active duty with U.S. Armed Forces, and includes widow(er) of a totally disabled veteran who remains unmarried and continues to own and occupy the home." Web page: "If you are a totally disabled veteran due to injuries sustained while on active duty with the U.S. Armed Forces," the home "may be eligible for an exemption from real property taxes, except for the minimum tax."
- TDIU / 100% schedular / 100% P&T: the county says "100% totally disabled." No permanence requirement appears. TDIU is not mentioned. A 100% schedular rating matches the words; whether a TDIU award (less than 100% schedular but paid at the 100% rate) counts is not stated. Flagged.
- Amount: full exemption "subject to the minimum real property tax (currently $300), per ROH § 8-11.1(g)."
- Deadline: "There is NO annual filing deadline, and the exemption will take effect beginning with the next tax payment date provided the claim for exemption is filed on or before the first tax payment (Aug. 19), the second tax payment (Feb. 19)." (Older county materials said September 30 on Form P-6; that is no longer the rule. The general home exemption deadline is still September 30.)
- Office / forms: Real Property Assessment Division (Downtown or Kapolei); Form BFS-RP-E-8-10.5 "Claim for Exemption Totally Disabled Veteran" with "a copy of a physician's certificate of disability"; continuance form E-8-10.5A.
- Surviving spouse: continues while the "widow or widower of the totally disabled veteran remains unmarried" and "continues to own and occupy the home."
- Residency / ownership: owner occupied; no income cap.
- County variation: Honolulu only.
- 2025-2026 changes: none found to the exemption itself; forms revised July/August 2026.

### 1b. Maui County: Severely Disabled Veteran exemption (MCC § 3.48.475) and Totally Disabled Veteran exemption
- Source: County form "Claim for Disability Exemption, Severely Disabled Veteran," https://www.mauicounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/102703/Disability-Exemption-for-Disabled-Veterans (403 to all fetches; content from search snippets of the form); Maui County Council press release July 13, 2022, https://mauicounty.us/budget-finance/press-release-council-to-hear-homeowner-exemption-bills-to-promote-tax-equity/
- Threshold language (form, quoting MCC 3.48.475): "'Severely disabled' means the individual has been given a 70 percent or higher disability rating by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs." Form: "Deadline for Filing: DECEMBER 31, 2026"; "the County of Maui, Real Property Assessment Division will verify the total service connected disability with the Veterans Administration."
- Amount (council press release, Bill 110, CD1 (2022)): the bill "would allow severely disabled veterans to pay just 50% of the otherwise applicable minimum real property tax," which "is $350 for the current fiscal year." The mayor signed the bill in October 2022 (news reports). I could not read the enacted ordinance, so the current minimum tax figure is unconfirmed.
- TDIU / 100%: at 70 percent or higher, a TDIU veteran rated 70 to 90 percent qualifies on the schedular rating alone. The older "totally disabled veteran" exemption (news reports said it required 100 percent) could not be read on an official page.
- Surviving spouse: not confirmed (form blocked).
- Office: Real Property Assessment Division, (808) 270-7297, RPA@co.maui.hi.us; forms at mauipropertytax.com (redirects to qpublic.net/hi/maui).
- 2025-2026 changes: none found.

### 1c. Hawaii County: Totally Disabled Veteran exemption (Hawaii County Code ch. 19; Form 19-73)
- Source: https://hawaiipropertytax.com/exemptions-programs/ (official county site, .com; 403/Cloudflare to all fetches; content from search snippets of that page and the county's 2025 Disability Exemption Brochure, https://hawaiipropertytax.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/114/2025/05/Disability-Exemption-Brochure.pdf)
- Threshold language (snippets of county page): a totally disabled veteran whose disability stems from active-duty injuries can have the principal home exempted from property tax "except for special assessments and the annual minimum tax"; "Both disability and veterans disability programs require 100% disability and are only available if you qualify and have submitted a homeowner application." County public notices 2022-2024: exempt "except for fifty percent of the minimum tax." County: "The minimum tax is $200 with very few exceptions."
- Deadline: "on or before June 30 for the first half payment or December 31 for the second half payment."
- Surviving spouse: exemption "remains valid as long as the veteran stays totally disabled, or the surviving spouse stays unmarried."
- Commercial use: "Any portion of the home used for commercial purposes is not entitled to the exemption."
- TDIU: not addressed. A 2018 county bill (Bill 165) to define the class as "100 percent disabled or 100 percent unemployable or both" was sent back for revision; I could not confirm its outcome. Flagged.
- Office: Real Property Tax Division, Hilo (808) 961-8201, Kona (808) 323-4880.
- 2025-2026 changes: none found.

### 1d. Kauai County: Disabled Veteran exemption (Kauai County Code § 5A-11.4 home exemption prerequisite)
- Source: https://www.kauai.gov/Government/Departments-Agencies/Finance/Real-Property-Tax/Assessment/ExemptionTax-Relief-Information (© 2026); "Understanding Your Real Property Taxes," Rev 1/22/2026, https://www.kauai.gov/files/assets/public/v/2/finance/rpa/2027-tax-relief-exemption-forms/understanding-rptaxes_1.22.2026.1aa.pdf
- Threshold language (PDF): "Real Property for which a home exemption under Section 5A-11.4 has been established by a veteran who is 80% to totally disabled due to injuries sustained while on duty as a member of the United States Armed Forces shall be exempt from all property tax (with the exclusion of multi-unit properties) except the minimum tax of $150. Qualified Additional Income Exemption filings are eligible for the discounted minimum tax of $75. The tax credit will be valid if the veteran claiming the exemption remains totally disabled. Following the death of the disabled veteran, the surviving spouse, civil union partner, or reciprocal beneficiary may continue to receive this tax credit on the real property for which a home exemption has been established."
- Lower tier (PDF): "Any person who is a disabled Veteran and is less than 80% disabled due to injuries received while on duty as a member of the United States Armed Forces shall, so long as he or she is disabled, be exempt of up to $50,000" (in addition to the home exemption).
- TDIU / 100%: the 80 percent floor means most TDIU veterans qualify on the schedular rating; "totally disabled" is not defined further.
- Deadline / forms (web page): "Veterans file RP Form P-6 and Claim for Home Exemption (P-3)" "on or before SEPTEMBER 30th" before the tax year; an approved homeowner exemption is required; exemptions are not automatic; report changes within 30 days.
- Surviving spouse (web page): valid while "the widow or widower of the disabled veteran remains unmarried" and "resides on the subject property as their principal residence."
- 2025-2026 changes: none found (minimum tax has risen over the years: $25 in 2012, $100 in 2017, $150 now).

## 2. Vehicles, driver license, parking

### 2a. State registration fee exemption, 100% service-connected (HRS § 249-31.5)
- Source: https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol04_Ch0201-0257/HRS0249/HRS_0249-0031_0005.htm (L 2015, c 128; am L 2019, c 188); OVS Benefits and Services page https://dod.hawaii.gov/ovs/benefits-and-services/ (© 2022, no update date)
- Threshold language: "A disabled veteran who: (1) Is a resident of Hawaii; (2) Has been other than dishonorably discharged from the United States uniformed armed forces; and (3) Is determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs or its predecessor to have a service-connected one hundred per cent disability rating for compensation or a service-connected disability rating of one hundred per cent, shall be exempt from payment of all annual vehicle registration fees as required by section 249-31; provided that this exemption shall not extend to any vehicle used for commercial purposes or to more than one vehicle owned by the disabled veteran."
- Amount: the $46 state fee (HRS 249-31). OVS: "$46.00 state registration fee exemption only. The veteran still pays the remaining balance." OVS requires a new original letter each year, mailed to the veteran; "the DMV will not accept a prior year's letter"; vehicle must have current registration and safety check. OVS cites "Act 188 HB601" (2019 amendment).
- P&T not required; TDIU: the statute says "rating for compensation," which a TDIU award at the 100 percent rate arguably meets, but neither the statute nor OVS says so. Not flagged because the 100 percent language is explicit.
- 2025-2026: HB 657 (2025) HD1 and SB 607 (2025, 2026) would have repealed this fee exemption and instead exempted 100 percent disabled veterans from the vehicle weight tax. HB 657 reached only HD1 (no HD2 or SD1 exists on the data server); SB 607 2025 died and the 2026 version was re-referred to committee on Jan. 21, 2026 with no SD1 produced. HRS 249-31.5 shows no 2025 or 2026 amendment. Not enacted.

### 2b. County weight tax exemption, VA-furnished car (HRS § 249-6)
- Source: https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol04_Ch0201-0257/HRS0249/HRS_0249-0006.htm
- "Passenger cars owned by persons who, by reason of their service disability, have been furnished such cars by the federal government and have been so certified by the Department of Veterans Affairs, shall be exempted from the tax herein provided for. This exemption shall not extend to vehicles used for commercial purposes, nor to more than one vehicle of any disabled veteran."
- There is no general weight tax exemption for 100 percent disabled veterans (see 2a). HRS 249-6.5 exempts one vehicle of Guard/reserve/active members assigned to a Hawaii unit (not a veteran benefit).

### 2c. Special number plates for military service (HRS § 249-9.2)
- Source: https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol04_Ch0201-0257/HRS0249/HRS_0249-0009_0002.htm (am L 2024, c 83)
- "the director of finance shall provide, for a fee, one set of special number plates" on proof of Purple Heart, veteran status, Pearl Harbor survivor, former POW, VA or OVS certification as a combat veteran or veteran of Vietnam, Korea, WWII, Persian Gulf, Iraq or Afghanistan, or Gold Star family eligibility; applicants (other than civilians/current members in some categories) "shall also provide a copy of the applicant's most recent discharge paper or separation document that indicates an honorable discharge or general (under honorable conditions) discharge." Designs: "COMBAT WOUNDED," "VETERAN," "GOLD STAR FAMILY," etc. Plates "shall not be transferable"; survivor "may retain the front special number plate as a memorial."
- OVS: cost is "same as a regular license plate"; motorcycles available. No free or discounted disabled veteran plate exists in Hawaii.

### 2d. Honolulu county registration fee exemption, veterans 65+ (Bill 62 (2024), CD2; effective July 1, 2026)
- Source: https://www.honolulu.gov/csd/veterans/ (© 2026); OVS news item https://dod.hawaii.gov/ovs/news/city-county-of-honolulu-introduces-a-new-exemption-for-veterans-65-and-older/
- "Beginning on July 1, 2026, veterans of the U.S. armed forces 65 and older who were not dishonorably discharged may receive an exemption" from the "$20 City and County of Honolulu motor vehicle registration fee for one non-commercial vehicle annually." Not waived: "$46 state registration fee; state weight tax; county weight tax; $7 highway beautification fee; 50 cent emblem and $5 initial plate fee." Apply once in person: "Schedule an AlohaQ appointment and appear in person at a satellite city hall" with form CS-L(MVR)50B and proof of veteran status, age and ownership. Council passed October 2025; mayor signed November 2025. OVS pages cite the ordinance as 25-45 in one place and 25-65 in another; not resolved.

### 2e. Driver license, parking placard
- No fee waiver for disabled veterans was found in HRS chapter 286 or on OVS. Disabled parking placards follow the general disability rules (HRS 291-51 et seq.), not researched further. Not included as a row.

## 3. Hunting, fishing, parks
- Hunting (HRS § 183D-22, https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol03_Ch0121-0200D/HRS0183D/HRS_0183D-0022.htm): fee is "$10 for any person who has resided in the State for one year or longer, or who is a member of the armed forces of the United States on active duty and the spouse and children thereof"; "$95 for all other persons"; "Free to all Hawaii residents sixty-five years of age or older and to all persons with Hansen's disease who are residents of Kalaupapa, Molokai." No veteran or disability provision. DLNR Game Mammal Hunting page (https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/recreation/hunting/mammal/, © 2026): "Resident hunting licenses cost $10.00, and non-resident licenses are $95.00"; "A current-year, $10.00 Hawai‘i Wildlife Conservation Stamp is also required"; hunter education required except persons born before Jan. 1, 1972 who held a Hawaii license before July 1, 1990; "A person is considered a resident of Hawai‘i if they have resided in the State of Hawai‘i for at least one year." A 2014 bill (HB 1907) for a $3 disabled veteran license did not become law.
- Freshwater fishing (HRS § 188-50; DLNR DAR page https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/dar/licenses-and-permits/freshwater-game-fishing-license/, © 2026): "Resident and military 16 yrs and over $5"; "minors 9-15 yrs $3"; "non-resident $25"; "7-day tourist $10; 30-day tourist $20"; "seniors 65 yrs and over free." No disability or veteran discount.
- Ocean fishing (HRS § 188-72, L 2021, c 48, https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol03_Ch0121-0200D/HRS0188/HRS_0188-0072.htm): license required only of "any nonresident of the State who has attained the age of fifteen, except members of the armed forces of the United States on active duty in the State and their spouse and minor children." Fees $20 one day, $40 seven days, $70 annual. Residents need no license. Rule HAR 13-74-11 effective May 2, 2024 (DLNR DAR release).
- State parks (DLNR DSP Haena page https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/dsp/?p=212; DLNR release Oct. 7, 2020, https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/?p=20807): "Residents = Free with Hawaii DL or ID" for entry and parking; "Non-residents = $5 per person" and "$10 Per Vehicle." Release: "Parking and entrance fees to all State Parks for Hawaiʻi residents are free"; "Residents will now pay $20/night for tent campsites, while the non-resident rate is set at $30/night." No veteran or disability pass exists.

## 4. Education and tuition
- UH Board of Regents Policy RP 6.208, Board Exemptions to Non-Resident Tuition, Effective Date August 15, 2024 (https://www.hawaii.edu/policy/docs/temp/rp6.208.pdf). Exempt from non-resident tuition: "6. Veterans eligible to use Post 9/11 GI Bill or Montgomery GI Bill Active Duty Program educational benefits per the Isakson and Roe Veterans Health Care and Benefits Improvement Act of 2020 (P.L.116-315), who live in Hawai‘i and those who subsequently move but maintain continuous enrollment." Also item 7 (transferred Post-9/11), 8 (Fry Scholarship), 9 (Survivors' or Dependents' Educational Assistance), and "10. Veterans with service-connected disabilities who are eligible for benefits provided for in Title 38, U.S. Code, Chapter 31, otherwise known as the Veteran Readiness and Employment or VR&E ... program, who live in Hawai‘i and those who subsequently move but maintain continuous enrollment."
- UH Student Affairs veterans page (https://www.hawaii.edu/offices/student-affairs/veterans-military-connected/, last modified Sept. 11, 2026): "covered individuals" under the Choice Act or using VA educational assistance "may be eligible for an exemption from non-resident tuition"; contact the campus Residency Officer. Executive Policy 6.205 reimburses tuition for deployed active duty, Guard and Reserve members (not a veteran benefit).
- No state tuition waiver or scholarship exists for veterans or for dependents of disabled or deceased veterans. SB 951 (2021) and HB 426 (2021-2022), which would have waived UH tuition for financially needy dependent children of disabled veterans, KIA members and Purple Heart recipients, did not pass. A Hawaii National Guard state tuition assistance program exists for current Guard members only (not researched further).

## 5. State income tax
- HRS § 235-7(a) (https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol04_Ch0201-0257/HRS0235/HRS_0235-0007.htm): excluded from gross income: "(1) Income not subject to taxation by the State under the Constitution and laws of the United States; ... (3) Any compensation received in the form of a pension for past services; ... (7) Income received by each member of the reserve components ... and the Hawaii National Guard as compensation for performance of duty, equivalent to pay received for forty-eight drills (equivalent of twelve weekends) and fifteen days of annual duty, at an ... (E) E-5 pay grade after eight years of service."
- 2025 Form N-11 Instructions (https://files.hawaii.gov/tax/forms/2025/n11ins.pdf), Pensions: "Hawaii does not tax qualifying distributions from an employer-funded pension plan. If you received qualifying distributions from an employer-funded profit sharing, defined contribution, or defined benefit plan, or from a government retirement system (e.g., federal civil service, military pension, state or county retirement system), enter the qualifying amount here." "For a distribution to qualify, it must be paid by a pension plan by reason of retirement, disability, or death." National Guard Duty Pay Exclusion: "Hawaii does not tax the first $8,636 received by each member of the reserve components of the army, navy, air force, marine corps, coast guard of the United States of America, and the Hawaii national guard, as compensation for performance of duty as such."
- VA disability compensation and DIC are excluded from federal gross income and so fall under 235-7(a)(1). SBP annuities: the N-11 text covers government retirement system payments "by reason of ... death"; no Hawaii document naming SBP was found. HB 1572 (2024), which would have limited the pension exclusion to Hawaii-earned pensions, did not become law (235-7 unchanged). No 2025-2026 change.

## 6. Employment preference
- HRS § 76-103 (https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol02_Ch0046-0115/HRS0076/HRS_0076-0103.htm): "The extent to which veteran's preference shall be given to veterans, to disabled veterans, to spouses of disabled veterans, and to surviving spouses of deceased servicemen who have not remarried shall be provided by rules and regulations."
- DHRD "Important Information" for job announcements (https://dhrd.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/Important-Information-for-job-announcement_MASTER.pdf; a 2019 DOT version says the same): "VETERANS PREFERENCE POINTS: (Open Competitive Recruitments Only). To receive 5 Veterans Preference Points, an applicant must submit a copy of the DD214 (Member 4) verifying dates of honorable service. To receive 10 Veterans Preference Points, submit a copy of an official statement/letter from the U.S. Department of Veteran Affairs or armed service dated within the past 12 months which confirms your qualification to receive 10 points preference."
- The disability percentage for 10 points and spouse/surviving spouse rules live in DHRD administrative rules that I could not locate (DHRD page URLs guessed returned 404). Maui County's FAQ describes its own 5/10 point system (10 points for a service-connected disability or VA compensation, certain spouses and mothers). Flagged.
- No business or professional license fee waiver for veterans was found.

## 7. Other (homes, cemeteries, grants, bonuses)
- Special housing grant: HRS § 363-11 (https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol07_Ch0346-0398/HRS0363/HRS_0363-0011.htm): "The office shall develop rules to specify the amount to be provided a veteran for the purpose of bearing the cost not borne by the federal government for a specially designed home for disabled veterans. The veteran must have been a bona fide resident of the State before entering active service with the armed forces and must qualify for a federal grant under the Department of Veterans Affairs' Specially Adapted Housing program. In no event shall the State pay a qualified veteran a share greater than the federal government toward the purchase or remodeling of such home." HAR § 7-31-4.1 (https://dod.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/chapter-31.pdf, eff. Feb. 9, 1989): "the office shall provide a grant not to exceed $5,000 to remodel or purchase a home for a disabled veteran"; "contingent on the availability of funds"; veteran "shall be physically residing in the state at the time the federal grant is approved as well as at the time the application for the grant and receipt of the State grant are made"; home "shall be in the State"; disbursed "to the veteran in care of the United States Department of Veterans Affairs regional office to be placed in escrow"; "The special housing grant shall be made to a veteran only once." (The leads in the prompt cited HRS 363-3.5; that section is actually the advisory board. The grant is 363-11.)
- Cemeteries: OVS "Arranging for Burial in the Hawaii State Veterans Cemetery (Kaneohe)" (https://dod.hawaii.gov/ovs/veterans-cemetery/arranging-for-burial-in-the-hawaii-state-veterans-cemetery-kaneohe/, revised 2/12/2024): burial is "an honor reserved for veterans of the U.S. Armed Forces and their eligible dependents"; spouses and children "under 21 years of age" or "under 23 years of age if pursuing a course of instruction," and unmarried adult children "physically or mentally disabled and incapable of self-support before reaching the age of 21 years." State provides gravesite, marker, opening/closing, perpetual care, and a liner "subject to change upon availability of funds"; "There is no charge for a Memorial Marker"; VA "will pay the plot/interment allowance fee"; family "must pay for Mortuary services"; "Gravesites ... cannot be reserved in advance"; "Fees are subject to change without notification." HAR ch. 7-32 says the state collects the plot-allowance equivalent plus liner cost for dependents (search snippet; not read directly). OVS Veteran Cemeteries page lists county-run cemeteries in Hilo, Kona, Makawao (Maui), Hoolehua (Molokai), Lanai City and Hanapepe (Kauai).
- State veterans homes: OVS State Veteran Homes page (https://dod.hawaii.gov/ovs/state-veteran-homes/) names the Yukio Okutsu State Veterans Home, Hilo, (808) 961-1500, and the Daniel K. Akaka State Veterans Home, Kapolei (online interest form), with no eligibility text. A search snippet of an older OVS page said residents must be veterans in good standing meeting general VA eligibility who were Hawaii residents before entering the military or lived in Hawaii more than a year before applying; that page (dod.hawaii.gov/ovs/?p=138) now returns 404. Flagged.
- OVS also provides free certified copies of the DD-214 if on file, VA claims help, and service-credit validation for the state Employees' Retirement System (not rows).
- No state veterans bonus or emergency grant program was found.

## 8. 2025-2026 law changes checked
- HB 657 (2025) and SB 607 (2025 and 2026): exempt 100 percent disabled veterans from the vehicle weight tax and repeal the $46 fee exemption. Not enacted (no HD2/SD1 drafts exist; SB 607 re-referred to TRS/PSM, WAM on Jan. 21, 2026; HRS 249-31.5 unchanged).
- HB 574 (2025): $3,000 income tax credit for retired Hawaii National Guard members; carried over, not enacted.
- Act 91 (2025) amended HRS 249-9.3 (organizational special plates), not a veteran benefit.
- Honolulu Bill 62 (2024), CD2: county $20 registration fee exemption for veterans 65+, signed November 2025, effective July 1, 2026.
- Honolulu dropped the September 30 deadline for the totally disabled veteran exemption (reflected in the June 2026 guide).
- Hawaii's 2025 N-11 raised the reserve/Guard pay exclusion to $8,636 (indexed to E-5 pay).
- No 2025 or 2026 state law changed veteran property tax, income tax or tuition rules.
