## 1. Property tax (filed January 1 to April 15 with the county assessor or online through the Tax Commission TAP portal)

Idaho's veteran property tax benefits are set by state statute and are uniform statewide. The county assessor takes paper applications and verifies ownership and occupancy; the State Tax Commission runs the program and pays the counties. Only the home-value cap for the circuit breaker varies by county (it is tied to the county median).

### 1a. Special property tax reduction for disabled veterans, $1,500 (Idaho Code 63-705A)
- Source: https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title63/T63CH7/SECT63-705A/ (history: added 2018, ch. 183; am. 2019, 2020, 2021 x2, 2023 ch. 137). Tax Commission program page: https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/property/homeowners/veteran-benefit/ ("last updated: September 22, 2026"). Press release March 25, 2026: https://tax.idaho.gov/pressrelease/veterans-with-disabilities-can-apply-for-property-tax-benefit-2/
- Threshold language (63-705A(1)): "For tax year 2021 and thereafter, regardless of any reduction received under section 63-705, Idaho Code, a veteran with a service-connected disability of one hundred percent (100%) or a disability rating based on individual unemployability rating that is compensated at the one hundred percent (100%) disability rate, as certified by the United States department of veterans affairs, shall receive a special reduction in property taxes or occupancy taxes levied on his homestead, as defined in section 63-701, Idaho Code."
- Amount: "The special tax reduction shall be in the amount of one thousand five hundred dollars ($1,500) or for the amount of the veteran's actual property taxes or occupancy taxes, as applicable, whichever is less. If a veteran qualifies for tax reduction under both this section and section 63-705, Idaho Code, the combined tax reduction amount may not exceed the actual amount of the veteran's property taxes or occupancy taxes on his homestead."
- TDIU / 100% schedular / 100% P&T: all three qualify for the money. The statute names "one hundred percent (100%)" with no permanence requirement, and separately names "individual unemployability ... compensated at the one hundred percent (100%) disability rate." The Tax Commission page says you must be "recognized as a veteran with a 100% service-connected disability" or receive "100% compensation due to individual unemployability" as of January 1. "Permanent and total" appears only in subsection (4), which governs reapplication: "An applicant who is a veteran with a one hundred percent (100%) service-connected permanent and total disability, having once applied for and received the reduction under this section, shall be entitled to continue receiving the benefit in subsequent years without applying annually, unless the veteran changes homesteads." Tax Commission: "If your disability is permanent and total, you don't need to reapply each year. The benefit will renew automatically." Everyone else reapplies annually with a current VA letter.
- Deadline, office, forms: 63-705A(2) adopts the circuit-breaker procedures of 63-701 to 63-710 "with the exception of any income documentation." Tax Commission: "You must apply between January 1 and April 15, 2027, for a reduction to 2027 property taxes." Apply online (TAP portal, idahotap.gentax.com/TAP?Link=PTR) or mail the paper form to the county assessor; Tax Commission phone (208) 334-7736. "The Disabled Veterans benefit doesn't have an income limit." "The property must have a current homeowner's exemption." Mobile homes qualify. "This program won't reduce solid waste, irrigation, or other fees that government entities charge."
- Mid-year move (63-705A(5)): if the veteran was granted the benefit before April 15, then "after April 15 but before October 1" moves to another qualifying homestead and "notifies the state tax commission before October 1," the Tax Commission must have the counties transfer the benefit.
- Surviving spouse (63-705A(3)): "In the event that a qualified veteran applies for the special tax reduction in this section but then dies, the veteran's surviving spouse is entitled to receive the special tax reduction in that year and subsequent years, until such time as the surviving spouse remarries, dies, or no longer has property tax levied on the homestead." Tax Commission: "a surviving spouse can use this benefit" but "the benefit isn't transferable to a new property after the death of the veteran." Nothing requires the veteran to have been P&T for the spouse carryover; the veteran must have applied before dying.
- Residency / ownership: "homestead" (63-701(2)) is "the dwelling, owner-occupied by the claimant ... and used as the primary dwelling place of the claimant ... and so much of the land surrounding it, not exceeding one (1) acre"; "a manufactured home may be a homestead." Claimant must be an owner and occupy "as of January 1 or before April 15" (63-701(1), 63-703(4)). No minimum length of Idaho residency.
- County variation: none; statewide amount.
- 2025-2026 changes: none. Last substantive amendment was 2023, ch. 137 (House Bill 258, signed March 24, 2023). The 2020 amendment (House Bill 552, ch. 246) added the individual unemployability language; the amount went from $1,320 to $1,500 for tax year 2021 (2021 ch. 68/360). The IDVS state benefits page still says "$1,320" and is out of date on this point. I scanned every 2025 and 2026 bill title for veteran/property-tax items; none touched 63-705A.

### 1b. Property Tax Reduction (circuit breaker) for veterans 10% or more (Idaho Code 63-701, 63-705)
- Sources: https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title63/T63CH7/SECT63-701/ (history ends "am. 2025, ch. 122, sec. 41; am. 2025, ch. 208, sec. 22"); https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title63/T63CH7/SECT63-705/ (last am. 2023, ch. 200); Tax Commission page https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/property/homeowners/reduction/ ("last updated: September 23, 2026").
- Threshold language (63-701(1)(e)): claimant must on January 1 be "A disabled veteran of any war engaged in by the United States, whose disability is recognized as a service-connected disability of a degree of ten percent (10%) or more, or who has a pension for nonservice-connected disabilities, in accordance with laws and regulations administered by the United States department of veterans affairs." Other claimant classes: age 65+, "A widow or widower," SSA/RRB/OMB-recognized disabled person, former POW/hostage, blind, fatherless/motherless child under 18. Note the statute says "of any war"; the Tax Commission web page's short list does not restate the veteran category, so expect the assessor to apply the statute.
- Amount (63-705): "a maximum reduction of one thousand five hundred dollars ($1,500)" at the lowest income bracket down to "two hundred fifty dollars ($250)" at the top bracket. Tax Commission: "$250 to $1,500."
- Income cap: statute sets the greater of "$37,000 per household for tax year 2023 and each tax year thereafter" or "185% of the federal poverty guidelines for a household of two." Tax Commission for 2027 taxes: "Your total 2026 income, after deducting medical expenses, was $40,030 or less." (A county-hosted Tax Commission brochure gave $39,130 of 2025 income for 2026 taxes; not confirmed on tax.idaho.gov.) Income definition (63-701(5)) includes "veterans disability pensions and compensation," BUT "'Income' does not include veterans disability pensions received by a person described in subsection (1)(e) ... if the disability pension is received pursuant to a service-connected disability of a degree of forty percent (40%) or more," and "'Income' does not include dependency and indemnity compensation or death benefits paid ... by the United States department of veterans affairs." Medical expenses and up to $5,000 of funeral expenses are deductible.
- Home value cap (63-705): homes assessed above "the greater of four hundred thousand dollars ($400,000) or two hundred percent (200%)" of the county median homestead value are not eligible and are referred to the deferral program. This is the only county-varying element.
- Deadline/office: apply "between January 1 and April 15" each year, online (TAP) or on paper with the county assessor; must have the homeowner's exemption (63-602G) and occupy as primary residence by April 15.
- Surviving spouse: a widow or widower is a claimant in his or her own right (63-701(1)(c)).
- 2025-2026 changes: 63-701 was amended twice in 2025 (ch. 122 sec. 41 and ch. 208 sec. 22). I did not read those session laws; they appear to be large multi-section bills (section numbers 41 and 22) and the current text above is post-amendment. No 2026 change.

### 1c. Surviving spouse summary
- $1,500 veteran reduction: carries to the surviving spouse on the same homestead until remarriage, death, or no tax levied; not transferable to a new home (1a above).
- Circuit breaker: any widow or widower qualifies subject to income and value caps (1b).
- No Idaho law gives a surviving spouse of a veteran killed on active duty a separate property tax exemption. None found.

### 1d. Not available in Idaho
- No full homestead exemption for 100% or P&T veterans. The $1,500 reduction is the ceiling.
- No specially adapted housing (SAH) exemption in the property tax code. None found in Title 63, chapters 6 or 7.
- No partial-exemption tiers by rating other than the 10% circuit-breaker gateway.

## 2. Vehicles, driver license, parking

### 2a. Disabled Veteran "D.V." plate and registration fee waiver (Idaho Code 49-403; parking under 49-410(7))
- Source: https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title49/T49CH4/SECT49-403/ (history ends "am. 2023, ch. 16"); 49-410: https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title49/T49CH4/SECT49-410/ (last am. 2023, ch. 16); ITD Form 3397 (Rev. 7/24): https://itd.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/3397.pdf; IDVS plate page https://veterans.idaho.gov/idaho-military-license-plate-applications/ (updated April 28, 2026).
- Threshold language: "No fee shall be charged for the registration or reregistration of a motor vehicle owned by a veteran, who is at the time of the registration or reregistration receiving compensation from the veterans administration or in lieu thereof, from any of the armed forces of the United States, for one hundred percent (100%) service-connected disability or for any of the following specific disabilities: Loss or permanent loss of use of one (1) or both feet; loss or permanent loss of use of one (1) or both hands; loss of sight in both eyes or permanent impairment of vision in both eyes to the degree as to constitute virtual blindness." Also covers veterans "who has established his rights to benefits under the provisions of Public Law 662, 79th Congress, as amended, and Public Law 187, 82nd Congress" (the VA automobile grant laws).
- Limits: "the privilege shall not extend to more than one (1) vehicle at a time"; excludes vehicles over 26,000 pounds GVW and 49-434(5) vehicles. "These license plates shall not be issued by the counties but shall be issued by the department."
- TDIU / P&T: the statute requires compensation "for one hundred percent (100%) service-connected disability"; it does not say permanent and does not mention individual unemployability. ITD Form 3397 says "Applicants must be a veteran with a 100% service-connected permanent disability" and "Applicant must provide a 100% rating letter from Veteran Affairs." The form's "permanent" wording is stricter than the statute. Flagged needs_verification for TDIU and non-permanent 100% veterans.
- Fees: Form 3397: "Plate fees and registration do not apply. Applicants are limited to one set of Disabled Veteran/Service-Connected Disabled Purple Heart plates." "Do not send fees with the application."
- Parking: 49-403: "A vehicle displaying plates issued in accordance with the provisions of this section shall be afforded the same privileges specified in section 49-410(7), Idaho Code. Together with a license plate issued pursuant to this section the department shall issue a special placard pursuant to section 49-410(4)." 49-410(7): may "park for unlimited periods of time in parking zones or areas that are otherwise restricted," "park in any public parking space with metered parking without being required to pay any parking meter fee," and "park in spaces and zones designated for persons with a disability," except where all parking is prohibited or limited to under 48 hours.
- Apply: mail ITD Special Plates, PO Box 7129, Boise ID 83707-1129, or specialplates@itd.idaho.gov. Form notes: "Contact Idaho Parks and Recreation on applying for a free Idaho Parks Passport."
- 2025-2026 changes: none (last amendment 2023 ch. 16).

### 2b. Purple Heart plates (49-403A), Medal of Honor plates (49-415A)
- Sources: https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title49/T49CH4/SECT49-403A/ ; https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title49/T49CH4/SECT49-415A/
- Purple Heart: "The applicant shall be charged the regular registration fee but shall not be charged the plate fee required in section 49-450." Multiple vehicles allowed. Surviving spouse may keep plates and may reapply within 5 years of the death. Subsection (6): "A purple heart recipient who qualifies for a disabled veteran fee exemption under section 49-403, Idaho Code, shall have a choice between a purple heart recipient license plate or a disabled veteran license plate and shall not be charged a plate fee or a fee for registration or reregistration of the motor vehicle. ... The privilege shall not extend to more than one (1) vehicle owned by the veteran at a time." ITD Form 3397 adds that a second set of disabled Purple Heart plates pays registration. Since January 1, 2015 the accessible symbol can be added for recipients who qualify under 49-410.
- Medal of Honor: "The license plates shall be provided free of charge. The applicant shall pay the regular annual registration fees." Up to two vehicles; proof by VA certification; surviving spouse may keep or reapply within 5 years. IDVS: contact ITD Special Plates (208) 334-8649.

### 2c. Driver license: no fee waiver; free VETERAN designation at renewal
- Source: ITD flyer "Idaho's Veteran Designation" (Revised 11-16-23): https://itd.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Veterans_Designation.pdf
- "There will be no additional charge to add the veteran designation when obtaining a new or renewed Idaho driver's license or identification card. There will be a duplicate fee to add a veteran designation at any other time." "The designation is permanent." Acceptable documents include DD-214 copy 4, NGB-22, VA Verification of Service Letter, "100% Disabled Veteran Card or Letter," VA Veteran ID card, and proof of a veteran license plate.
- No Idaho statute or ITD page waives driver license or ID card fees for disabled veterans. None found.

### 2d. Sales tax, excise tax, placards
- No Idaho sales tax exemption for vehicles bought by disabled veterans was found in Title 63 chapter 36 or on tax.idaho.gov. Treated as not available (not exhaustively searched; see Could not verify).
- The disability placard comes automatically with the DV plate (49-403, 49-410(4)(e)).

## 4. Education and tuition

### 4a. Armed Forces and Public Safety Officer Dependent Scholarship (Idaho Code 33-4302)
- Source: https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title33/T33CH43/SECT33-4302/ (history ends "am. 2025, ch. 94, sec. 22")
- Threshold language: eligible are a spouse or child of an Idaho resident who entered active service as an Idaho resident (per the DD-214) and who "Has been determined by the federal government to be a prisoner of war or missing in action" or "Died of, or is determined to be unemployable due to, injuries or wounds sustained during active duty or inactive duty training." Subsection (7): unemployable means the VA has found individual unemployability or the SSA has found total and permanent disability from service injuries. A parallel category covers service members stationed in Idaho on orders. Public safety officers killed or totally and permanently disabled in the line of duty are also covered.
- TDIU / 100% / P&T: the statute keys on unemployability (VA IU) or death/POW/MIA. A 100% schedular or P&T rating that is not IU does not match the text. Flagged.
- Benefit: admission to undergraduate study at a public Idaho college or career technical college "without the necessity of paying tuition and fees therefor"; books, equipment and supplies "not to exceed seven hundred fifty dollars ($750)" per academic period; on-campus housing and subsistence for months of full-time enrollment if living on campus; total "shall not exceed a total of thirty-six (36) months or four (4) nine (9) month periods."
- Residency/age: child must be an Idaho resident who completed secondary school in Idaho; spouse must be an Idaho resident married at the time of the event (and still married in disability cases). No age cap; benefits run "for a period of nineteen (19) years after achieving a high school diploma" or "ten (10) years after the event," whichever is longer (for benefits starting on or after July 1, 2022).
- Administration: State Board of Education and U of I Regents may adopt rules. I could not find the Board's program page (boardofed.idaho.gov/scholarships and nextsteps.idaho.gov did not show it); apply through the State Board of Education / school financial aid office. Flagged.
- 2025 change: ch. 94 sec. 22 (part of a larger bill; content not reviewed). 2023 HB 219 tied residency to the DD-214.

### 4b. Operation Education (University of Idaho)
- Source: https://www.uidaho.edu/current-students/operation-education/prospective-scholars/eligibility-requirements
- "available to military personnel who sustained permanent disability while serving our nation since Sept. 11, 2001." Dependents (spouses) "may also be eligible." Search-result text from the U of I FAQ adds: honorably separated, disability must impact major life activities, admitted degree-seeking, generally full time, priority to Idaho residents and combat-zone injuries, must apply for VA benefits including Vocational Rehabilitation. Covers tuition, books, fees, housing, transportation, adaptive equipment, medical, child care, tutoring. Contact operationeducation@uidaho.edu, (208) 885-6229. Donor-funded scholarship, not a statutory entitlement.

### 4c. Not available / federal only
- No Idaho statute waives tuition for veterans themselves. In-state tuition for GI Bill users at Idaho public schools flows from federal law (Choice Act / Isakson-Roe sec. 1005); Idaho Code 33-3717B (resident student definition) was not confirmed to contain a veteran clause. Not listed as a state benefit.

## 5. State income tax

### 5a. Military retirement deduction (Idaho Code 63-3022A), 2025 change
- Sources: https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title63/T63CH30/SECT63-3022A/ (history ends "am. 2025, ch. 13, sec. 2; am. 2025, ch. 302, sec. 1"); Tax Commission deduction page https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/income-tax/individual-income/popular-credits-and-deductions/idaho-retirement-benefits-deduction (updated March 31, 2026); 2025 Form 39R instructions EIN00046 (03-02-2026): https://tax.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/forms/EIN00046/EIN00046_03-02-2026.pdf ; Tax Commission release March 11, 2026: https://tax.idaho.gov/?p=218294 ; H0040 bill page: https://legislature.idaho.gov/sessioninfo/2025/legislation/H0040/
- Statute 63-3022A(1)(b): "Retirement benefits paid by the United States of America to a retired member of the military services of the United States, or the unremarried widow or widower of such member, who: (i) Is classified as disabled, as defined in subsection (4) of this section; (ii) Has attained the age of sixty-two (62) years by the end of the tax year; or (iii) Was employed during the tax year and received sufficient income from such employment to be required to file a federal return under section 6012(a)(1) of the Internal Revenue Code."
- "Disabled" (63-3022A(4)): "a disabled person described in section 63-701, Idaho Code, or an individual who qualifies as a person with a 'permanent disability' under section 49-117(7)(b)(iv)" (physician-certified permanent disability). Tax Commission 2025 instructions, page 31: "The following individuals are classified as disabled: An individual recognized as disabled by the Social Security Administration, the Railroad Retirement Board, or the Office of Management and Budget; A veteran of a U.S. war with a service-connected disability rating of 10% or more; A veteran of a U.S. war with a nonservice-connected disability pension; A person who has a physician-certified permanent disability with no expectation of improvement." Instructions, line 8e: "The Idaho Retirement Benefits Deduction for Retired Service Members. You must meet at least one of the following: Classified as disabled (any age), or Age 62 or older, or Under age 62, employed during the year, and received sufficient income from such employment to be required to file a federal return."
- Cap: "The maximum amounts you can deduct for 2025 are: Single $48,216; Married filing jointly $72,324. The retirement benefits you and your spouse received under the federal Social Security Act and the federal Railroad Retirement Act further reduce these maximum amounts. The amount deducted can't be more than the amount of qualified benefits included in federal income." Married filing separately cannot claim it.
- 2025 change: House Bill 40 (2025, ch. 13), long title "Amends existing law to exempt certain precious metals from capital gains tax, lower the income tax rate, and exempt certain military benefits from income taxation," signed March 6, 2025, retroactive to January 1, 2025. House Bill 479 (2025, ch. 302), listed as "Military retirement tax deduction, trailer" and "Corporate income tax, trailer," made follow-up amendments. Tax Commission release of March 11, 2026: "Military retirement pension deduction criteria changed" for "2025 income tax returns"; income tax rate cut "from 5.69% to 5.3%, effective January 1, 2025"; grocery (food) tax credit now "$155 per person." Before 2025 the test was age 65, or age 62 and disabled.
- VA disability compensation: Idaho taxable income starts from federal adjusted gross income (63-3022), which excludes VA compensation, so it is untaxed. No Tax Commission page states this in so many words; IDVS notes veterans 62+ and disabled whose only income is VA disability can still claim the grocery credit.
- SBP/DIC: DIC is federally tax-free and therefore not taxed. SBP annuities to an "unremarried widow or widower" of a military retiree qualify for the deduction under 63-3022A(1)(b).

## 6. Employment preference (Idaho Code 65-501 to 65-513)
- Sources: https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title65/T65CH5/SECT65-504/ (last am. 2014, ch. 26); 65-502 definitions (last am. 2024, ch. 266); 65-503 eligibility (last am. 2020, ch. 44); 65-203 veteran definition (90 days); IDVS employment page https://veterans.idaho.gov/benefits-services/employment-services/ (updated May 4, 2026).
- 65-504(2)(a): "Five (5) points shall be added to the earned rating of any veteran and the widow or widower of any veteran as long as he or she remains unmarried." 65-504(2)(b): "Ten (10) points shall be added to the earned rating of veterans discharged under honorable conditions who qualify as disabled veterans because they have served on active duty in the armed forces at any time and have a current service-connected disability of ten percent (10%) or more. Alternatively, ten (10) points shall be added to the earned rating of the widow or widower of any disabled veteran as long as he or she remains unmarried or the spouse of any eligible disabled veteran who cannot qualify for any public employment because of a service-connected disability." Points are "used only for the purpose of initial appointment and not for the purpose of any promotion, transfer or reassignment."
- 65-504(2)(c): veterans with "a current service-connected disability of thirty percent (30%) or more shall be offered an interview if they are one (1) of the qualified applicants on the register for the position"; employers need not interview more than ten such applicants. (IDVS page phrases it as top 25; the statute controls.)
- 65-502(4): "Disabled veteran" means veterans separated under honorable conditions who "have a current service-connected disability of ten percent (10%) or more" or "Are purple heart recipients." 65-203(1): veteran = active duty and "discharged or separated under honorable conditions after ninety (90) days of continuous active duty," or earlier "because of a service-connected disability."
- Applies to "public employer[s]" statewide (state, county, city, school district). No absolute preference for disabled veterans in Idaho; it is points plus the 30% interview rule. Idaho Code 65-513 permits (does not require) private employers to use a veterans preference.
- Business/professional licenses: Idaho Code 67-2606 and Title 67 ch. 94 give expedited processing and military-training credit; no fee waiver found.
- 2025-2026 changes: none.

### 7b. Idaho State Veterans Cemeteries
- Sources: https://veterans.idaho.gov/cemeteries/ ; https://veterans.idaho.gov/cemetery/idaho-state-veterans-cemetery-boise/
- Boise (10100 N Horseshoe Bend Rd) and Blackfoot are state cemeteries; Snake River Canyon National Cemetery (Buhl) is federal. "Generally, an honorably discharged veteran who served on active duty before September 7, 1980, is eligible"; later service needs "at least 24 months of active duty or a federally ordered deployment." "There is no requirement to be a resident of the state of Idaho." "Interment fees for eligible veterans are paid by the Department of Veteran's Affairs." Guard/reserve not meeting federal rules, and spouses/dependents, pay a fee: "The ISVC will provide the amount, as there are several factors to consider." Pre-authorization is free; send discharge papers and marriage certificate.

### 7c. Emergency financial relief
- Sources: https://veterans.idaho.gov/pressrelease/joining-forces-idaho-grant-application-emergency-assistance-financial-relief/ (posted Feb. 11, 2026; updated May 19, 2026); IDVS state benefits page https://veterans.idaho.gov/benefits-services/state/
- Joining Forces Idaho application: "Applicant must be a resident of Idaho"; show veteran status with "Separation papers (DD214 #4 or similar) showing character of discharge"; need must be "current and NOT ongoing"; examples "electric bill, rent or car repairs"; send to JFIdaho@veterans.idaho.gov. No dollar cap stated.
- IDVS state page (Emergency Relief for Veterans policy IDVS-PO-19-01): "Provides up to a $1,500 grant, in cases of extreme emergency, to Veterans"; "Must have entered the military from Idaho, or lived within the state for at least 5 years"; event within 90 days. The policy PDF returned 404. Flagged.
- Idaho Veterans Support Fund: grants up to $5,000 to organizations/projects serving veterans (not to individuals); funded by a check-off on Form 40 line 38.

### 7d. Not available
- No state veteran bonus, no state housing grant, no state burial allowance beyond the cemeteries. None found on IDVS or in Title 65.
