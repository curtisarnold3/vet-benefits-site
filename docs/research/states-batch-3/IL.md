## 1. Property tax (35 ILCS 200, Article 15; filed with the Chief County Assessment Office; application period set by each county)

### 1a. Standard Homestead Exemption for Veterans with Disabilities (SHEVD) and WWII veterans (35 ILCS 200/15-169)
- Source: https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=003502000K15-169 (Source note: "P.A. 102-136, eff. 7-23-21; 102-895, eff. 5-23-22; 103-154, eff. 6-30-23; 103-596, eff. 7-1-24." No 2025 or 2026 amendment.) Also IDOR PIO-74 (R-07/25), https://tax.illinois.gov/localgovernments/property/taxrelief.html; Cook County Assessor, https://www.cookcountyassessoril.gov/veterans-disabilities-exemption (footer 2026).
- Threshold language (b-3.1), tax year 2023 and after: "(1) if the veteran has a service connected disability of 30% or more but less than 50%, as certified by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs as of the date the application is submitted for the exemption under this Section for the applicable taxable year, then the annual exemption is $2,500; (2) if the veteran has a service connected disability of 50% or more but less than 70% ... then the annual exemption is $5,000; (3) if the veteran has a service connected disability of 70% or more ... then the first $250,000 in equalized assessed value of the property is exempt from taxation under this Code; and (4) if the taxpayer is the surviving spouse of a veteran whose death was determined to be service-connected and who is certified by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs as a recipient of dependency and indemnity compensation under federal law ... then the first $250,000 in equalized assessed value of the property is also exempt."
- (b-4): "For taxable years on or after 2024, if the veteran was a member of the United States Armed Forces during World War II, then the property is exempt from taxation under this Code regardless of the veteran's level of disability."
- The $250,000 figure changed meaning in 2023. Before TY2023 "qualified residence" meant property "with an equalized assessed value of less than $250,000" (homes at or above that EAV got nothing). For TY2023 and after, "qualified residence" is simply "real property, but less any portion of that property that is used for commercial purposes, that is the primary residence of a veteran with a disability." The cap moved into the benefit: the first $250,000 of EAV is exempt and anything above is taxed. The lead's "$250,000 EAV cap" description is the pre-2023 rule.
- TDIU / 100% schedular / 100% P&T: The tiers key on "service connected disability of X% or more ... as certified by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs." TDIU, "individual unemployability," "total" and "permanent" appear nowhere in the tier language. A 100% schedular veteran and a 100% P&T veteran both land in the 70%+ tier. A TDIU veteran whose combined schedular rating is 70% or more also lands there; a TDIU veteran rated under 70% (for example 60% plus TDIU) is not addressed by any official Illinois source found. The only place permanence matters is annual reapplication, see below.
- Definitions (f): "Service-connected disability" means an illness or injury "(i) that was caused by or worsened by active military service, (ii) that is a current disability as of the date of the application ... as demonstrated by the veteran's United States Department of Veterans Affairs certification, and (iii) for which the veteran receives disability compensation." For TY2023+, "veteran" means "an Illinois resident who has served as a member of the United States Armed Forces on active duty or State active duty, a member of the Illinois National Guard, or a member of the United States Reserve Forces and who has a service-connected disability, as certified by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs, and receives disability compensation." (Honorable discharge was required for TY2022 and prior; the TY2023+ definition drops that word and substitutes receipt of VA compensation.) "Property rented for more than 6 months is presumed to be used for commercial purposes."
- Annual reapplication (e): "each taxpayer who has been granted an exemption under this Section must reapply on an annual basis, except that a veteran who qualifies as a result of his or her service in World War II need not reapply. Application must be made during the application period in effect for the county of his or her residence." Then: "On and after May 23, 2022 (the effective date of Public Act 102-895), if a veteran has a combined service connected disability rating of 100% and is deemed to be permanently and totally disabled, as certified by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs, the taxpayer who has been granted an exemption under this Section shall no longer be required to reapply for the exemption on an annual basis." Read literally this requires both a 100% combined rating and the P&T designation; a TDIU P&T veteran with a sub-100% combined rating does not match the words. Cook County's page restates it as veterans "rated 100% disabled and classified as 'permanently and totally disabled'" being auto-renewed, and says "All applicants must reapply annually" otherwise, including the 70% to 99% tier. The lead's claim that "some counties" may drop annual renewal for P&T is not how the statute reads: the no-reapply rule is statewide and mandatory for 100% P&T, and no county option appears in 15-169 (the county-option automatic renewal in 15-168(d-15) is for the separate persons-with-disabilities exemption).
- Proration (e-1): "If the person qualifying for the exemption does not occupy the qualified residence as of January 1 of the taxable year, the exemption granted under this Section shall be prorated on a monthly basis."
- Nursing home (b-5): exemption continues "so long as the residence continues to be occupied by the qualifying person's spouse or ... if the residence remains unoccupied but is still owned by the person who qualified."
- Surviving spouse (c): "The tax exemption under this Section carries over to the benefit of the veteran's surviving spouse as long as the spouse holds the legal or beneficial title to the homestead, permanently resides thereon, and does not remarry. If the surviving spouse sells the property, an exemption not to exceed the amount granted from the most recent ad valorem tax roll may be transferred to his or her new residence as long as it is used as his or her primary residence and he or she does not remarry." For TY2023+, "surviving spouse" means (i) spouse of a veteran who had the exemption; (ii) spouse of a veteran "killed in the line of duty"; (iii) spouse of a veteran "who did not obtain an exemption under this Section before death, but who would have qualified ... and whose surviving spouse has been a resident of Illinois from the time of the veteran's death through the taxable year"; (iv) a DIC recipient as of January 1 of the tax year. Cook County requires the death certificate and proof of ownership for new or transferred spouse claims, and notes the spouse-who-never-claimed path "doesn't apply to earlier years" than 2023.
- Stacking (d): "A taxpayer who claims an exemption under Section 15-165 or 15-168 may not claim an exemption under this Section."
- Office, forms, deadline: file with the Chief County Assessment Office (Cook County Assessor in Cook). IDOR PIO-74 names Form PTAX-342 for the spouse transfer; the PTAX-342 PDF URL could not be located (IDOR form links returned 404). Deadlines are county-set; Cook County says the 2025 filing period "has ended" and late filings are processed as "Certificates of Error" that can reach back to 2021. Cook County documentation: first-time filers need a DD-214 "certified by the Cook County Clerk or Illinois Department of Veterans Affairs"; all filers need a VA disability certification letter showing name, percentage and effective date, plus photo ID and an occupancy affidavit.
- Residency / ownership / income caps: Illinois resident; primary residence; owner or lessee liable for taxes; no income cap.
- County variation: amount and tiers are uniform statewide; application period, forms handling and late-filing procedure vary by county.
- 2025-2026 changes: none enacted. HB 2809 (percentage-of-value tiers), SB 99 (surviving spouse first $250,000), HB 3136 (caregiver-owned property), HB 4405 (veterans killed in the line of duty who were not Illinois residents; would waive the spouse residency rule) were introduced in the 104th GA; HB 4405's last action is "Rule 19(a) / Re-referred to Rules Committee." None appear in the 15-169 source note.

### 1b. Veterans with Disabilities (Specially Adapted Housing) exemption (35 ILCS 200/15-165)
- Source: https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=003502000K15-165 (Source: "P.A. 104-234, eff. 8-15-25", a name-change act only). IDOR PIO-74.
- Threshold language: "Property up to an assessed value of $100,000, owned and used exclusively by a veteran with a disability, or the spouse or unmarried surviving spouse of the veteran, as a home, is exempt. As used in this Section, a 'veteran with a disability' means a person who has served in the Armed Forces of the United States and whose disability is of such a nature that the Federal Government has authorized payment for purchase or construction of Specially Adapted Housing as set forth in the United States Code, Title 38, Chapter 21, Section 2101." No percentage rating; eligibility is SAH grant approval. Covers federal-funded adaptations, replacement homes bought with proceeds of a prior SAH home, and since TY2015 charity-built or charity-adapted homes where the veteran "has been approved to receive funds" under 38 U.S.C. 2101 and a licensed inspector certifies compliance with VA Pamphlet 26-13.
- Surviving spouse: "'unmarried surviving spouse' means the surviving spouse of the veteran at any time after the death of the veteran during which such surviving spouse is not married." IDOR: "valid for as long as the veteran, the spouse, or the unmarried surviving spouse resides on the property."
- Renewal: "This exemption must be reestablished on an annual basis by certification from the Illinois Department of Veterans Affairs to the Department, which shall forward a copy of the certification to local assessing officials." IDOR lists no form and says to contact a Veteran Service Officer. Cannot be combined with 15-168 or 15-169. A parallel exemption exists under the Mobile Home Local Services Tax Act (IDVA Benefits Guide).

### 1c. Returning Veterans' Homestead Exemption (35 ILCS 200/15-167)
- Source: https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=003502000K15-167 (last amended P.A. 97-333, 2011). IDOR PIO-74 (Form PTAX-341). Cook County: https://www.cookcountyassessoril.gov/returning-veterans-exemption.
- "In all counties, the reduction is $5,000 for the taxable year in which the veteran returns from active duty in an armed conflict involving the armed forces of the United States ... Beginning in taxable year 2010, the reduction shall also be allowed for the taxable year after." "'veteran' means an Illinois resident who has served as a member of the United States Armed Forces, a member of the Illinois National Guard, or a member of the United States Reserve Forces." No disability requirement. Must own (or hold legal/equitable interest) and occupy as principal residence and be liable for taxes. If the home is bought after January 1 of the return year, the exemption applies "for the next taxable year, and only the next taxable year." "(d) The exemption under this Section is in addition to any other homestead exemption provided in this Article 15." Cook County states it cannot be combined with SHEVD; that conflicts with (d), so flagged in notes. Cook County: DD-214 or military orders plus travel voucher; filed annually; no automatic renewal.

### 1d. Homestead Exemption for Persons with Disabilities (35 ILCS 200/15-168), not veteran-specific
- Source: https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=003502000K15-168. $2,000 EAV reduction for a "person with a disability" ("unable to engage in any substantial gainful activity by reason of a medically determinable physical or mental impairment ... not less than 12 months"); proof is SSA disability eligibility, an Illinois Person with a Disability ID card showing Class 2 disability, or a state-designated exam. Forms PTAX-343 and annual PTAX-343-R (IDOR). (d-15): for TY2022 through 2027, Cook County (and other counties by ordinance) may renew without annual application. "(e) A taxpayer who claims an exemption under Section 15-165 or 15-169 may not claim an exemption under this Section." Relevant to veterans only as the fallback if SHEVD does not apply (for example a 20% rating with SSA disability), since a veteran must choose one.

## 2. Vehicles, driver license, parking
### 2a. Plates for veterans with disabilities (625 ILCS 5/3-609)
- Source: https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=062500050K3-609. Two texts are posted: "Text of Section from P.A. 104-603" (eff. 7-1-27) and "Text of Section from P.A. 104-682" (eff. 7-31-26). Current operative text (104-682): "(a) Any veteran who holds proof of a service-connected disability from the United States Department of Veterans Affairs, and who has obtained certification from a licensed physician, physician assistant, or advanced practice registered nurse that the service-connected disability qualifies the veteran for issuance of registration plates or digital registration plates or decals to a person with disabilities in accordance with Section 3-616, may, without the payment of any registration fee, make application to the Secretary of State for license plates for veterans with disabilities displaying the international symbol of access, for the registration of one motor vehicle of the first division, one motorcycle, or one motor vehicle of the second division weighing not more than 8,000 pounds. (b) Any veteran who holds proof of a service-connected disability ... and whose degree of disability has been declared to be 50% or more, but whose disability does not qualify the veteran for a plate or decal for persons with disabilities under Section 3-616, may, without the payment of any registration fee, make application to the Secretary for a special registration plate ... without the international symbol of access." P.A. 104-603 version: "up to 2 sets" (eff. July 1, 2027). "(c) Renewal of such registration must be accompanied with documentation for eligibility of registration without fee unless the applicant has a permanent qualifying disability ... The Illinois Department of Veterans Affairs may assist in providing the documentation of disability." (f) lets applicants redact medical details not needed to prove service connection or degree.
- No 100% requirement; the waiver is the registration fee (standard passenger fee is $148 plus surcharges, 625 ILCS 5/3-806). IDVA Benefits Guide (01/25): "Disabled Veteran License Plates may be issued at no cost for the first set of plates to any U.S. Armed Forces Veteran if the veteran holds proof of a service-connected disability ... and a licensed physician has certified that ... the veteran qualifies for the issuance of a disabled placard or plate." ilsos.gov could not be reached; the SOS page URL in the IDVA guide is https://www.ilsos.gov/departments/vehicles/license_plate_guide/military/disabled_vet.html (unverified). Parking privileges attached to the symbol-of-access plate were not independently verified (3-616 not read).
### 2b. Purple Heart plates (625 ILCS 5/3-623); Medal of Honor (3-609.1); POW (3-620)
- https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=062500050K3-623: "a person eligible to be issued Purple Heart plates may display the plates on one vehicle without the payment of any registration or registration renewal fee"; after P.A. 104-603 (eff. 7-1-27): "on up to 2 vehicles." Available to Illinois-resident recipients and surviving spouses. Medal of Honor and ex-POW plates are likewise fee-free (one set now, 2 sets from 7-1-27).
### 2c. Driver's license "veteran" designation (625 ILCS 5/6-110(e-5)); ID card fees (15 ILCS 335/12)
- 6-110(e-5): "the Secretary of State shall designate a space on each original or renewal driver's license where, at the request of the applicant, the word 'veteran' shall be placed. The veteran designation shall be available to a person identified as a veteran under subsection (e) of Section 6-106 of this Code who was discharged or separated under honorable conditions." IDVA page https://veterans.illinois.gov/services-benefits/veteran-driver-s-license.html: bring DD-214 (or Form 13038, DD-2 Retired, VA ID card, VA summary of benefits letter) to any SOS facility; no added cost at renewal; before renewal "$5 and an updated State ID card for $10"; "Veterans over the age of 65 can obtain an updated State ID card at no cost."
- 15 ILCS 335/12 fee schedule: "Applicant 65 years of age or over ... No Fee"; "Individual living in Veterans Home or Hospital ... No Fee." There is no general free ID or license for veterans or disabled veterans in Illinois.
### 2d. Not found
- No vehicle sales/use tax exemption for disabled veterans (the only military item on IDVA's legislation page is SB 1705 / P.A. 103-384, a use tax exemption for active duty purchases paid by the federal government). No parking placard fee issue (Illinois placards are free to all qualifying persons, not verified here).

## 3. Hunting, fishing, parks
### 3a. Fishing license exemption (515 ILCS 5/20-5(d)); hunting license (IDVA)
- https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=051500050K20-5 (Source note includes P.A. 104-234, 104-435 eff. 11-21-25; P.A. 104-790 eff. 1-1-27 does not change (d)): "(d) Veterans who, as certified by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs, are at least 10% disabled with service-related disabilities or in receipt of total disability pensions may fish with sport fishing devices during those periods of the year it is lawful to do so without being required to have a license, on the condition that their respective disabilities do not prevent them from fishing in a manner which is safe to themselves and others." No residency requirement in the text.
- IDVA permits page, https://veterans.illinois.gov/services-benefits/permits.html (footer 2026): "veterans who receive 10% or greater service-connected compensation or total disability pension" get the license free "regardless of state of residency"; "Veterans are required to present a DD214 and a Disability Affidavit"; apply for "the Disability Affidavit for Illinois Hunting and Fishing Privilege card" at a Veteran Service Office. IDVA Benefits Guide (01/25): "Free fishing and hunting licenses are issued for disabled veterans who are receiving 10 percent or greater service-connected compensation, or total disability pension benefits. The veteran may obtain this license from the IDVA by providing disability documentation that is no more than one year old."
- Wildlife Code 520 ILCS 5/3.1 (hunting license) exempts Illinois residents with a Type 1 or Type 4, Class 2 disability and active duty members on leave, but has no 10% veteran clause; 520 ILCS 5/3.2 gives returning veterans a half-price license. The statutory basis for the free hunting license was not located; whether habitat, waterfowl and deer/turkey permits are also free is not stated by IDVA. Row flagged.
### 3b. Camping and shelter fees (IDNR)
- https://dnr.illinois.gov/parks/camp/discounts.html (footer 2026; PDF version dated 2016): "Every Veteran who is a resident of Illinois and Disabled by the permanent loss of use of a leg or both legs or an arm or both arms or any combination thereof, or any person who is severely disabled as to be unable to move without the aid of crutches or a wheelchair; or is receiving 100% service connected disability compensation benefits either from the Veterans Administration or military disability retired pay (other than TDRL, Temporary Disability Retired List); or is a former prisoner of war." "On any day of the week, Disabled Veterans or Former Prisoners of War will be charged NO CAMPING FEE BUT WILL BE CHARGED THE $10.00 UTILITY FEE" (Classes AA, A, A-P, B/E, B/E-P); at B/S, B/S-P, C, D "NO CAMPING FEE." Shelter reservation fees waived. Immediate family (spouse, minor children, grandchildren under 18) covered when with the veteran. Requires the "Department of Veterans Affairs Disability Certification Card," issued at IDVA field offices on federal proof. TDIU is not addressed ("100% service connected disability compensation benefits" could be read to include payment at the 100% rate, but no official source says so). Class 2/2A disability card holders get half-price Monday to Thursday. Illinois state parks generally have no entrance fee (not verified here).

## 4. Education and tuition
### 4a. Illinois Veteran Grant (110 ILCS 947/40)
- https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=011009470K40 (Source: P.A. 104-234). Qualified applicant: "(1) At the time of entering federal active duty service the person was ... (A) An Illinois resident. (B) An Illinois resident within 6 months of entering such service. (C) Enrolled at a State-controlled university or public community college in this State. (2) ... (A) He or she served at least one year of federal active duty. (B) He or she served less than one year ... and received an honorable discharge for medical reasons directly connected with such service. ... (D) ... served less than one year of federal active duty in a foreign country during a time of hostilities ... (3) The person received an honorable discharge ... (4) The person returned to this State within 6 months after leaving federal active duty service" (or 6 months after a military spouse's service ended or was stationed in Illinois); alternative (5): Illinois resident at application who "at some point after leaving federal active duty service, was a resident of Illinois for at least 15 consecutive years." Benefit: "not required to pay any tuition or mandatory fees while attending a State-controlled university or public community college in this State for a period that is equivalent to 4 years of full-time enrollment, including summer terms." An "entitlement." Applications to ISAC; "the Department of Veterans Affairs shall assist the Commission." isac.org blocked (Cloudflare). IDVA education page: "pays tuition and mandatory fees at all Illinois state-supported colleges, universities and community colleges."
### 4b. Illinois National Guard Grant (110 ILCS 947/45)
- https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=011009470K45 (Source: P.A. 104-2, eff. 6-16-25, which also dissolves the dedicated fund into GRF on June 30, 2026): "Any person who has served at least one year in the Illinois National Guard or the Illinois Naval Militia ... shall ... be awarded a grant ... consisting of exemption from tuition and fees for not more than the equivalent of 4 years of full-time enrollment ... while he or she is a member"; "any person who has served over 10 years ... an additional 2 years." Ends when membership ends (with a one-year grace for 5+ years service and 6 months active duty).
### 4c. Deceased, Disabled, and MIA/POW Veterans' Dependents Scholarship (105 ILCS 5/30-14.2) and $250 Educational Opportunity Grant (330 ILCS 105)
- Statute https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=010500050K30-14.2 (Source: P.A. 104-234; 104-238 eff. 1-1-26; 104-852 eff. 8-21-26): "'Eligible veteran or serviceperson' means any veteran or serviceperson, including an Illinois National Guard member who is on active duty or is active on a training assignment, who has been declared by the U.S. Department of Defense or the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs to be a prisoner of war or missing in action, or has died as the result of a service-connected disability or has become a person with a permanent disability from service-connected causes with 100% disability and who (i) at the time of entering service was an Illinois resident, or (ii) was an Illinois resident within 6 months after entering such service, or (iii) is a resident of Illinois at the time of application for the Scholarship and, at some point after entering such service, was a resident of Illinois for at least 15 consecutive years." Benefit: "shall not be required to pay any tuition or mandatory fees while attending a State-controlled university or public community college in this State for a period equivalent to 4 years of enrollment, including summer terms." Spouse: "If the surviving spouse remarries or if there is a divorce ... Scholarship benefits will be terminated at the end of the term." "(c) ... The Illinois Department of Veterans Affairs shall determine, by rule, the eligibility" (added by P.A. 104-238 / HB 2572, eff. 1-1-26; the same act amended the Educational Opportunity Grant Act). P.A. 104-852 is the annual revisory act (technical).
- IDVA page https://veterans.illinois.gov/services-benefits/education/mia-pow-scholarship.html (footer 2026): veteran must be MIA, POW, rated "100% permanent and totally disabled by service connected causes," rated 100% permanent and totally disabled from service-connected causes due to unemployability, or death "rated service connected by the U.S. Department of Veterans' Affairs." This is the one Illinois benefit that explicitly counts TDIU (when P&T). Points system, 120 maximum; "Benefits expire 12 years after the enrollment date"; apply through the ISAC student portal with marriage certificate (spouse), DD-214 or casualty report, USDVA award letter, proof of acceptance; rules at 95 Ill. Adm. Code 116. IDVA Benefits Guide: "Educational Opportunity Grant of $250 is provided annually to each child between the ages of 10 and 18 years of a veteran who either died on active duty, who's death is ruled service connected, is 100% totally and permanently disabled, or 100% disabled for at least two years."
### 4d. University of Illinois Children of Veterans scholarship: IDVA describes it as "one honorary scholarship" per county for children of WWI, WWII and Korean War veterans; not a general tuition waiver. Not included as a row.

## 5. State income tax
- 35 ILCS 5/203(a)(2)(E) subtracts compensation "paid to a resident by reason of being a member of any component of the Armed Forces of the United States" and Illinois National Guard pay; (F) subtracts "distributions under the provisions of any retirement or disability plan for employees of any governmental agency or unit." https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=003500050K203
- IDOR Publication 120 (R-12/25), https://tax.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/tax/research/publications/pubs/documents/pub-120.pdf: subtract on Form IL-1040, Line 5 income from "a government retirement and government disability plan, including military plans"; attachments include "government retirement, including military plans" and "governmental disability, including military plans." Publication 102 (R-12/25): "Illinois does not tax military pay earned by service persons"; "You may subtract military pay on Schedule M." VA disability compensation and DIC are excluded from federal AGI, the Illinois starting point, so they are untaxed (inference from 203(a)(1); IDOR does not state it in so many words). SBP paid under the federal military plan is a distribution from a government retirement plan and falls within (F); IDOR does not name SBP specifically. No 2025-2026 change found.

## 6. Employment preference
- Personnel Code 20 ILCS 415/8b.7, https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=002004150K8b.7 (Source: P.A. 103-108; 103-746 eff. 1-1-25): "(b) The preference granted under this Section shall be in the form of points, or the equivalent, added to the applicable scores ... (c) A veteran is qualified for a preference of 10 points if the veteran currently holds proof of a service connected disability from the United States Department of Veterans Affairs or an allied country or if the veteran is a recipient of the Purple Heart. (d) A veteran who has served during a time of hostilities with a foreign country is qualified for a preference of 5 points if ... (1) ... at least 6 months, or (2) ... for the duration of hostilities ... or (3) ... discharged on the basis of hardship, or (4) ... released from active duty because of a service connected disability and was discharged under honorable conditions. (e) A person not eligible for a preference under subsection (c) or (d) is qualified for a preference of 3 points if ... served for at least 6 months and has been discharged under honorable conditions ... or (4) served a minimum of 4 years in the Illinois National Guard or reserve component." "(f) The augmented ratings shall be used when determining the rank order." "(h) A surviving unremarried spouse of a veteran who suffered a service connected death or the spouse of a veteran who suffered a service connected disability that prevents the veteran from qualifying for civil service employment shall be entitled to the same preference"; (i) 10 points for one parent of an unmarried veteran with service-connected death or disqualifying disability. (k) CMS must verify with "a certified copy of the applicant's most recent DD214 ... NGB-22."
- CMS Veterans Outreach Program, https://careers.illinois.gov/employment-resources/vetsopgm.html (guide labeled 4-26): "Just check the box on your application"; "Your DD-214 is securely stored"; contact veteransoutreachbyemail@illinois.gov, (800) 643-8138. Spouse/parent page: VA award letter "Must be dated within the last year" and "Must show the veteran is 100% service-connected disabled" (CMS's administrative reading of 8b.7(h) and (i)); form CMS255. Also: P.A. 104-136 (SB 2175, eff. 1-1-26) gives State employees who are veterans 4 days per year for VA-authorized care of service-connected conditions; P.A. 104-078 (SB 220) paid leave for funeral honors details. No professional license fee waiver for veterans was found; IDFPR must expedite service member/spouse applications within 30 days (P.A. 102-384).

## 7. Other (homes, cemeteries, grants, bonuses)
- Veterans Homes, https://veterans.illinois.gov/services-benefits/homes/homes.html (footer 2026; 20 ILCS 2805/2.01): five homes (Anna, LaSalle, Manteno, Quincy, Chicago). "Any honorably discharged veteran is entitled to admission if he or she" has qualifying service; must be "disabled by disease, wounds, or otherwise" and "incapable of earning a living"; must have entered service from Illinois or "been a resident of Illinois for one year immediately preceding the date of application." Widows/widowers at Anna and Quincy if beds are vacant; Gold Star family members per VA eligibility. Cost "$1720 (as of June 2025)" per month as a maintenance charge based on resident and spouse income; "The inability to contribute to the cost of care does not prevent admission."
- War Participation Bonus / POW compensation, https://veterans.illinois.gov/services-benefits/benefits/compensation-benefits.html: WWII "$10 per month for domestic service and $15 per month for foreign service"; Korea, Vietnam, Persian Gulf and Global War on Terrorism $100 one-time, each requiring 12 months of Illinois residency before entry and the named campaign medal; "Survivors are entitled to a benefit of $1,000, if the veteran's death was service-connected" (WWII hostile action; Vietnam Agent Orange); POW held in Southeast Asia on or after 1/1/1961: $50 per month of captivity. Applied through an IDVA online form or VSO.
- Burial, https://veterans.illinois.gov/services-benefits/survivor-burial.html: "the Illinois Department of Veterans' Affairs pays up to $125.00 for the setting of such marker"; "the county Veterans Assistance Commission shall provide burial for any honorably discharged indigent veteran" and spouse; state "Roll of Honor." No state veterans cemetery is listed.
- Veterans Cash, https://veterans.illinois.gov/programs/veterans-cash.html: grants of "$25,000 to $100,000" to "eligible tax-exempt nonprofit organizations and governmental organizations that provide services to Illinois veterans in need," including "emergency financial assistance"; quarterly cycles (FY27 Q3 window Aug. 14 to Sept. 30, 2026). Not a direct grant to veterans.
- Illinois Warrior Assistance Program (IDVA links to https://www.illinoiswarrior.org/, not .gov): 24-hour helpline 1-866-554-4927 for PTSD/TBI support. Noted only.
- Military Family Relief Fund and SNAP: P.A. 104-067 (SB 32, eff. 1-1-26) makes veteran households at or below 200% of poverty categorically SNAP-eligible, subject to federal approval. Shelter adoption fee waiver for veterans (P.A. 103-434). Not rows.
