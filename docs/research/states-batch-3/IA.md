# Iowa (IA) — State Benefits for Disabled Veterans

Research date: 2026-10-17. Official agency: Iowa Department of Veterans Affairs (IDVA), https://dva.iowa.gov (va.iowa.gov now redirects here).

Sourcing note: Everything below is anchored on the Iowa Code 2026 PDFs from the Iowa Legislature (legis.iowa.gov, each section footer reads "Iowa Code 2026, Section ..."), enrolled 2026 bills on legis.iowa.gov, the Iowa Department of Revenue (revenue.iowa.gov; the old tax.iowa.gov URLs redirect there), Iowa DOT (iowadot.gov and ia.iowadot.gov), Iowa DNR (iowadnr.gov), the Iowa Department of Education (educate.iowa.gov, which now runs the former College Student Aid Commission programs), the Board of Regents rule in the Iowa Administrative Code (legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac), and IDVA (dva.iowa.gov). No non-.gov sites were used as sources. legis.iowa.gov Code PDFs could not be read by WebFetch (compressed PDF) but were downloaded with curl and read with pdftotext. The IDVA "benefits" and "state-benefits" pages carry no last-updated date (footer "© 2026 State of Iowa"). The shared web-search budget ran out near the end; two items are listed under "Could not verify" for that reason.

Important 2026 change: Senate File 2472 (2026) repealed Iowa Code 425.15 and re-enacted the disabled veteran credit, word for word, as Iowa Code 425.1(2)(b), retroactive to assessment years beginning January 1, 2026. The Iowa Code 2026 PDF still shows 425.15; the Department of Revenue page already cites 425.1(2)(b). Both texts are quoted below and are identical except for the new half-acre rule.

## 1. Property tax (filed with the city or county assessor; deadline July 1)

Iowa's veteran property-tax items are set by state statute and are uniform statewide. Assessors (county or city) take the applications; the county board of supervisors allows them. Amounts do not vary by county.

### 1a. Disabled Veteran Homestead Tax Credit — full credit (Iowa Code 425.15, Code 2026; re-enacted as 425.1(2)(b) by SF 2472, 2026)
- Source (statute): https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/425.15.pdf (footer "Iowa Code 2026, Section 425.15"); enrolled SF 2472: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/publications/LGE/91/SF2472.pdf (Division XX, secs. 132, 145, 147, 148)
- Source (agency): https://revenue.iowa.gov/disabled-veteran-homestead-property-tax-credit (no date shown; cites "2026 Iowa Acts SF 2472")
- Threshold language (425.15(1), identical in new 425.1(2)(b)(1)): "the credit allowed on the homestead from the homestead credit fund shall be the entire amount of the tax levied on the homestead: a. A veteran of any of the military forces of the United States, who acquired the homestead under 38 U.S.C. §21.801, 21.802, prior to August 6, 1991, or under 38 U.S.C. §2101, 2102. b. A veteran as defined in section 35.1 with a permanent service-connected disability rating of one hundred percent, as certified by the United States department of veterans affairs, or a permanent and total disability rating based on individual unemployability that is compensated at the one hundred percent disability rate, as certified by the United States department of veterans affairs. c. A former member of the national guard of any state who otherwise meets the service requirements of section 35.1, subsection 2, paragraph “b”, subparagraph (2) or (7), with a permanent service-connected disability rating of one hundred percent ... or a permanent and total disability rating based on individual unemployability that is compensated at the one hundred percent disability rate ... d. An individual who is a surviving spouse or a child and who is receiving dependency and indemnity compensation pursuant to 38 U.S.C. §1301 et seq."
- TDIU definition (425.15(6), identical in 425.1(2)(b)(7)): "“permanent and total disability rating based on individual unemployability” means a condition under which a person has either a permanent service-connected disability rating of sixty percent or two or more permanent service-connected disability conditions in which one of the conditions has at least a forty percent rating and the combined rating for all the conditions is at least seventy percent, and the person has an administrative adjustment added to the service-connected disability rating, due to individual unemployability, such that the United States department of veterans affairs rates the veteran permanently and totally disabled for purposes of disability compensation."
- TDIU / 100% schedular / 100% P&T: The statute requires the 100% rating to be "permanent." A 100% schedular rating that the VA has not marked permanent does not match the words. TDIU is explicitly included, but only when the VA rates the veteran "permanently and totally disabled" and pays at the 100% rate (the 60% / 40%+70% combination is the federal TDIU schedular floor restated). Revenue page: "permanent and total disability ratings based on individual unemployability paid at the 100% disability rate" qualify, and applicants "are not required to have a 100% service connected disability rating" (that sentence refers to the TDIU and SAH-grant routes).
- Amount: the entire tax levied on the homestead (no dollar cap). Revenue: "The disabled veteran tax credit is a property tax credit equal to the entire amount of the tax levied on a homestead." Revenue "Tax Credits and Exemptions" page: "The current credit is equal to 100% of the actual tax levy."
- Homestead size (2026 change): SF 2472 sec. 132, new 425.1(2)(b)(6): "(a) For an owner who makes an application to secure the credit provided in this paragraph before July 1, 2026, and for the beneficiary of such an owner, “homestead” shall mean the same as defined in section 425.11 for each succeeding assessment year. (b) For an owner who makes an application to secure the credit provided in this paragraph on or after July 1, 2026, and for the beneficiary of such an owner, “homestead” shall mean the same as provided in section 425.11, except the homestead shall not include appurtenances and shall not exceed one-half acre." Revenue: for applications on or after July 1, 2026, "the maximum size is 1/2 acre and does not include appurtenances"; earlier applicants keep the 425.11 definition (one-half acre in a city plat, up to 40 acres outside, including appurtenances).
- Deadline, office, forms: Revenue: "The filing deadline for the application is July 1. Applications received after July 1 apply to the following assessment year." File Form 54-049 (Disabled Veteran Homestead Tax Credit Application) and the Homestead Tax Credit and Exemption form 54-028 with the assessor. Revenue Tax Credits page: "Claim must be filed on or before July 1. A current Benefits Paid letter and a DD214 must be included with your application." The 54-049 form page calls it a "U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs Benefit Summary Letter." IDVA links the form at https://revenue.iowa.gov/forms/application-disabled-veteran-homestead-tax-credit-54-049. No annual refiling; the credit moves with the owner: 425.15(4) "If an owner acquires a different homestead, the credit allowed under this section may be claimed on the new homestead."
- Exclusivity: 425.15(3) "An owner or a beneficiary of an owner who elects to secure the credit provided in this section is not eligible for any other real property tax exemption provided by law for veterans of military service." (So no stacking with the 426A military exemption.)
- Surviving spouse rules: 425.15(2)(a): "For an owner described in subsection 1, paragraph “a”, “b”, or “c”, the credit allowed shall be continued to the estate of an owner who is deceased or the surviving spouse and any child, as defined in section 234.1, who are the beneficiaries of a deceased owner, so long as the surviving spouse remains unmarried." Revenue Tax Credits page: "The surviving spouse as a beneficiary of the estate of a veteran who received the credit may continue to receive the credit as long as the spouse resides in the qualified homestead and does not remarry." Separately, a surviving spouse or child receiving DIC qualifies in their own right under paragraph (d); 425.15(2)(b): "no longer eligible for the credit upon termination of dependency and indemnity compensation." Revenue: "A surviving spouse who receives DIC payments is eligible for the credit even upon remarriage."
- Residency / ownership / income caps: "Veteran as defined in section 35.1" means (35.1(2)(a), (c), (d)) "A resident of this state who served..." and was "discharged under honorable conditions"; 35.1(2)(d) covers any "resident of this state who served in any component of the armed forces ... who is in receipt of service-connected disability." Revenue: "The veteran must be an owner to qualify for the credit"; the person must be "occupying the homestead" and "liable for and pays property tax on the homestead." SF 2472 sec. 140 (425.11): "The homestead includes the dwelling house which the owner, in good faith, is occupying as a home on July 1 of the year for which the credit or exemption is claimed and occupies as a home for at least six months during the calendar year." No income cap: Revenue "The income restriction is not required to receive the credit."
- County variation: none in amount or eligibility; assessor offices handle the paperwork.
- 2025-2026 changes: SF 2472 (2026), "Signed by Governor" (bill history; effective dates 05/18/2026, 07/01/2026, 01/01/2027; the homestead division "applies retroactively to assessment years beginning on or after January 1, 2026"). Sec. 145: "REPEAL. Section 425.15, Code 2026, is repealed." Sec. 147: owners already "receiving homestead credits or exemptions under chapter 425, subchapter I ... shall continue to receive such credits and exemptions ... without refiling." The same Act ends the regular $4,850 homestead credit for non-veterans from assessment year 2026 (new 425.1(2)(c)(2)) and replaces it with an exemption under 425.1A; the disabled veteran full credit is kept. HF 912, HF 910 and HF 469 (2025), which would have created tiered credits for veterans rated below 100%, were only "Introduced, referred to" committee (bill histories) and did not pass. Iowa has no partial credit for ratings under 100%.

### 1b. Military Service Property Tax Exemption — $4,000 taxable value (Iowa Code chapter 426A)
- Source: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/426A.pdf ("Iowa Code 2026, Chapter 426A"); Revenue Tax Credits and Exemptions page https://revenue.iowa.gov/taxes/tax-guidance/tax-credits-deductions-exemption/tax-credits-and-exemptions; Form 54-146a (07/14/2025) https://revenue.iowa.gov/media/2353/download
- Threshold language (426A.11(2)(b)): "The property, not to exceed four thousand dollars in taxable value for the assessment years beginning on or after January 1, 2023, of an honorably separated, retired, furloughed to a reserve, placed on inactive status, or discharged veteran, as defined in section 35.1, subsection 2, paragraph “a” or “b”." 426A.11(4): "“veteran” also means a resident of this state who is a former member of the armed forces of the United States and who served for a minimum aggregate of eighteen months and who was discharged under honorable conditions. However, “veteran” also means a resident of this state who is a former member of the armed forces of the United States and who, after serving fewer than eighteen months, was honorably discharged because of a service-related injury sustained by the veteran." Includes manufactured/mobile homes (426A.11(5)).
- No disability rating required. Amount was $1,852 before 2023; 2023 Acts ch. 71 raised it to $4,000 "retroactively to assessment years beginning on or after January 1, 2023."
- Deadline, office: 426A.13(2): "The person shall file with the appropriate assessor on forms obtained from the assessor the claim for exemption for the year for which the person is first claiming the exemption. The claim shall be filed not later than July 1 of the year for which the person is claiming the exemption." Form 54-146: "This application must be filed or postmarked to your city or county assessor on or before July 1. An application received after July 1 will be considered an application for the following year. Once filed, the claim for exemption is applicable to subsequent years and no further filing shall be required provided the claimant or the claimant's spouse owns the property on July 1 of each year." The discharge (DD-214) must be "recorded in the office of the county recorder" (426A.13(1)).
- Residency: 426A.13(1) "A person named in section 426A.11, who is a resident of and domiciled in the state of Iowa, shall receive a reduction equal to the exemption." Owner must be "the equitable or legal owner of the property designated." One exemption per veteran, in one county only (426A.12(2), 426A.13(4)).
- Surviving spouse / relatives (426A.12): if the veteran does not claim it, it goes in order to "The spouse, or surviving spouse remaining unmarried, of a veteran ... where they are living together or were living together at the time of the death of the veteran," then a widowed, unremarried dependent parent, then the minor child(ren) of a deceased veteran.
- Interaction: a veteran taking the full disabled-veteran credit (1a) cannot also take this exemption (425.15(3)).
- 2025-2026 changes: none found beyond the form revision (54-146a dated 07/14/2025). Revenue page: "The amount of exemption varies. Must own the property on July 1 of each claim year."

### 1c. No other veteran property-tax tiers
- Iowa has no partial credit for 10% to 90% ratings and no separate SAH-only exemption; the SAH/auto-grant homestead route (38 U.S.C. 2101/2102) is folded into 1a paragraph (a). The regular Homestead Tax Credit and Exemption (54-028; exemption "equal to the amount of 10% of the taxable value of the homestead, not less than $5,500 and not more than $20,000" for owners 65 and older per the Revenue page) is not veteran-specific.

## 2. Vehicles, driver license, parking

### 2a. Disabled Veteran (DV) plates and registration-fee exemption (Iowa Code 321.105(5), 321.166(6), 321L.2(5))
- Sources: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/321.105.pdf ("Iowa Code 2026"); Iowa DOT Disabled Veteran Plate page https://ia.iowadot.gov/mvd/vehicleregistration/vehicleregistration/plates/dveteran (no date shown)
- Threshold language (321.105(5)): "Seriously disabled veterans who have been provided with an automobile or other vehicle by the United States government under the provisions of 38 U.S.C. §3901 – 3904, shall be exempt from payment of the registration fee provided in this chapter for that vehicle, and shall be provided, without fee, with one set of regular registration plates or one set of any type of special registration plates associated with service in the United States armed forces for which the disabled veteran qualifies under section 321.34. The disabled veteran, to be able to claim the benefit, must be a resident of the state of Iowa. In lieu of the set of regular or special military registration plates available without fee, the disabled veteran may obtain a set of nonmilitary special registration plates or personalized plates issued under section 321.34 by paying the additional fees associated with those plates."
- This is NOT a rating-based benefit. It applies only to a vehicle bought with the VA automobile grant (38 U.S.C. 3901-3904). Iowa DOT: "only vehicles that meet the above eligibility criteria will qualify for a DV plate"; "DV plates cannot be automatically transferred to a replacement vehicle"; apply at "your county treasurer's office" with "A copy of the DD214 form" and documentation confirming federal funding for the vehicle. 321.166(6): plates "shall display the alphabetical characters “DV”."
- Parking: Iowa DOT: "vehicles displaying a DV plate may park free of charge in any city-owned or operated parking lot or metered space in Iowa"; the DV plate alone does not authorize accessible-space parking, but 321L.2(5): "The regular registration plates issued for the disabled veteran's vehicle without fee pursuant to section 321.105 entitle the disabled veteran to all of the rights and privileges associated with persons with disabilities parking permits under this chapter," and a PWD sticker may be added to DV or special plates (321L.2(1)(a)(2)). 321L.2 also lets DOT "accept a certification of disability from the United States department of veterans affairs in lieu of a statement from a physician" for any parking permit.
- Commonly repeated claim NOT supported: there is no Iowa registration-fee waiver for veterans based on a 100% (or any) disability rating. HF 291 (2025) and HF 912 (2025) proposed rating-based registration fee reductions; both were only introduced.
- 2025-2026 changes: none enacted.

### 2b. Military special plates: Veteran, Purple Heart, Ex-POW, Medal of Honor (Iowa Code 321.34(8), (8A), (12A), (18))
- Sources: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/321.34.pdf ("Iowa Code 2026"); Iowa DOT Veteran Plate page https://ia.iowadot.gov/mvd/vehicleregistration/vehicleregistration/plates/veteran (no date shown)
- 321.34(12A)(a): "An owner ... who applies for any type of special registration plates associated with service in the United States armed forces shall be issued one set of the special registration plates at no charge, but shall be subject to the annual registration fee of fifteen dollars, if the owner is eligible for, but has relinquished ... or has not been issued, ex-prisoner of war or legion of merit special registration plates." (b): "one set ... at no charge and subject to no annual registration fee if the owner is eligible for, but has relinquished ... or has not been issued, medal of honor registration plates under subsection 8 or disabled veteran registration plates under section 321.105." (d): surviving spouse may keep the plates "upon payment of the same annual registration fee, if applicable. If the surviving spouse remarries, the surviving spouse shall return the special plates."
- Medal of Honor (321.34(8)): "issued at no charge ... A person who is issued special plates under this subsection is exempt from payment of any annual registration fee for the motor vehicle bearing the special plates." Ex-POW (8A): "issued at no charge and are subject to an annual registration fee of fifteen dollars." Purple Heart (18): one set "at no charge" (regular registration fee still applies).
- Veteran plate (DOT page): eligibility "The owner of a vehicle who served in the U.S. Armed Forces" with honorable discharge; proof "A copy of the DD 214 (other than copy 1)"; Guard/Reserve need 90 days active duty other than training or 20 years; numbered plates: one set at no charge, second set $25; "$5 annual renewal fee to the Veterans License Fee Fund, plus the annual registration renewal fee"; personalized $25 first set. Apply by mailing the Special Plate Application to Vehicle & Motor Carrier Services, P.O. Box 9278, Des Moines, IA 50306-9278; plates are picked up at the county treasurer in 3 to 6 weeks.
- Iowa DOT Military/Veteran Services page: "Iowa offers military plates, some waiving city parking fees, like Medal of Honor, Disabled Vet, Purple Heart, & Ex-POW."

### 2c. Driver license fee waiver for 100% permanent veterans; veteran designation (Iowa Code 321.191(10))
- Sources: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/321.191.pdf ("Iowa Code 2026"); Iowa DOT Military Fee Waiver https://iowadot.gov/drivers-licenses-ids/get-or-renew-drivers-licenses-ids-permits/fees/military-fee-waiver; Designations https://iowadot.gov/drivers-licenses-ids/get-or-renew-drivers-licenses-ids-permits/designations (no dates shown)
- Threshold language (321.191(10)): "Fees waived — veterans. Notwithstanding the provisions of this section to the contrary, the department shall not charge the following fees for a driver's license to the following applicants: a. The fees set forth under subsections 2 and 5 to an applicant who is a veteran with a permanent service-connected disability rating of one hundred percent, as certified by the United States department of veterans affairs." Subsection 2 is the noncommercial license fee ("four dollars per year of license validity"); subsection 5 is the motorcycle fee ("two dollars per year").
- TDIU: not mentioned in 321.191(10)(a); DOT asks for the VA "Summary of Benefits" letter listing the combined rating. Whether a TDIU P&T letter is accepted is unstated. Flagged.
- Honorably discharged veterans (any rating) and active duty: DOT page says chauffeur (Class D) and CDL fees are waived for "Honorably discharged veterans, including those with a general discharge 'under honorable conditions'" with a DD-214, and for active duty with a CAC. Operator and motorcycle fees are waived only with the 100% permanent rating. DOT: "cannot retroactively apply a waiver or issue refunds."
- Veteran designation (DOT Designations page): "Honorably discharged veterans are eligible to have a “veteran” designation placed on their driver's license"; DD 214 must show honorable discharge and at least 90 days of active duty (not training), or county Veterans Affairs completes Iowa DOT form 432035; adding it to a current card or getting a duplicate "there will be a fee of $10."
- 2025-2026 changes: none found.

### 2d. Not found: vehicle sales/use tax exemption
- No Iowa exemption from the 5% fee for new registration (use tax) for disabled veterans was found in the DOT pages or Code sections reviewed (321.105A was downloaded but not fully read for budget reasons). Treat as not existing unless verified.

## 3. Hunting, fishing, parks

### 3a. Lifetime hunting and fishing combined license, lifetime fur harvester license, deer tag — any service-connected rating or ex-POW (Iowa Code 483A.24(16), (17); DNR Form 542-1412)
- Sources: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/483A.24.pdf ("Iowa Code 2026"); DNR Form 542-1412 (rev. 7/2026) https://www.iowadnr.gov/media/5563/download; DNR fishing licenses page https://www.iowadnr.gov/things-do/fishing/fishing-licenses; DNR news release 2025-08-19 https://www.iowadnr.gov/news-release/2025-08-19/new-deer-tag-option-available-disabled-veterans; SF 2471 (2026) https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/publications/LGE/91/SF2471.pdf
- Threshold language (483A.24(16)): "Upon payment of the fee established by rules adopted pursuant to section 483A.1 for a lifetime fishing license or lifetime hunting and fishing combined license, the department shall issue a lifetime fishing license or lifetime hunting and fishing combined license to a resident of Iowa who has served in the armed forces of the United States on federal active duty and who was disabled or was a prisoner of war during that veteran's military service. ... As used in this subsection, “disabled” means entitled to a service connected rating under 38 U.S.C. ch. 11." No minimum percentage (a 0% service-connected rating is still a rating).
- Fee: statute leaves it to rule; DNR Form 542-1412 (7/2026): "Lifetime Hunting and/or Fishing License (dependent on whether Hunter Education is on file) (License fee: $7.00, includes administrative fee; Optional Hard Card: $6.00)"; "Lifetime Fur Harvester License (License fee: $7.00)"; "One (1) extra Any Deer tag for any firearm season (Tag fee: $33.00) (Note: Tag available on August 15 annually)." "NOTE: This license does not include any additional fish and wildlife fees. You must still purchase the appropriate fish and wildlife fees each year." (The $7 in the IDVA leads is confirmed by the DNR form.)
- Residency (DNR form): "a current resident of Iowa and have met our qualifications of residency, including being a resident of Iowa for at least 90 days prior to applying for this license."
- Documents: "Copy of DD214 - #2 or higher (must show characterization such as Honorable or General, Under Honorable Conditions)"; "Copy of a current letter provided by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs which indicates that you are entitled to a service connected disability rating"; hunter education certificate if born after January 1, 1972 and not on file. Submit by mail, by email to Webmaster@dnr.iowa.gov, or upload at GoOutdoorsIowa.com; "You will be notified by email or mail when you have been authorized to obtain this license" and then buy it online or at any license vendor.
- 2026 change: SF 2471 (2026), "Signed by Governor," effective 07/01/2026, amends 483A.24(17): "Upon payment of a fee ... of five dollars, and any processing fee, for a lifetime fur harvester license." The 7/2026 DNR form still prints $7.00 for the fur harvester license, so the fur harvester fee is flagged.
- Deer tag (news release Aug. 19, 2025): "The fee is $33"; apply with the same lifetime-license application; veterans already on file need no new application.

### 3b. Annual armed forces license ($5) and lifetime trout license ($65) (Iowa Code 483A.24(21), (20))
- 483A.24(21): "Upon payment of a fee, not to exceed five dollars, established by rules ... for an annual armed forces fishing license or annual armed forces hunting and fishing combined license, the department shall issue [it] to a resident of Iowa who has served in the armed forces of the United States on federal active duty." DNR release 4/12/2023: "The new annual fishing license, or hunting and fishing combo license, is $5" plus a $2 processing fee; one-time application with DD214 (Form 542-1411). Not disability-based.
- 483A.24(20): lifetime trout fishing license "to a person who is at least sixty-five years of age or to a person who qualifies for the disabled veteran homestead credit under section 425.15" (SF 2472 sec. 144 changes the cite to 425.1(2)(b)). DNR fishing page: veterans with a 100% service-connected rating or the Disabled Veterans Homestead Credit "are eligible for a Lifetime Trout License at a cost of $65.00" (Form 542-0708).
- Free licenses (483A.24(18)): free annual fishing or combined license for residents "who have permanent disabilities and whose income falls below the federal poverty guidelines" or age 65+ below poverty (Form 542-8056); free special fishing license for residents with "severe mental or physical disabilities" (483A.24(11), Form 542-1456). Not veteran-specific.
- Nonresident disabled veterans (483A.24(5)): 75 deer and 75 turkey licenses per year for nonresident veterans "disabled" (30% or more) hunting with an approved disabled-hunt organization, at resident fees.

## 4. Education and tuition

### 4a. NEW 2026: Regents tuition and mandatory fee waiver, 100% permanent service-connected (HF 2491, 2026; new Iowa Code 262.9(43))
- Source: enrolled HF 2491 https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/publications/LGE/91/HF2491.pdf; bill history: "Status: Signed by Governor ... Effective date: 07/01/2026"; Passed House 97-0 (March 4, 2026), Passed Senate 46-0 (March 30, 2026), "Signed by Governor" April 30, 2026.
- Threshold language: "Direct the institutions of higher education governed by the board to waive all tuition and mandatory fees for a veteran, as defined in section 35.1, who has a permanent service-connected disability rating of one hundred percent as certified by the United States department of veterans affairs and who applies to and is admitted for enrollment at the institution. b. A veteran shall only be eligible for a waiver under this subsection if the veteran is not eligible for or has exhausted any other available federal or state sources of financial assistance for such tuition and fees, including but not limited to the Pell grant program and education benefits from the United States department of veterans affairs. The institution shall prorate the waiver each semester to cover any difference between other federal or state financial assistance obtained by the veteran and the amount of such tuition and fees."
- TDIU: not mentioned (unlike the property tax credit). "Permanent ... one hundred percent" only. Flagged.
- Scope: University of Iowa, Iowa State, University of Northern Iowa (Regents institutions) only; not community colleges or private colleges. Last-dollar after Pell and GI Bill. Residency: "veteran as defined in section 35.1" (Iowa resident for most categories). No Regents implementation page was found yet (effective July 1, 2026); flagged needs_verification.

### 4b. Resident (in-state) tuition for veterans, Guard/Reserve, active duty and dependents (Iowa Admin. Code 681—1.4(2)"b"; Iowa Code 262.9(3))
- Source: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/rule/681.1.4.pdf ("[ARC 0417D, IAB 7/8/26, effective 8/12/26]")
- Language: "(4) Honorably discharged veterans who have served an aggregate of 90 days or more on Title 10 orders, other than for training, are eligible for resident status for purposes of undergraduate, graduate, professional, or certificate tuition and mandatory fees. All other discharges will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis." "(6) Active duty, Title 10, service members and their dependents, including domestic partner, are eligible for resident status." "(2) Iowa National Guard and U.S. Armed Services reserve component service members assigned to a unit physically located in Iowa who have completed their initial entry training requirements or have served for one year are eligible for resident status." "(7) The rules for classification of veterans and qualified individuals shall be in full compliance with all federal laws, including Section 702 of the Veterans Access, Choice, and Accountability Act of 2014 (Choice Act)." Rock Island Arsenal personnel and their spouse/domestic partner/child also get resident status. Community colleges follow a parallel State Board of Education policy (not fetched).

### 4c. War Orphans / Children of the Fallen educational assistance (Iowa Code 35A.13(9); IDVA Branstad-Reynolds Scholarship)
- Sources: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/35A.13.pdf ("Iowa Code 2026"); IDVA https://dva.iowa.gov/about/scholarship-children-fallen (no date)
- Statute (35A.13(9)(c)): for "a child who is less than thirty-one years of age, and who is the child of a person who died on or after September 11, 2001, during active federal military service ... and who at the time of entering into active military service had maintained the person's residence in the state for a period of at least six months immediately before entering into active military service, the commission shall provide state educational assistance in an amount of no more than the highest resident undergraduate tuition rate established per year for an institution of higher learning under the control of the state board of regents less the amount of any state and federal education benefits, grants, or scholarships received by the child, or the amount of the child's established financial need, whichever is less." Must "begin postsecondary education prior to reaching age twenty-six"; lifetime cap "five times the highest resident undergraduate tuition rate." Pre-9/11 deaths (35A.13(9)(b)): child must have "lived in the state of Iowa for two years preceding application"; "not more than six hundred dollars per year" and "not more than three thousand dollars ... during the child's lifetime."
- IDVA Children of Fallen Iowa Service Members Scholarship (Branstad-Reynolds Fund): parent "was a resident of Iowa at the time of entering military service and at the time of death"; "Date of death must be after September 11, 2001, but does not have to be combat-related"; application "must be received at the Iowa Department of Veterans Affairs office on, or before, December 31st"; proof of dependency and DD 1300 or death certificate.
- The "$11,844 per year" figure in the leads appears only on third-party pages; the statute sets the cap as the highest Regents resident undergraduate tuition, so the dollar amount changes yearly. Not used.
- No tuition waiver for children or spouses of living disabled veterans exists in Iowa (SF 336 (2023) proposed one and did not pass).

## 5. State income tax (Iowa Code 422.5, 422.7)
- Sources: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/422.5.pdf and https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/422.7.pdf ("Iowa Code 2026"); Revenue Military Tax Information https://revenue.iowa.gov/military-tax-information (no date)
- Rate: 422.5(1)(a): tax "upon ... the entire taxable income as defined in this subchapter at a rate of three and eight-tenths percent." (Flat 3.8% beginning tax year 2025 under SF 2442 (2024); the 2026 Code shows the flat rate.)
- Military retirement: 422.7(20)(a): "Subtract, to the extent included, retirement pay received by a taxpayer from the federal government for military service performed in the armed forces, the armed forces military reserve, or national guard. b. The exclusion of retirement pay under this subsection is in addition to any exclusion provided under subsection 19." Revenue: exclusion is "retroactive to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2014," applies "regardless of a taxpayer's age," "for both residents and nonresidents of Iowa," and "The exclusion also applies to military survivor benefits received under 10 U.S.C. 1447" (SBP). 422.7(21) covers survivor benefits.
- Active duty pay: 422.7(29): "Subtract, to the extent included, all pay received by the taxpayer from the federal government for military service performed while on active duty status in the armed forces, the armed forces military reserve, or the national guard." 422.7(28) excludes military student loan repayments.
- VA disability compensation and DIC: not in federal adjusted gross income, so not in Iowa net income; Iowa law does not separately mention them (Revenue page does not address them). Iowa also fully exempts retirement income for taxpayers 55+ (422.7(19), per Revenue "general $6,000/$12,000 pension exclusion" language is outdated on the page; the statute text was not fully read). Flag minor.
- 2025-2026 changes: none affecting veterans found.

## 6. Employment preference (Iowa Code chapter 35C; 400.10)
- Sources: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/35C.pdf and https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/400.10.pdf ("Iowa Code 2026")
- 35C.1(1): "In every public department and upon all public works in the state, and of the counties, cities, and school corporations of the state, veterans who are citizens and residents of the United States are entitled to preference in appointment and employment over other applicants of no greater qualifications. ... For purposes of this section, “veteran” means as defined in section 35.1 except that the requirement that the person be a resident of this state shall not apply."
- 35C.3 (duty to appoint): "if the applicant is of good moral character and can perform the duties of the position applied for, the officer, board, or person shall appoint the applicant to the position, place, or employment. The appointing officer, board, or person shall set forth in writing and file for public inspection the specific grounds upon which it appointed or refused to appoint the person." Refusal notice within ten days on request; remedies: mandamus within 300 days (35C.4), district court appeal within 20 days (35C.5), arbitration within 60 days (35C.5A). 35C.6: removal only "for incompetency or misconduct shown after a hearing." 35C.2: not disqualified "by reason of any physical disability, provided such ... disability does not render such person incompetent to perform properly the duties."
- Points (400.10(1), municipal civil service, and 35C.1(4) for point-rated exams in political subdivisions): "veterans ... shall have five percentage points added to the veteran's grade or score attained in qualifying examinations for appointment to positions and five additional percentage points added to the grade or score if the veteran has a service-connected disability or is receiving compensation, disability benefits, or pension under laws administered by the United States department of veterans affairs. An honorably discharged veteran who has been awarded the Purple Heart incurred in action shall be considered to have a service-connected disability. However, the percentage points shall be given only upon passing the exam and shall not be the determining factor in passing." Not for promotions or police/fire chief appointments.
- State jobs: the application form must ask about military service and DAS informs the hiring agency (35C.1(2)). No separate state-point system appears in chapter 35C; the preference is the "over other applicants of no greater qualifications" rule. Spouse preference: none found in chapter 35C. Business/professional license fee waivers for veterans: not researched (HF 912 (2025) proposed "business fee waivers" and did not pass).
- 2025-2026 changes: none found.

## 7. Other (homes, cemeteries, grants, county offices)
- Iowa Veterans Trust Fund (Iowa Code 35A.13; Iowa Admin. Code 801—14): https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/35A.13.pdf; rules https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/chapter/801.14.pdf (IAC 2/5/25); IDVA https://dva.iowa.gov/benefits/state-benefits. Purposes (35A.13(8)): travel for wounded veterans' follow-up care; job retraining; "Unemployment assistance during a period of unemployment due to prolonged physical or mental illness or disability resulting from military service"; durable medical equipment; "hearing care, dental care, vision care, or prescription drugs"; counseling; honor guard; ambulance/ER; "Emergency expenses related to vehicle repair, housing repair, or temporary housing assistance"; TBI screening; homelessness prevention "not to exceed one thousand dollars per recipient." Rules 801—14.3(1): "The department may not pay benefits under this chapter if the available liquid assets of a veteran are in excess of $20,000. ... an applicant's household income, including VA pension benefits, service-connected disability income, and social security income, shall not exceed 300 percent of the federal poverty guidelines." 14.4(3): unemployment subsistence up to $500/month, $3,000 per 12 months, $6,000 lifetime; "A rating from the VA of 100 percent due to individual unemployability (IU) rated permanent and total indicates that a veteran is unemployable and will not qualify for assistance under this subrule." 14.4(8)(e): housing repair and vehicle repair/replacement lifetime maximum "$10,000 each"; transitional housing $1,000 per 12 months. IDVA: "Apply through your county VSO." "Veteran" for the rules = 35.1 definition or Iowa resident with 90 days active federal service, honorable discharge.
- Injured Veterans Grant (Iowa Code 35A.14; 801—11): https://dva.iowa.gov/benefits/state-benefits/injured-veterans-grant. "Service members or veterans who were seriously injured in the line of duty in a hazardous area after September 11, 2001" where the member "was receiving combat or hazardous duty pay"; "up to $10,000," paid in "standard $2,500 increments up to a $10,000 maximum" (801—11.1). Grant is exempt from Iowa income tax (rule 701—302.68 cited on the page).
- Iowa Veterans Home, Marshalltown: https://dva.iowa.gov/veterans-home/admissions. "Honorably discharged veterans," dependent or surviving spouses, and Gold Star parents; "Applicants shall have met the residency requirements of the state on the date we admit them"; "Spouses must be married to a veteran for at least a year before they apply"; "All applicants must be in need of either residential or nursing care"; if the veteran has a VA disability rating of 70% or higher and needs nursing care, "there is no out of pocket expense for the care and services." Applications at any County Commission of Veterans Affairs office or VA Medical Center; admissions 800-645-4591.
- Iowa Veterans Cemetery, Van Meter (34024 Veterans Memorial Dr., Adel, IA 50003): https://dva.iowa.gov/veterans-cemetery/eligibility-burial. "Any service member honorably separated from the service, their spouse, and eligible dependent children"; "Eligible veterans are interred for free"; "There is a $300 interment fee for an eligible spouse or dependent child"; "Living in Iowa is not a requirement to be eligible for burial"; "Pre-need determination is free."
- County Commissions of Veteran Affairs (Iowa Code chapter 35B): https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/35B.6.pdf. Every county employs an executive director/administrator who "shall" be recognized as "a county veterans service officer" accredited under 38 C.F.R. 14.628(c); duties include "Inform members of the armed forces, veterans, and their dependents of all federal, state, and local laws enacted for their benefit" and assist with "any and all compensation, pensions, hospitalization, insurance, education, employment" benefits. IDVA lists 99 county offices at https://dva.iowa.gov/find-veteran-services. SF 93 (2026, effective July 1, 2026) lets current or former Guard/Reserve members who are not 35.1 "veterans" serve as commissioners; the executive director must still be a 35.1 veteran.
- Military Homeownership Assistance (Iowa Finance Authority): IDVA lists "$5,000 grant for members who served after September 11, 2001 and bought a home after March 10, 2005" (90 days Aug. 2, 1990 to Apr. 6, 1991 also qualify). IFA page not fetched; narrative only.
- No state veteran bonus program is currently listed on IDVA's state benefits page.
