## 1. Property tax (filed with the municipal assessor; deadline April 1)

Maine's veteran exemptions are set by state statute and are uniform statewide, but the dollar amount is multiplied by each town's certified assessment ratio (36 M.R.S. 653(1)(K); Bulletin 7 sec. 10), so the assessed-value reduction varies by town. Towns may also adopt a local veteran benefit program under 36 M.R.S. 6234 to make up the ratio difference. The property must be in the town where the veteran lives ("having a taxable situs in the place of residence").

### 1a. Veteran exemption, $6,000 of just value (36 M.R.S. 653(1)(C))
- Source: https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/36/title36sec653.html (page data extracted 10/05/2026; last amended PL 2025, c. 650, Pt. M, sec. 2). Agency guidance: MRS Property Tax Bulletin No. 7, https://www.maine.gov/revenue/sites/maine.gov.revenue/files/inline-files/bull7.pdf ("October 14, 2025; replaces October 6, 2022 revision").
- Threshold language (statute): "The estates up to the just value of $6,000, having a taxable situs in the place of residence, of veterans who served in the Armed Forces of the United States: (1) During any federally recognized war period, including the Korean Conflict, the Vietnam War, the Persian Gulf War, the periods from August 24, 1982 to July 31, 1984 and December 20, 1989 to January 31, 1990, Operation Enduring Freedom, Operation Iraqi Freedom and Operation New Dawn, or during the period from February 1, 1955 to February 27, 1961, or who were awarded an expeditionary medal for service in the Armed Forces of the United States, when they have reached the age of 62 years or when they are receiving any form of pension or compensation from the United States Government for total disability, service-connected or nonservice-connected, as a veteran. ... or (2) Who are disabled by injury or disease incurred or aggravated during active military service in the line of duty and are receiving any form of pension or compensation from the United States Government for total, service-connected disability."
- TDIU / 100% schedular / 100% P&T: The statute says "total disability" and "total, service-connected disability." It does not say "100 percent" and does not require permanence. Bulletin 7 sec. 2(O)(2)(c) adds: "A veteran receiving compensation as unemployable at the 100% level is considered totally disabled," and sec. 2(O)(3)(c) lists "Receives a pension or compensation from the federal government for total disability because of injury or disease incurred or aggravated during active military service in the line of duty or because of unemployability." So TDIU qualifies, 100% schedular qualifies, and 100% P&T qualifies. A veteran with war-period service also qualifies at age 62 with no rating, or with a nonservice-connected VA pension for total disability.
- Non-wartime veterans: qualify only under paragraph (C)(2), i.e. total service-connected disability. Bulletin 7 confirms the three alternative service criteria (war period, expeditionary medal, or total service-connected disability).
- Definition of veteran (653(1)(E)): "an individual who was on active duty in the Armed Forces of the United States and who, if discharged, retired or separated from the Armed Forces, was discharged, retired or separated under other than dishonorable conditions."
- Amount: $6,000 of just value, times the certified ratio ("if a veteran is eligible for a $6,000 exemption and the municipality's certified ratio is 85%, the veteran exemption under 36 M.R.S. § 653 will be $5,100"). WWI-era veterans: $7,000 (653(1)(C-1)).
- Deadline, office, forms (653(1)(G)): "shall make written application and file written proof of entitlement on or before the first day of April, in the year in which the exemption is first requested, with the assessors of the place in which the person resides. ... an application and proof of entitlement filed pursuant to this paragraph is confidential." Bulletin 7 sec. 7: "Once the veteran or other qualifying individual is approved for the an exemption, an annual application for exemption is not necessary unless specifically requested by the assessor." Unorganized territory: file with the State Tax Assessor. Proof (sec. 8): DD-214, VA letter or certificate, birth certificate if based on age.
- Residency / ownership: 653(1)(F): "An exemption may not be granted to any person under this subsection unless the person is a resident of this State." Property may be owned by the veteran, jointly with a spouse, or held in a revocable living trust; a life-estate holder counts as owner (Bulletin 7 sec. 11). Only one paragraph of the section per person (653(1)(J)). Property conveyed to obtain the exemption does not qualify except between spouses (653(1)(I)).
- Income caps: none.
- County variation: none by county; Maine administers property tax by municipality, and the ratio adjustment varies by town.
- 2025-2026 changes: PL 2025, c. 113, Pt. D, secs. 16-19 (2025) rewrote paragraphs D, D-1, D-2, D-3 (survivor and SAH language, trusts). PL 2025, c. 650, Pt. M, sec. 2 (2026 supplemental budget, H.P. 1491, L.D. 2212) added 653(3): "The exemptions provided in this section are discontinued beginning with property tax years beginning on or after April 1, 2027." See 1d. Bulletin 7 (October 2025) predates c. 650 and does not mention the sunset.

### 1b. Specially adapted housing exemption, $50,000 (36 M.R.S. 653(1)(D-1))
- Source: same statute page.
- Threshold language: "The estates up to the just value of $50,000, having a taxable situs in the place of residence, for specially adapted housing units, of veterans who served in the Armed Forces of the United States during any federally recognized war period ... or who were awarded an expeditionary medal ... and who are veterans as described in 38 United States Code, Section 2101, and who received a grant from the United States Government for any such housing, or of the unremarried surviving spouses of those veterans."
- TDIU / P&T: the statute keys on the federal SAH grant under 38 U.S.C. 2101 (which itself requires permanent and total service-connected disability). Bulletin 7 sec. 3(B): "If a qualifying veteran is totally and permanently disabled and received a grant from the United States Government for a specially adapted housing unit under 38 U.S.C. § 2101, the veteran is eligible for an exemption from property tax for up to $50,000 of just value for that specially adapted housing unit." Proof: "letter from the VA or other federal agency verifying the veteran applicant is totally and permanently disabled and received a grant from the federal government for specially adapted housing."
- Note the statute text requires war-period or expeditionary-medal service for the SAH tier (unlike paragraph (C)(2)). The older MRS form reportedly used the word "paraplegic"; the current statute does not.
- Surviving spouse: unremarried surviving spouse keeps the $50,000 and "does not need to meet the requirement under Section 1(N)(3)" (Bulletin 7 sec. 3(B) under spouse).
- Same April 1 deadline, assessor, residency and ratio rules as 1a. Discontinued for tax years from April 1, 2027; replaced by the $50,000 SAH add-on in 683-A(2)(E).

### 1c. Surviving spouse, minor child, widowed parent exemption, $6,000 (36 M.R.S. 653(1)(D))
- Source: same statute page (paragraph D as amended by PL 2025, c. 113, Pt. D, sec. 16).
- Threshold language: "The estates up to the just value of $6,000, having a taxable situs in the place of residence, of the unremarried surviving spouse or minor child of any veteran who would be entitled to the exemption if living, or who is in receipt of a pension or compensation from the Federal Government as the surviving spouse or minor child of a veteran. The estates up to the just value of $6,000 ... of the parent of a deceased veteran who is 62 years of age or older and is an unremarried surviving spouse who is in receipt of a pension or compensation from the Federal Government based upon the service-connected death of that parent's child."
- Remarriage ends eligibility ("unremarried"). Bulletin 7 "qualifying spouse": "Was married to the veteran at the time of the veteran's death and has not remarried." Must be a Maine permanent resident; the exemption applies to the survivor's own residence (it does not require staying in the veteran's former home, but the property must be the survivor's place of residence). WWI-era survivors: $7,000 (D-2, D-3).
- Late filing: Bulletin 7 sec. 7: "An assessor may abate taxes for a qualifying spouse or child who misses the April 1 application deadline, if that qualifying individual applies within one year of the commitment date and the related veteran died during the 12-month period preceding April 1 for which the tax was committed."
- Intestate death: spouse and minor children "are jointly entitled to only a single exemption."

### 1d. 2027 consolidated homestead exemption with tiered veteran add-on (36 M.R.S. 683-A; PL 2025, c. 650, Pt. M)
- Source: https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/36/title36sec683-A.html (enacted PL 2025, c. 650, Pt. M, sec. 8); session law PDF https://lldc.mainelegislature.org/Open/Laws/2025/2025_PL_c650.pdf (Chapter 650, H.P. 1491 - L.D. 2212, "An Act Making Supplemental Appropriations and Allocations"; "See title page for effective date," i.e. the Second Regular Session general effective date of July 29, 2026).
- Threshold language: "1. ... For property tax years beginning on or after April 1, 2027, up to $25,000 of the just value of a homestead owned by an applicant for the preceding 12 months is exempt from taxation. 2. Homestead property tax exemption; veterans. A homestead eligible for exemption under subsection 1 is eligible for an additional exemption if the permanent resident is a veteran, as follows. A. If the veteran does not have a service-connected disability rating or has a service-connected disability rating of less than 60% as determined by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs, the homestead is eligible for an additional exemption of $5,000 of just value if the veteran is under 62 years of age. B. ... $6,000 of just value if the veteran is 62 years of age or older. C. ... $7,000 of just value if the veteran served during or before World War I. D. If the veteran has a service-connected disability rating of 60% or greater ...: (1) For a veteran with a service-connected disability rating of 60%, ... up to $10,000 of just value; (2) ... 70%, ... up to $20,000; (3) ... 80%, ... up to $30,000; (4) ... 90%, ... up to $40,000; and (5) For a veteran with a service-connected disability rating of 100%, the homestead is eligible for an additional exemption of up to $50,000 of just value. E. If the veteran has received a grant from the United States Government for a specially adapted housing unit under 38 United States Code, Section 2101, the homestead is eligible for an additional exemption of $50,000 of just value."
- TDIU / P&T: the new law uses "service-connected disability rating of 100%" with no permanence requirement. It does not mention unemployability; a TDIU veteran's schedular rating is below 100%, so how TDIU will be treated under 683-A is unknown until MRS issues guidance (sec. M-21 requires the State Tax Assessor to "develop and make publicly available guidance regarding the consolidation"). Flagged needs_verification.
- Survivors: "An applicant seeking an additional exemption under this subsection as the unremarried surviving spouse, minor child or parent of a deceased veteran is eligible for the additional exemption that the deceased veteran would be eligible for pursuant to this subsection if living." New 36 M.R.S. 681(6) defines veteran to include those survivors (sec. M-5). The age-62 and war-period tests disappear for rated veterans; war-period service no longer matters except the WWI tier.
- Application (new 684(5), sec. M-14): "on or before the first day of April in the year in which such an exemption is first requested, make written application and, for an individual seeking an additional exemption for veterans under section 683-A, subsection 2, file written proof of entitlement with the assessors of the place in which the individual resides." Confidential (684(6)). Ownership for the preceding 12 months and permanent residence required (683-A(1); 682).
- Transition (sec. M-20): property that received the homestead, veteran (653) or blind (654-A) exemption "in the property tax year beginning April 1, 2026 is presumed to be eligible for that equivalent property tax exemption or additional property tax exemption for the property tax year beginning April 1, 2027 unless the State Tax Assessor determines that property is no longer eligible."
- Related: sec. M-18 amended 36 M.R.S. 5219-KK(2-E) (see section 5). No MRS guidance or bulletin on 683-A was found yet.

### 1e. Items checked and not found
- No full property tax exemption for 100% disabled veterans exists in Maine. Bills to create one were introduced in earlier Legislatures (e.g., LD 1207 (126th), LD 82 (128th), LD 1271 (129th), LD 1569 (131st)) but the only enacted change is the 2027 tiered add-on above.
- No 2025 or 2026 chaptered law raising the $6,000 amount or expanding 653 to 100% non-wartime veterans was found; 653(1)(C)(2) already covers non-wartime veterans with total service-connected disability.

## 2. Vehicles, driver license, parking

### 2a. Disabled Veteran plates, no registration fee, free placard (29-A M.R.S. 523(2))
- Source: https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/29-A/title29-Asec523.html (523(2) last amended PL 2025, c. 152, sec. 1, "Effective May 30, 2025"); session law https://lldc.mainelegislature.org/Open/Laws/2025/2025_PL_c152.pdf (H.P. 1039 - L.D. 1581, "An Act Regarding the Motor Vehicle Excise Tax and Registration Fees Exemption for Certain Veterans," emergency, effective May 30, 2025). BMV chart: https://www.maine.gov/sos/bmv/vehicles/veteran-plate-or-decal/vehicles/available-veteran-plates.
- Threshold language: "The Secretary of State, on application and upon evidence of payment of the excise tax required by Title 36, section 1482 or upon evidence of exemption from excise tax under Title 36, section 1483, subsection 12, shall issue, with no annual registration fee, a registration certificate and upon request special designating plates to be used in lieu of regular registration plates for a vehicle with a registered gross weight of not more than 26,000 pounds to any 100% disabled veteran when that application is accompanied by certification from the United States Department of Veterans Affairs or any branch of the United States Armed Forces as to the veteran's permanent disability and receipt of 100% service-connected benefits. Upon request a disability placard may be issued at no fee. - Disabled Veteran parking plate (29-A M.R.S. 521(14)): "A person qualifying for special designation plates pursuant to section 523, subsection 1 or 2 may request disabled veterans parking registration plates." Disability plates/placards: no fee; vehicles displaying them "may park in a parking area with a meter without a charge, and may park a length of time that does not exceed twice the limit otherwise allowed" (BMV Disability Plates or Placards page). Motorcycle DV plate: 523(2-A).
- Registration fee chart (https://www.maine.gov/sos/bmv/vehicles/registration-fees): DV Disabled Veteran "No Fee"; VX Disabled Veteran Parking Plate "No Fee"; XV Disabled Veteran Motorcycle "No Fee"; PH Purple Heart "No Fee"; VT Special Veteran "$35/$37."

### 2b. Excise tax exemption (36 M.R.S. 1483(12) and (12-A))
- Source: https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/36/title36sec1483.html (sub-12 amended PL 2025, c. 578, sec. 1; sub-12-A corrected RR 2025, c. 2); session laws c. 152 (above) and https://lldc.mainelegislature.org/Open/Laws/2025/2025_PL_c578.pdf (H.P. 205 - L.D. 305, "An Act Regarding the Motor Vehicle Excise Tax Exemption for Leased Automobiles for Certain Veterans," "Effective July 1, 2026"). BVS page: https://www.maine.gov/veterans/benefits/tax-finance-benefits.
- Threshold language (sub-12): "Automobiles owned by veterans who are granted free registration of those vehicles by the Secretary of State under Title 29-A, section 523, subsection 1 or 2 or who are disabled by injury or disease incurred or aggravated during active military service in the line of duty and are receiving any form of pension or compensation from the United States Government for total, service-connected disability. A maximum of 3 automobiles owned by a veteran are exempt from the excise tax. In order for an automobile to qualify for an exemption from the excise tax under this subsection, the name of the qualifying veteran must appear on the automobile's certificate of title. If there are multiple owners of an automobile, at least one of the owners whose name appears on the certificate of title must meet the requirements of this subsection." Sub-12-A (leases, effective July 1, 2026) mirrors this for "Automobiles leased by veterans" with the name "on the automobile's lease agreement."
- TDIU: the excise statute uses the same "total, service-connected disability" phrase as the property tax statute, where MRS counts TDIU as total disability, but no BMV or BVS source says so for excise tax. BVS: "This would require the veteran to provide proof of 'total, service-connected disability' in the form of a current Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) Benefit Summary letter." BVS also states: "veterans who are otherwise eligible for the exemption are authorized regardless of whether they have a disabled veteran's plate. It is important to note that a veteran must pay the regular associated registration fee if they choose a different registration plate."
- Resource Guide: "As of May 30, 2025, certain veterans qualify for an excise tax exemption for up to three vehicles in which their name appears on the vehicles' titles. ... Applicants must provide their DD Form 214 to prove veteran status, as well as their VA ratings letter."
- 2025-2026 changes: c. 152 (May 30, 2025) added the 3-vehicle cap and the name-on-title rule "to prevent the misuse of the motor vehicle excise tax exemption" (c. 578 preamble); c. 578 (effective July 1, 2026) added leased vehicles because "a significant group of disabled veterans lease, rather than own, their vehicles and was excluded."
- Active duty (1483(16)): vehicles of members permanently stationed in or outside Maine or deployed over 180 days are also exempt (not veteran-specific).

## 4. Education and tuition

### 4a. Veterans Dependents Educational Benefits (37-B M.R.S. 505(2))
- Sources: https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/37-B/title37-Bsec505.html (sub-2 last amended PL 2019, c. 377 and c. 601); https://www.maine.gov/veterans/benefits/education/veterans-dependents-educational-benefits.
- Threshold language (BVS, quoting the statute): "Maine provides a 100% waiver of tuition and all mandatory fees for spouses and dependents of veterans as defined in Title 37B, Chapter 7, Section 505(2). Specifically, veterans must meet the following criteria: Has a 100% total permanent disability rating resulting from service-connected disability. Or who Was killed in action; Died from a service-connected disability as a result of service; At the time of death was totally and permanently disabled due to a service-connected disability, but whose death was not related to the service-connected disability; or Is a member of the Armed Forces on active duty who has been listed for more than 90 days as missing in action, captured or forcibly detained." Statute (2.A(3)(a)): "Has a total permanent disability resulting from a service-connected disability as a result of service."
- TDIU / 100% schedular / 100% P&T: "total permanent disability" is required; 100% P&T qualifies; a non-permanent 100% rating does not match; TDIU is not mentioned and a TDIU rating without a permanent designation does not match "total permanent."
- Residency: "'Veteran' means any person who served in the military or naval forces of the United States and entered the service from this State or has been a resident of this State for five years immediately preceding application for aid and, if living, continues to reside in this State throughout the duration of benefits." The 5 years must be continuous (statute), except for those receiving benefits on or before January 1, 2006. BVS verifies residency with a driver's license, state ID, vehicle registration or hunting/fishing license.
- Dependents: child (natural or adopted before 18; stepchild if the parent has been married to the veteran 5 years) at least 16, high school graduate, enrolled and receiving benefits before age 22 (26 if the child's own military service prevented earlier enrollment); spouse means "the person currently legally married to a living veteran" or the unremarried widow or widower not previously divorced from the veteran; one spouse per veteran.
- Schools and amount: "Schools covered under this program include the University of Maine System, Maine Community Colleges, and Maine Maritime Academy." Children: free tuition including mandatory and lab fees for certificate, associate and bachelor programs, capped at the cost of 120 undergraduate credit hours at the in-state rate at UMaine Orono, 10 academic years, 2.0 GPA; spouses: 120 credit hours including master's programs, 10 years. Room and board excluded. "In order to be eligible for benefits under this subsection, a student must apply for a Federal Pell Grant." Apply with the Maine Veterans Dependents Educational Benefits Application (BVS).
- 2025-2026 changes: none found.

### 4b. In-state tuition for veterans (20-A M.R.S. 10010)
- Source: https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/20-A/title20-Asec10010.html (last amended PL 2013, c. 488).
- "If a current member of the United States Armed Forces or a veteran of the United States Armed Forces who has been honorably discharged is enrolled in a program of education at any campus of the University of Maine System, the Maine Community College System or the Maine Maritime Academy, that member or veteran is eligible for in-state tuition rates, regardless of the member's or veteran's state of residence."
- Maine National Guard tuition assistance exists (Resource Guide: "Current law provides a 100% tuition waiver for Maine's National Guard") but is not a disabled-veteran benefit and was not researched further.

## 5. State income tax
- Military retirement: 36 M.R.S. 5122(2)(M-2)(1)(b), https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/36/title36sec5122.html: subtraction of "An amount equal to the aggregate of retirement benefits under military retirement plans included in the individual's federal adjusted gross income." (2)(c): "'Military retirement plan' means retirement plan benefits received as a result of service in the active or reserve components of the United States Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard or Space Force. For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026, 'military retirement plan' means retirement plan benefits received as a result of service in the active or reserve components of the uniformed services of the United States as defined in 37 United States Code, Section 101(3)." History: PL 2025, c. 271, Pt. C and PL 2025, c. 452 (L.D. 366).
- MRS FAQ (https://www.maine.gov/revenue/faq/individual-income-tax, "Revised: April 13, 2026"): "For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2016, the benefits received under a military retirement plan, including survivor benefits, are fully exempt from Maine income tax. In addition, for tax year 2025, you and your spouse may each deduct up to $48,216* of pension income ... For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025, the amount of your non-military pension income deduction may be subject to phaseout."
- MRS 2025 Legislative Changes (https://www.maine.gov/revenue/sites/maine.gov.revenue/files/inline-files/legischange25_0.pdf): "military retirement pay received as a result of service in the United States Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard, Space Force, National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, and Public Health Service will be fully exempt from Maine income tax. Effective September 24, 2025. 36 M.R.S. § 5122(2)(M-2); L.D. 366; P.L. 2025, c. 452."
- SBP: 5122(2)(HH): "annuity payments made to the survivor of a deceased member of the military who died as the result of service ... under a survivor benefit plan or reserve component survivor benefit plan pursuant to 10 United States Code, Chapter 73 reduced by any amount claimed as a modification under paragraph M, M-1 or M-2."
- VA disability compensation: Maine's statute has no VA-pay subtraction because VA compensation and DIC are excluded from federal AGI, the starting point for Form 1040ME. No Maine page states this in so many words; the statement rests on the structure of 36 M.R.S. 5122 (modifications to federal AGI).
- Active duty pay earned outside Maine under orders: 5122(2)(LL) subtraction.
- Property Tax Fairness Credit, additional credit for 100% P&T veterans: 36 M.R.S. 5219-KK(2-E), https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/36/title36sec5219-KK.html (amended PL 2025, c. 650, Pt. M, sec. 18): "For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2023, in addition to the credit under subsection 2-D, a resident individual who is a veteran who is 100% permanently and totally disabled is allowed an additional credit against the taxes imposed under this Part in an amount equal to the amount calculated under subsection 2-D. ... 'veteran who is 100% permanently and totally disabled' means having a rating by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs at 100% for one or more service-connected disabilities that are rated permanent." Sub-2-D (amended PL 2025, c. 650, Pt. CCCC): credit equals benefit base above 4% of income; "For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026, the credit may not exceed $1,500 for resident individuals under 65 years of age ... for resident individuals 65 years of age and older, the credit may not exceed $2,000." Married filing separately do not qualify. TDIU does not match "rated at 100% ... rated permanent."

## 6. Employment preference
- 5 M.R.S. 7054 (the lead) is "(REPEALED)" by PL 2015, c. 438, sec. 1. The current law is 5 M.R.S. 7054-B, https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/5/title5sec7054-B.html (amended PL 2025, c. 634, secs. 1-3; session law https://lldc.mainelegislature.org/Open/Laws/2025/2025_PL_c634.pdf, Second Regular Session 2026, "See title page for effective date," i.e. July 29, 2026).
- Threshold language: "'Veteran' means a person who has served on active duty in the United States Armed Forces including the Reserves of the United States Armed Forces and the National Guard and received a discharge other than dishonorable." "2. Interview. In filling any position in the classified service, the employing agency shall offer an interview to any veteran, gold star spouse or spouse of an eligible service member who meets the minimum qualifications established for the position. 3. Retention preference. In any reduction in personnel in the state service, employees who are veterans, gold star spouses or spouses of eligible service members must be retained in preference to all other competing employees in the same classification with equal seniority, status and performance reviews."
- No points system, no disabled-veteran tier, no spouse-of-disabled-veteran tier. BVS Employment page (https://www.maine.gov/veterans/benefits/employment): "Public Law 2015, Chapter 438 provides a preference for qualified veterans who apply for vacancies in classified State service by ensuring that they are offered an interview. In addition, in accordance with Executive Order 2016-002, if a veteran applies for a State job and is not ultimately hired for that job, it is the policy of the Bureau of Human Resources to provide guidance to that veteran on other State of Maine openings." Maine Hire-A-Vet is a Department of Labor campaign, not a statutory preference; the BVS page describes CareerCenter DVOP/LVER staff and the OPOR licensing initiative crediting military training toward occupational licenses (no fee waiver cited).

## 7. Other (homes, cemeteries, grants, bonuses)
- Veterans Temporary Assistance Fund / Veterans' Emergency Financial Assistance Program (37-B M.R.S. 505(1-C)): "The bureau may provide a grant of temporary assistance not to exceed $2,000 to a veteran currently a resident of this State who has filed a valid claim for a veteran's pension, pending notification of the award"; and "not to exceed $2,000 to a veteran currently a resident of this State who demonstrates to the bureau's satisfaction a financial need and suffers an emergency, including but not limited to: (1) Damage to that veteran's home due to fire, flood or hurricane that is not fully compensable by insurance; (2) Illness or the illness of an immediate family member; or (3) Hardship that would result in the veteran becoming homeless." Veteran: honorable or general-under-honorable discharge, Reserve retirees, and case-by-case others and Maine National Guard. BVS page (https://www.maine.gov/veterans/benefits/tax-finance-benefits): grants "up to a $2,000" including "vehicle repair to maintain employment ... up to $500 for a dental emergency"; "To qualify for Veteran Emergency Financial Assistance Program (VEFAP) funds, a discharge status of 'Honorable' or 'General Under Honorable Conditions' is required." Vendor: Fedcap Veterans Forward, 844-653-0316.
- Maine Veterans' Homes (37-B M.R.S. ch. 11; 607, https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/37-B/title37-Bsec607.html): "The chief executive officer shall grant admission only to veterans who were residents of Maine at the time of their entry into the United States Armed Forces or who are residents of Maine at the time of application, and to the spouses, widows or widowers of eligible veterans, as long as suitable facilities are available. Parents of armed services members who are killed in action or die as a consequence of wounds received in battle are also eligible, as so-called 'gold star' parents." Six homes: Augusta, Bangor, Caribou, Machias, Scarborough, South Paris (mainevets.org, non-.gov).
- Maine Veterans' Memorial Cemetery System (37-B M.R.S. 504; BVS https://www.maine.gov/veterans/memorial-cemetery-system/burial): four cemeteries (Central Maine Civic Center Drive and Mt. Vernon Road in Augusta, Northern Maine in Caribou, Southern Maine in Springvale). "All burials must be without charge." Eligible veteran: honorable or general discharge; "If having served as an enlisted person after September 7, 1980 or as an officer after October 16, 1981, served for a minimum of 24 continuous months or the full period for which the person was called to active duty." Spouses, surviving spouses (including those remarried to a non-veteran, death on or after January 1, 2000), minor and disabled adult children eligible. "Headstones and vaults are not provided at state expense." Guard/Reserve members assigned outside Maine without 5 years Maine residency pay an interment fee.
- Housing: SaluteME (MaineHousing) 0.50% rate discount for veterans, first-time-buyer rule waived; not disability-based; BVS Housing page. No state housing grant or state bonus found.
- Veteran ID: Maine does not issue a state veteran ID card; the driver's license designation (2d) serves that role.
