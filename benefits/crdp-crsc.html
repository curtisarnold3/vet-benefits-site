CRDP and CRSC: for military retirees

Normally your military retired pay is reduced dollar-for-dollar by your VA compensation (the "VA waiver"). Two programs give some or all of that back. You can get one, not both.

At a glance

CRDPConcurrent Retirement and Disability Pay. Restores retired pay for 20-year retirees (including Guard and Reserve with 20 qualifying years, and TERA) with a VA rating of 50% or more. Automatic. Taxable.
CRSCCombat-Related Special Compensation. For retirees, including Chapter 61 medical retirees with under 20 years, whose disability is combat-related (Purple Heart, armed conflict, hazardous duty, instrumentality of war, or simulated war). DFAS lists a 10% minimum VA rating. You apply to your service branch with DD Form 2860. Tax-free.
ChoosingDFAS runs an Open Season every January (Jan 1 to 31 in 2026). You get a letter if you are eligible for both
Official pagesDFAS CRDP · DFAS CRSC · Apply for CRSC

Which one is better?

If every one of your rated conditions is combat-related, CRSC is usually better because it is tax-free. If only some are, CRDP often pays more because it restores the full waiver amount at 50%+. DFAS's letter shows both numbers. A retiree services office or a VSO can help you compare.

Traps

  • Chapter 61 (medical) retirees with under 20 years do not get CRDP. CRSC is the only route, and only for combat-related conditions.
  • CRDP for Chapter 61 retirees with 20+ years is offset by the difference between disability retired pay and longevity retired pay.
  • CRSC is not a VA program. VA rates you; your branch decides what is combat-related.
  • The Major Richard Star Act, which would extend concurrent receipt to combat-injured medical retirees with under 20 years, had not become law as of Oct 2026 based on the sources we could reach. Needs verification Check congress.gov.
