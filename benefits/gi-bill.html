Chapter 33: the Post-9/11 GI Bill (and how it differs from Chapter 35)

The GI Bill is based on how long you served, not your rating. It is yours. Chapter 35 is a separate benefit for your family that turns on P&T. Many families can use both.

At a glance

Who90+ days of active duty after Sept 10, 2001; or Purple Heart after 9/11 with any service; or 30 continuous days and a discharge for a service-connected disability
Rating neededNone
How much36 months. Public in-state tuition paid in full at the 100% tier; private school cap $30,908.34 per year (Aug 1, 2026 to Jul 31, 2027); housing allowance; up to $1,000 a year for books
Expires?Never, if your service ended on or after Jan 1, 2013. Otherwise 15 years after separation
FormVA Form 22-1990 (22-1990e for a family member using transferred benefits)
Official pageva.gov Post-9/11 GI Bill

Traps

  • Transfer has to happen while you are still in. Veterans cannot transfer the GI Bill after separation. If you are still serving, set it up in milConnect now.
  • Fry Scholarship covers children and spouses of service members who died in the line of duty after 9/11. Same tuition structure as the Post-9/11 GI Bill. Form 22-5490. A dependent eligible for both Fry and Chapter 35 must choose.
  • Combined cap: using more than one VA education program is capped at 48 months total.
